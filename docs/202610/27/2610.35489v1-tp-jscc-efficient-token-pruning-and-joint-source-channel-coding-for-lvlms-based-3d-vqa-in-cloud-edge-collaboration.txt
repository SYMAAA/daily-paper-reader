# TP-JSCC: Efficient Token Pruning and Joint Source-Channel Coding for LVLMs-based 3D-VQA in Cloud-Edge Collaboration 

Zhengxin Li, Chongzhen Tian, Hui Yuan, Senior Member, IEEE , Hao Liu, and Sam Kwong, Fellow, IEEE 

Abstract —Deploying large vision-language models (LVLMs) on resource-constrained embodied agents for 3D visual question answering necessitates cloud-edge collaboration. However, of-floading massive visual data to the cloud over bandwidth-limited and noisy channels introduces severe latency and transmission distortion. To address this, we propose TP-JSCC, an efficient token pruning and joint source-channel coding method for LVLMs-based 3D-VQA in Cloud-Edge Collaboration. First, we develop a Local and Global Semantics-based Hybrid Token Pruning (LGS-HTP) module to significantly reduce the visual token count. By decoupling foreground-background tokens and jointly selecting local and global semantics, LGS-HTP effectively reduces redundancy while preserving reasoning-critical tokens. Second, we introduce a lightweight Token Joint Source-Channel Coding (T-JSCC) module to further compress the pruned tokens and ensure their robust transmission over error-prone channels. By employing lightweight cascaded linear layers and Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR)-adaptive mechanism, this module adapts to fluctuating channel conditions while maintaining minimal compu-tational overhead. Extensive experiments on ScanQA and SQA3D datasets indicate the exceptional efficiency and robustness of our approach. Specifically, on ScanQA validation set, the LGS-HTP module could achieve 90% pruning ratio while incurring less than a 1% CIDEr loss. Building on this, the T-JSCC further compresses the tokens by 18 ×, resulting in an overall 180 × data volume reduction for robust transmission, while saving 90% in computational complexity for LVLM deployment. Our code is available at https://github.com/yuanhui0325/TP-JSCC. 

Index Terms —Token Compression, Joint Source-Channel Cod-ing, Large Vision-Language Models, 3D Visual Question Answer-ing, Embodied Agents.

Consequently, integrating LVLMs into EAs is an inevitable trend toward general-purpose embodied intelligence. However, the remarkable capabilities of LVLMs come at the cost of substantial computational overhead. Most EAs, such as intelligent drones and domestic service robots, are subject to strict physical constraints, including size, thermal limits, and battery power, rendering on-device LVLM inference compu-tationally prohibitive. Therefore, it is necessary to develop a Cloud-Edge Collaborative Paradigm (CE-CP) for EAs [3]. In this paradigm, EAs function as edge terminals focusing on data acquisition, compression, and transmission, while resource-abundant cloud servers execute the LVLM inference. Although CE-CP avoids the on-device computational bot-tlenecks, it shifts the heavy burden to the communication channels. In error-prone communication environments, such as post-disaster rescue and deep-well exploration, commu-nication channels are typically plagued by severe channel noise and strictly limited bandwidth. Under such conditions, directly transmitting raw high-resolution visual data becomes intractable. Specifically, the massive volume of raw pixels incurs unacceptable latency, while their inherent vulnerability to noise leads to severe transmission distortion, degrading the inference performance of LVLMs. Therefore, robust Joint Source-Channel Coding (JSCC) paradigms are necessary to ensure reliable transmission. However, before designing the coding scheme, a funda-mental question must be addressed: whether to compress raw visual pixels or their higher-level semantic representations (i.e., visual tokens). Compressing visual tokens offers three distinct advantages. First, visual encoders intrinsically filter out pixel-level noise and spatial redundancy, yielding a highly compact representation that optimizes bandwidth utilization at the source. Second, despite being distilled features, to-kens retain structured correlations within the semantic space, unlocking the potential for deep compression far beyond the theoretical limits of pixel-level coding. Third, as latent representations, visual tokens are highly non-invertible, which substantially mitigates the risk of privacy leakage. Therefore, from the perspectives of both communication efficiency and privacy preservation, transmitting compressed visual tokens emerges as a highly effective paradigm [4]–[7]. To fully exploit the transmission potential of visual tokens over bandwidth-limited and error-prone communication chan-nels, we propose an efficient token pruning and JSCC method for LVLM-based 3D-VQA in cloud-edge collaboration, named arXiv:2610.35489v1 [cs.CV] 26 Oct 2026 JOURNAL OF L ATEX CLASS FILES, VOL. 14, NO. 8, AUGUST 2021 2

First, to fundamentally eliminate token-level redundancy, we propose a Local and Global Semantics-based Hybrid Token Pruning (LGS-HTP) module. By explicitly decoupling fore-ground from background tokens and jointly preserving local and global semantics, this module effectively mitigates the severe performance degradation of LVLM typically induced by massive pruning. Second, to further mitigate feature-level redundancy within the pruned tokens and enhance transmis-sion robustness, we propose a Token-based Joint Source-Channel Coding (T-JSCC) module. Constrained by the limited computational capacity of EAs, T-JSCC adopts an all-linear cascaded architecture to ensure minimal on-device overhead. Furthermore, it incorporates a Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) adaptive mechanism to dynamically mitigate the impact of fluctuating channel noise, ensuring robust token reconstruction at the cloud server. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows. 

1) TP-JSCC method: To address LVLM inference demands under on-device computational constraints and severely noisy, low-bandwidth communication channels, we propose an ef-ficient token pruning and JSCC method. The edge device handles token compression, while the cloud server performs decoding and complex LVLM inference, effectively offloading the computational burden away from the edge. 

2) LGS-HTP module: To tackle the issue of token redun-dancy, we propose the LGS-HTP module which first extracts foreground-background priors to decouple tokens, and then jointly selects local semantic tokens and global semantic tokens to perform pruning. 3) T-JSCC module: To further reduce token redundancy and mitigate severe transmission distortion of pruned to-kens over error-prone communication channels, we design a lightweight T-JSCC module based on an all-linear cascaded projection. Furthermore, we incorporate an SNR-adaptive mechanism to enable channel-aware transmission, ensuring high robustness against varying channel conditions. A. 3D-VQA 

Existing 3D-VQA methods can be broadly categorized into three paradigms according to the representation modality of the input data: point-cloud-based methods [8]–[11], hybrid methods combining point clouds and multi-view images [12]– [15], and multi-view-image-based methods [16]–[19]. Specif-ically, point-cloud and hybrid methods typically employ 3D encoders to extract high-dimensional geometric features for cross-modal fusion. However, their overall performance is severely bottlenecked by the scale and annotation quality of 3D training datasets. For instance, direct 3D point-cloud training is data-starved, as the point-cloud modality in existing high-quality datasets such as ScanQA [8] contain only about 41,000 point-cloud-text pairs, which is orders of magnitude smaller than 2D datasets (e.g., LAION [20] includes approximately 5.8 billion image-text pairs). Consequently, these models often struggle to establish robust cross-modal alignment, thereby constraining their generalization capabilities. In contrast, multi-view-image-based methods directly pro-cess 2D image sequences and leverage the powerful zero-shot capabilities of pretrained LVLMs to perform 3D-VQA reasoning, effectively bypassing the point-cloud-text alignment bottleneck. A representative pipeline, LLaVA-OV [18], utilizes the frozen SigLIP [21] image encoder to extract visual to-kens and feeds them alongside text tokens into a pretrained Qwen [22] model. Motivated by this architecture, cdViews [19] introduces a lightweight attention-based view selector to replace uniform multi-view sampling, filtering for semantically relevant views to improve reasoning. Given that frequently transmitting all multi-view visual to-kens in a cloud-edge collaborative architecture inevitably intro-duces substantial question-irrelevant redundancy and severely degrades communication efficiency, we adopt the novel multi-view paradigm (cdViews + LLaVA-OV) as our base architec-ture. Utilizing view selection as a front-end filter, we construct a robust token compression framework upon this foundation to resolve the inherent transmission bottleneck. 

B. Visual Token Pruning 

Although LVLMs have significantly advanced 3D-VQA, their superior capability intrinsically relies on processing a massive number of visual tokens. The direct transmission of these tokens which are semantically redundant [23], degrades communication efficiency while simultaneously imposing an unnecessary computational burden on cloud servers [24]. Con-sequently, visual token pruning has emerged as an indispens-able strategy to jointly minimize both communication payload and LVLM computation overhead. Existing token pruning approaches can be classified into text-dependent [25]–[28] and text-independent [29]–[35] cate-gories. Text-dependent pruning utilizes cross-modal attention mechanisms to measure semantic alignment between visual tokens and textual queries. Although such methods theo-retically preserve more task-relevant features, they face a severe operational trade-off: lightweight alignment networks often fail to establish accurate cross-modal connections, in-creasing the risk of irreversible removal of critical tokens [36]; conversely, employing heavy neural networks to enhance alignment precision incurs prohibitive computational overhead [26], fundamentally contradicting the objective of pruning. In contrast, text-independent methods aim to preserve max-imum semantic information by assessing token importance via intrinsic statistical properties [29]. Being predominantly training-free [29]–[35], these methods avoid the ambiguity of supervision labels in weakly defined pruning tasks [28] and provide strong generalization across diverse applications. C. Deep Joint Source-Channel Coding 

Conventional communication systems founded upon Shan-non’s separation principle often suffer from the ‘cliff ef-fect’ in low-latency and finite-blocklength regimes, wherein a slight degradation in channel quality causes an abrupt per-formance collapse [37]–[40]. In contrast, deep-learning-based JSCC performs end-to-end joint optimization. This paradigm demonstrates superior robustness to variations in communi-cation channel conditions and exhibits graceful rather than catastrophic performance degradation, perfectly aligning with highly dynamic practical error-prone communication channels [38], [41]. Existing deep JSCC methods are broadly categorized into human-perception oriented [38], [42], [43] and machine-perception oriented [44], [45] approaches. The former max-imizes high-fidelity pixel reconstruction (evaluated by metrics like Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio, i.e., PSNR), which inherently preserves a massive amount of task-irrelevant information [44]. Therefore, machine-perception JSCC aligns better with vision tasks by directly transmitting essential semantic features optimized for tasks. Nevertheless, applying existing machine-perception JSCC to our cloud-edge LVLM framework faces two obstacles. First, there is a mismatch in representational modality: most existing methods focus on compressing CNN feature maps [44]–[47], and are not intrinsically designed for the discrete token sequences required by LVLMs. Second, there is a critical mismatch in computational constraints: although a few token-based JSCC schemes have recently emerged [4]–[7], they heavily rely on computationally intensive Transformer archi-tectures and do not take the intra and inter token redundancy into account. Such Transformer-based designs impose unac-ceptable memory, communication, and computation burdens on compute-sensitive EAs in CE-CP. This precisely motivates our design of a highly streamlined, Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)-based Lightweight Token-JSCC method tailored for robust token transmission under strict edge constraints. As illustrated in Fig. 1, building upon the CE-CP defined in Section III, our proposed method directly tackles the transmission bottleneck of massive visual tokens via a highly efficient two-stage strategy. First, to fundamentally reduce the communication payload and LVLM computational overhead, we design the LGS-HTP module to achieve token-level compression. Guided by pro-jected tokens TP and priors from a lightweight segmentation network, it performs token pruning on the native tokens TN

to obtain a subset of tokens T TP N , while preserving both local and global semantics. Second, to further compress the tokens and transmit them over a noisy communication channel, we introduce the T-JSCC module. At the edge device, it maps the pruned tokens 

T TP N into robust communication channel symbols. At the cloud server, it reconstructs these symbols into reconstructed visual tokens bT TP N , which are then projected to bT TP P and aligned with the reliably transmitted textual tokens Ttxt to execute LVLM reasoning and generate the final answer r. The detailed designs of the LGS-HTP and T-JSCC modules are elaborated in Section IV-A and IV-B, respectively.

B. Token Joint Source Channel Coding 

While the LGS-HTP module effectively eliminates token-level redundancy, significant redundancy exists along the chan-nel dimension of the pruned tokens T TP N . To further mitigate this channel-level redundancy and ensure robust transmis-sion under the limited computational constraints of EAs, we propose a T-JSCC module which achieves end-to-end token compression and noise robustness with strictly bounded on-device overhead. 

1) Lightweight Token-JSCC Architecture: As illustrated in Fig. 5, the proposed T-JSCC adopts a symmetric MLP-based encoder-decoder architecture. Crucially, it dynamically incor-porates the channel state (SNR) γ as a conditioning variable, which can be accurately estimated in real time using dedi-cated equipment and algorithms at the receiver [53], thereby providing channel-aware adaptivity across fluctuating channel conditions. Specifically, for an arbitrary token in the i-th view, denoted as ti,n ∈ RDN , we concatenate it with γ to form the network input [ti,n ; γ] ∈ RDN+1 . The encoder Fenc JSCC employs a pro-gressive bottleneck design to reduce dimensionality, mapping the conditional input to the channel symbols: 

yi,n = Fenc JSCC ([ ti,n ; γ]) , (18) where the dimensional transitions across the hidden layers strictly follow the configurations in Fig. 5, i.e., DN + 1 →

512 → 256 → 128 → Dc. This bottlenecked MLP minimizes computational overhead and subsequently reduces inference latency while preserving its nonlinear representation ability. Furthermore, the encoder and decoder process each token independently and in parallel with shared parameters, ensuring the overall complexity scales linearly with the number of input tokens to facilitate on-device deployment. Subject to average power constraints [39], the encoded symbols are transmitted over an error-prone communication channel. The decoder Fdec JSCC mirrors the bottleneck architec-ture of Fenc JSCC and symmetrically injects the channel state γ,reconstructing each token as 

bti,n = Fdec JSCC ([ byi,n ; γ]) . (19) After reconstructing the total native token set bT TP N , we employ the identical pre-trained projector Fproj (·) on the cloud server to linearly project it to bT TP P for the subsequent LVLM reasoning. 

2) Loss Design for T-JSCC module: Imposing a reconstruc-tion constraint only on the native tokens ( bT TP N ) is insufficient to guarantee the reconstruction quality of the projected tokens ( bT TP P ) obtained via the projection Fproj . This is because neural network projections inherently amplify minor input perturbations [54], even slight reconstruction errors in the reconstructed native tokens can be significantly magnified by the projector. To address this, we formulate a coarse-to-fine joint con-straint. The native reconstruction loss acts as a coarse-grained constraint, stabilizing the decoded bT TP N to provide a proper convergence direction. Meanwhile, the projected reconstruc-tion loss serves as a fine-grained constraint, ensuring the reconstruction quality of the projected tokens. Specifically, we define the coarse-grained reconstruction loss between the reconstructed native tokens bT TP N and the target native tokens T TP N as

A. Experimental Setup 

We evaluated our method on the ScanQA [8] and SQA3D [9] benchmarks. ScanQA provides ∼41K QA pairs covering diverse tasks (e.g., recognition and spatial reasoning), while SQA3D offers ∼33K questions emphasizing situated under-standing. Given its comprehensive nature, ScanQA serves as our primary testbed for all detailed analyses and ablation studies. Meanwhile, we further evaluate our model on SQA3D to validate its generalizability. For quantitative evaluation, we employed two complemen-tary metrics: Top-1 Exact Match (EM) and Consensus-based Image Description Evaluation (CIDEr). Top-1 EM strictly measures exact string-matching accuracy, serving as a direct indicator of absolute correctness. Meanwhile, CIDEr evaluates n-gram similarities, providing robustness against synonymous expressions to comprehensively assess semantic consistency and linguistic quality. Throughout our experiments and hy-perparameter selection, we jointly considered both metrics to optimally balance strict reasoning accuracy with textual expressiveness. For all experiments, we employed LLaVA-OV [18] as the foundational LVLM. As a state-of-the-art (SOTA) open-source LVLM, LLaVA-OV performs excellent in high-resolution vi-sual representation and multi-view reasoning, making it an ideal baseline architecture to validate the effectiveness of our proposed token compression framework.

Therefore, in this section, we primarily investigate the overall effectiveness of our proposed method, as well as the impact of different SNR training strategies on the model’s noise resilience. We first evaluate a baseline strategy that uniformly samples the SNR from 0 ∼25 dB during training, named UFM. The corresponding experimental results are presented in Table IX. First, a direct comparison between Table VI and Table IX highlights the critical necessity of our proposed T-JSCC method. Under identical SNR conditions, particularly in ex-treme low-SNR regimes (e.g., at 0 dB), the integration of T-JSCC yields a dramatic performance improvement over the baseline (boosting the Top-1 EM from a mere 7.55 to 26.97). This indicates that our method successfully mitigates the severe impact of channel noise on 3D-VQA performance. Nevertheless, further analysis of Table IX reveals that the UFM strategy leaves substantial room for improvement. Notably, as the SNR improves to favorable conditions (e.g., 20 and 25 dB), the performance exhibits a counter-intuitive decline rather than a continuous increase. We attribute this anomaly to the training instability caused by directly mixing high and low SNR samples. Because the reconstruction loss under extreme noise is inherently much larger than that under clean channels, jointly optimizing them with equal probability leads to severe gradient fluctuations. This prevents the model from stably converging across varying channel conditions. To mitigate this optimization bias, we introduce a progres-sive (PRG) sampling strategy, adopting an anti-curriculum learning [55] schedule. During early training epochs, we strictly confine SNR sampling to the high-noise regime (e.g., 0∼5 dB), compelling the encoder to prioritize noise-robust semantic feature extraction. As training advances, we in-crementally expand the sampling range epoch by epoch to encompass the full 0 ∼25 dB spectrum. The corresponding experimental results are presented in Table X. A comparative analysis between Table IX and Table X re-veals that the PRG strategy significantly enhances the model’s low-SNR resilience. Notably, under severe channel conditions (e.g., SNR = 0 dB and 5 dB), the Top-1 EM achieves substantial improvements. Concurrently, the peak performance is firmly sustained under favorable channel conditions (e.g., SNR = 25 dB). More importantly, instead of the counter-intuitive decline at higher SNRs, the performance now exhibits a stable and consistent upward trajectory as channel condi-tions improve, successfully resolving the training instability observed in the UFM baseline.

This paper focuses on a novel token-oriented CE-CP to address the severe computational constraints of EAs and the communication bottlenecks in 3D-VQA tasks over error-prone communication environments. Specifically, we proposed a highly efficient two-stage compression strategy comprising an LGS-HTP module and a T-JSCC module. By leveraging foreground-background priors and a joint selection mechanism for local and global semantic tokens, the LGS-HTP module ef-fectively mitigates semantic redundancy. Subsequently, the T-JSCC module achieves robust, channel-aware token transmis-sion by using an all-linear cascaded architecture and an SNR-adaptive mechanism. Extensive evaluations on the ScanQA dataset indicate the exceptional efficiency and robustness of the proposed method. Notably, our method reduces the edge-to-cloud transmission payload by approximately 180 ×. The LGS-HTP module incurs less than a 1% CIDEr loss at a 90% pruning ratio, and remarkably, under extreme channel noise (SNR = 0 dB), the end-to-end CIDEr score drops by a mere 4.9% with the help of the proposed T-JSCC module, while saving 90% computational complexity for the LVLM.
