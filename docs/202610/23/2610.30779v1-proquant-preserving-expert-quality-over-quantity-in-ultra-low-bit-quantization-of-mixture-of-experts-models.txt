# ProQuant: Preserving Expert Quality over Quantity in Ultra-Low-Bit Quantization of Mixture-of-Experts Models 

Jiaqi Chen 1*, Xianglong Yan 1*, Chengzhu Bao 1, Xie Chen 1, Yulun Zhang 1†1Shanghai Jiao Tong University  

> Correspondence: yulun100@gmail.com

Abstract 

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) large language mod-els reduce computation through sparse ac-tivation but still incur substantial storage costs. Mixed-precision post-training quanti-zation (PTQ) reduces these costs by quantiz-ing different model components at different bit widths, but existing methods often strug-gle to preserve generation quality under tight bit budgets. We identify two key challenges: (1) nonlinear quantization errors at extremely low precision are underestimated when cross-layer propagation is ignored; (2) direct router fine-tuning may cause routing drift, and cal-ibration gains may not generalize to down-stream tasks. To address these issues, we pro-pose ProQuant, a weight-only PTQ framework for MoE models. Prune-or-Prioritize Mixed-Precision Allocation (PoP) jointly optimizes expert pruning and bit-width assignment. It allows higher-precision experts to substitute for severely degraded ones while improving bit-budget utilization. Routing-Consistent RM-SNorm Finetune (RoCo) adapts expert input representations through mathematically equiva-lent transformations. It compensates for quan-tization errors to reduce routing drift. Exten-sive experiments demonstrate that ProQuant outperforms existing methods across diverse generative tasks. Code is available at https: //github.com/ThrowBug/ProQuant.git .

However, these approaches still face two key challenges. First, existing mixed-precision allocation strategies typically rely on manually designed importance metrics and consecutive bit-width search spaces. Although these metrics can capture local quantization errors, our statistical ex-periments demonstrate that they may still underes-timate how extremely low-bit quantization affects model performance (see Fig. 2). Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose ProQuant , a weight-only PTQ framework for large-scale MoE models that integrates mixed-precision allocation with lightweight post-quantization compensation. • We design PoP , which adopts a prune-or-prioritize joint decision to avoid severe degra-dation from extremely low-precision experts and better utilize limited bit budgets. • We develop RoCo , which decouples represen-tation adaptation from routing behavior and compensates for quantization errors while pre-serving routing consistency. This avoids rout-ing drift caused by directly fine-tuning full-precision parameters. • Experiments on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 and Qwen3.5-35B-A3B validate Pro-Quant on diverse generative tasks at average expert precisions of 1.5, 2.0, and 2.5 bits. 

2 Related Works 

2.1 Post-Training Quantization 

Post-training quantization (PTQ) directly converts pretrained models into low-precision representa-tions without costly retraining. A representative di-rection is second-order error compensation, where GPTQ (Frantar et al., 2023) leverages Hessian in-formation to compensate for quantization errors in the remaining weights during sequential column-wise quantization. Building on GPTQ, GPTAQ (Li et al., 2025a) and BoA (Kim et al., 2025) extend second-order error compensation by accounting for inter-layer error accumulation and cross-projection dependencies, respectively. Activation-aware scal-ing is employed in AWQ (Lin et al., 2024b) to protect salient weight channels, whereas other studies (Dettmers et al., 2024; Lee et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2024; Yuan et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2024, 2025c) exploit differences in weight saliency or quantization sensitivity to adopt differentiated quantization strategies. 2.2 Quantization of MoE Models 

MoE models pose additional quantization chal-lenges due to sparse expert activation. To capture token–expert relevance, MoEQuant (Chen et al., 2025c) and VEQ (Qin et al., 2026) incorporate token–expert affinity into calibration and quanti-zation objectives, with VEQ further introducing modality information for VLMs to account for tex-tual and visual differences. Router-aware meth-ods (Fu et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025b; Fang and Huang, 2026) explicitly align the quantized routing behavior with its full-precision counterpart to preserve expert selection. Expert heterogene-ity also motivates mixed-precision quantization. Sensitivity-aware methods (Li et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025b; Duanmu et al., 2025; Xie et al., 2025) allocate precision according to structural or expert sensitivity. More recent studies develop principled sensitivity metrics (Chowdhury et al., 2026) or per-form global expert-level bit allocation with router fine-tuning (Deng et al., 2026). Beyond mixed-precision allocation, other works directly improve low-bit representations or compensate for quanti-zation errors. MiLo (Huang et al., 2025a) recon-structs quantization residuals using adaptive low-rank compensators. CodeQuant (Yin et al., 2026) combines rotation and clustering to reduce quan-tization errors, whereas KBVQ-MoE (Xu et al., 2026b) removes cross-expert redundancy and ap-plies bias correction. Our method prunes less im-portant experts to prioritize precision for others and preserves routing via router-decoupled RMSNorm fine-tuning.

3.3 Routing-Consistent RMSNorm Finetune 

Prior studies either calibrate the router through routing alignment (Fu et al., 2025; Chen et al., 2025b), or adapt it after quantization using a global output-level objective (Deng et al., 2026). How-ever, we find that neither strategy yields consis-tent improvements on downstream tasks. Routing alignment constrains the quantized model to match the routing decisions of its full-precision counter-part. However, such local alignment lacks global information about the quantized model. Therefore, it cannot effectively adjust routing to compensate for quantization errors accumulated across layers. In contrast, directly optimizing the router with a global output-level objective can adapt routing ac-cording to the quantized model. However, fitting the router to the calibration set may bias it toward experts that are more frequently activated by the calibration data. Inspired by (Xiao et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024b), we propose an RMSNorm fine-tuning method based on mathematically equivalent transformations. 4 Experiments 

4.1 Settings Implementation Details. All experiments are con-ducted using PyTorch (Paszke et al., 2019) and HuggingFace Transformers (Wolf et al., 2020) on two NVIDIA A800 GPUs. We evaluate our method on Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 (Yang et al., 2025) and Qwen3.5-35B-A3B (Qwen Team, 2026). We use 128 sequences of 2,048 tokens sampled from C4 (Raffel et al., 2020) with seed 0 as the calibration set. For both softmax and linear atten-tion layers, the Q, K, V, and O projection weights are uniformly quantized to 4 bits, while all other linear-attention weights and router weights remain in full precision. We consider average expert bit-widths of 2.5, 2.0, and 1.5 bits with a candidate set of 0, 2, 3 bits, and quantize the shared experts of Qwen3.5 to 4 bits. See Appendix B for details. 

Models and Evaluation. We observe that the conventional zero-shot evaluation with lm-eval (Bi-derman et al., 2024), which is widely adopted in prior work, may not fully reflect model capabil-ity on practical generative tasks. Therefore, we primarily use EvalScope (Team, 2024) to evalu-ate generation-based performance across diverse capabilities, such as mathematical reasoning, sci-entific reasoning, and instruction following, on six generative benchmarks: GPQA-Diamond (Rein et al., 2024), MATH-500 (Hendrycks et al., 2021; Lightman et al., 2024), IFEval (Zhou et al., 2023), GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021), MMLU-Redux (Gema et al., 2025) and BFCL-v3 (Patil et al., 2025). We also report perplexity and zero-shot results as auxiliary metrics. 

Baselines. We compare ProQuant with rep-resentative mixed-precision quantization meth-ods for MoE LLMs, including PMQ from MC-MoE (Huang et al., 2025b), MxMoE (Duanmu et al., 2025), and GEMQ (Deng et al., 2026). For fair comparison, all methods use GPTQ (Frantar et al., 2023) with their respective bit-width alloca-tion or post-quantization compensation strategies.

Perplexity and Log-Likelihood Evaluation. 

Furthermore, following previous works, we report perplexity on WikiText-2 and C4, and conduct zero-shot likelihood-based evaluation using lm-eval in Table 2. The results show that ProQuant improves performance on realistic generation tasks while maintaining comparable results on conventional likelihood-based benchmarks. However, when con-sidered together with Table 1, these results sug-gest that strong performance on PPL and zero-shot likelihood-based metrics does not necessarily translate into better generation ability. In partic-ular, some mixed-precision quantization methods achieve favorable results on traditional likelihood-based evaluations, yet perform worse than a uni-formly quantized 2.0-bit baseline on generation-based tasks. This indicates that token-level like-lihood estimation, which underlies these conven-tional metrics, cannot fully reflect the practical ca-pabilities of quantized models in open-ended gener-ation scenarios and may lead to biased conclusions about the effectiveness of MoE quantization meth-ods. Therefore, we argue that generation-based evaluation is essential for a comprehensive assess-ment of MoE quantization models.

Thanks to the sparse routing mecha-nism of MoE models, a removed expert can be replaced by a slightly lower-ranked expert that re-tains higher precision. PoP prunes experts impaired by extremely low precision and reallocates their bit budget to preserve others’ effectiveness. 

Effect of RoCo. We evaluate the effectiveness of RoCo by comparing the full ProQuant frame-work with two ablation variants: w/o RoCo, which completely removes RoCo, and Direct RMSNorm FT, which directly fine-tunes RMSNorm without preserving routing consistency. The average score drops from 67.30 to 64.77 without RoCo, indi-cating that lightweight RMSNorm fine-tuning can effectively mitigate quantization-induced perfor-mance degradation. Direct RMSNorm FT im-proves the average score to 65.91, but still underper-forms the full ProQuant framework. This suggests that directly updating RMSNorm can provide par-tial recovery, but may also alter the router input and disturb the original routing behavior. RoCo decouples representation adaptation from routing, correcting quantization errors while preserving ex-pert selection and improving recovery on genera-tive tasks. 

Sensitivity to the Pruning Ratio. Since PoP and RoCo are decoupled, we analyze the effect of the maximum pruning ratio ρ without RoCo fine-tuning, so as to isolate the sensitivity of PoP to this hyperparameter. On Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507, the average performance varies only slightly as ρ ranges from 0.10 to 0.30, indicating that PoP is relatively robust to ρ within this range on this model. When ρ exceeds 0.30, the constraint is no longer active, and further increasing it does not af-fect the final allocation. The results also show that a moderate maximum pruning ratio is generally favorable for achieving higher average accuracy. 

5 Conclusion 

In this work, we presented ProQuant, which pre-serves MoE LLM performance on downstream generation tasks. More importantly, our study of-fers two insights into MoE quantization. First, we advocate a principle for extremely low-bit mixed-precision allocation: “discard it rather than de-stroy it.” When the bit budget cannot adequately preserve an expert’s functionality, removing it may be preferable to retaining a severely degraded ver-sion. Second, we caution against over-optimizing for calibration data or specific metrics and empha-size generalization to downstream tasks. This also calls for reexamining the use of perplexity and log-likelihood metrics: they measure a model’s abil-ity to assign probabilities to reference sequences, rather than to generate text autoregressively based on its own outputs, and cannot replace real-world generation evaluations. We hope these findings encourage MoE quantization research to prioritize model reliability and cross-task generalization. 8Limitations 

In this work, we focus on mixed-precision PTQ for large-scale MoE LLMs and evaluate ProQuant on models with up to 35 billion total parameters. Although ProQuant demonstrates strong genera-tion performance at this scale, resource constraints prevented us from extending our experiments to models with hundreds of billions or trillions of pa-rameters. Its effectiveness and scalability at these larger scales therefore remain to be validated.

A ProQuant Pipeline and Algorithms 

As shown in Fig. 3, ProQuant consists of three stages. First, as illustrated in Algorithm 1, we tra-verse the MoE layers of the pretrained model and evaluate the quantization cost of each expert under different candidate bit-widths, thereby determining the final bit-width of each expert under the global bit budget. We then apply GPTQ according to the resulting allocation to obtain the integer represen-tations of the quantized weights, together with the corresponding scales and zero-points. Finally, as shown in Algorithm 2, we freeze all other model parameters and fine-tune the RMSNorm scaling vectors to compensate for quantization errors. Af-ter fine-tuning, the learned scaling vectors are fused into the corresponding RMSNorm weights, while their element-wise inverses are fused into the asso-ciated router weights, yielding the final ProQuant model.

D Comparative Analysis of Expert Importance Metrics 

We compare the effects of different expert impor-tance metrics on mixed-precision allocation and further examine the need to explicitly remove the influence of expert activation frequency. For a fair comparison, we use {1, 2, 3} as the candidate ex-pert bit-widths, fix the average expert bit-width at 2.0 bits, and keep the subsequent allocation proce-dure and all other quantization settings identical. No post-quantization finetuning is performed for any method. As shown in Table 6, our importance metric achieves an average score of 61.40 across six bench-marks, outperforming the metrics used in MxMoE, PMQ, and GEMQ without finetuning by 11.51, 11.75, and 11.18 percentage points, respectively. These results demonstrate that the definition of ex-pert importance substantially affects downstream performance under the same bit-width budget and allocation settings. To examine the influence of activation frequency, we introduce PoP ∗, which does not explicitly divide the expert quantization cost by the expert activa-tion count, while keeping all other settings iden-tical to PoP. Explicitly removing the influence of activation frequency increases the average score from 47.84 to 61.40 and improves performance on five of the six benchmarks, with a decrease on MMLU-Redux. We hypothesize that expert acti-vation patterns on the C4 calibration set may be more closely aligned with those on MMLU-Redux. Such alignment could favor importance metrics that incorporate activation frequency on MMLU-Redux, while retaining this frequency dependence leads to lower performance on all other evaluated benchmarks.
