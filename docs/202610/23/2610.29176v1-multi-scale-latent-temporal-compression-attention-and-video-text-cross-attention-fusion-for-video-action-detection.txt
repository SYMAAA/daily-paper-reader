## Multi-Scale Latent Temporal Compression Attention and Video-Text Cross Attention Fusion for Video Action Detection 

Yicheng Qiu [0009-0006-8841-8648] and Keiji Yanai (B)[0000-0002-0431-183X] 

The University of Electro-Communications, Tokyo, Japan 

qiu-y@mm.inf.uec.ac.jp, yanai@cs.uec.ac.jp 

Abstract. In video action detection, modeling the temporal dependen-cies in continuous video streams is crucial. Previous methods based on deep neural networks can perform frame-level video recognition tasks. However, these methods struggle to model dynamic temporal patterns, facing challenges such as redundant frame information, difficulty balanc-ing global and local features, and insufficient fusion of visual and textual features. To address these issues, we propose a video temporal attention module based on the Multi-Scale Latent Temporal Compression Atten-tion, which aggregates continuous temporal sequences into feature repre-sentations across diverse temporal scales while efficiently reducing redun-dancy. We also introduce a Video-Text Cross Attention Fusion module that leverages cross-attention at both latent and standard feature levels, achieving fine-grained fusion between video and textual features to en-hance semantic alignment and feature interaction. Experimental results across multiple video recognition benchmarks demonstrate that our pro-posed methods achieve superior performance compared to existing meth-ods, validating the effectiveness and generalization capability. 

Keywords: Video Action Detection · Temporal Attention · Video-Text Fusion · Anomaly Detection 

1 Introduction 

Video action detection is a critical task in multimodal video understanding, with a wide range of real-world applications such as intelligent surveillance [26,35,37], autonomous driving [6, 30], human behavior analysis [34, 55], and embodied in-telligence [9]. Robust models are required to recognize and temporally localize predefined actions as well as abnormal events such as accidents or illegal activi-ties in long, untrimmed video streams. Early approaches extract spatio-temporal features with 3D convolutional networks, while recent methods build on Trans-former architectures, state space models [14, 38], and multimodal pre-training to model longer temporal context and to align visual content with category se-mantics. Trans-former attention [47] is widely used to model temporal context in videos, but two problems remain. First, attention over long sequences repeatedly processes a large amount of redundant frame information, which limits both feature qual-ity and training efficiency. Existing remedies either apply pooling [44], which irreversibly discards fine-grained details, or window-based attention [27], which restricts the receptive field and misses long-range dependencies; recent efficient temporal models [4] still struggle to obtain global context and local precision at the same time. To address this issue, we propose a video temporal attention module based on Multi-Scale Latent Temporal Compression Attention, termed MLTC-Attention. The key insight is to first project features into a compact la-tent space and then compress the temporal dimension at multiple scales before performing attention, rather than attending to all raw features directly. This compress-then-attend design, inspired by recent sparse and compressed atten-tion [50,61], filters temporal redundancy while larger compression scales preserve global context and smaller scales preserve local details, and a gate re-weights the scales according to the input content. Second, multimodal learning has become a key route to better video un-derstanding, but previous video-text methods [49, 68] align video and text fea-tures at a single representation level, which limits the depth of cross-modal interaction. To address this limitation, we propose a multimodal feature fusion module, termed Video-Text Cross Attention Fusion (VTCA-Fusion), which en-forces cross-modal alignment at two complementary levels: the standard feature space captures explicit semantic correspondences, while a parallel latent space branch, inspired by latent space feature interaction [62], captures compressed cross-modal patterns. By exploiting the shared category information between video and text, the module achieves fine-grained alignment and complementar-ity, producing fused features that are more discriminative. – MLTC-Attention, which compresses features in a multi-scale temporal la-tent space and adaptively extracts dynamic features across different tem-poral scales. It strikes a balance between global and local feature extrac-tion, thereby enhancing the ability to efficiently process temporal features in video. 

– VTCA-Fusion, which utilizes cross attention between video features and en-hanced category text features at both the standard and the latent level. It overcomes the limitations of existing methods in fusing video and text fea-tures, and achieves fine-grained alignment and complementarity between the two modalities. 

– The framework, built around the MLTC-Attention and VTCA-Fusion mod-ules, is applicable to various video action detection tasks. Experimental val-idation on four benchmarks demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed modules. 

2 Related Work 

2.1 Video Understanding Models 

Video representation learning has progressed from 3D CNNs such as C3D [46] and I3D [3], to Transformers such as ViViT [1] and Video Swin Transformer [27], self-supervised VideoMAE [48] and InternVideo [51], and SSM-based Video-Mamba [19]. On the multimodal side, CLIP [40], ImageBind [13], and Language-Bind [68] align vision with language and other modalities, Video-LLaVA [22] and InternVL [8] connect video encoders with large language models, and M2-CLIP [49] adapts multimodal models to video action recognition. 

2.2 Video Action Detection Models 

Temporal action detection has evolved from boundary-matching methods like BMN [23] and graph-based G-TAD [56] to Transformer-based approaches such as ActionFormer [64]. TriDet [42] employs multi-scale feature pyramids, Tem-poralMaxer [44] uses max-pooling, ActionMamba [4] and efficient temporal at-tention with state space models [36, 39] leverage SSM [14] for global temporal modeling, and recent works [12,20] explore cross-layer decoupling and boundary refinement. 

2.3 Video Anomaly Detection Models 

MIL-based methods [43] learn anomaly scores from video-level labels and were later improved with graph convolutions [66], feature magnitude learning [45], multi-sequence Transformers [21], and dual memory units [67]. Recent methods transfer CLIP [40] knowledge to weakly supervised anomaly detection [33,53,62], use text prompts for normality guidance [59] or MIL enhancement [5], and model short and long anomalies across temporal scales [31]. 4 Y. 3.3 Multi-Scale Latent Temporal Compression Attention Block 

Standard self-attention [47] over the full temporal sequence incurs O(τ 2) com-plexity, while pooling-based methods [44] reduce cost but irreversibly discard fine-grained information. Window-based attention [27] restricts the receptive field and cannot model long-range dependencies. Our MLTC addresses this by first projecting features into a lower-dimensional latent space to reduce per-token cost, then compressing the temporal dimension at multiple scales so that larger scales capture global context while smaller scales preserve local dynamics. At-tending to the compressed keys and values reduces the cost of each compression branch from O(τ 2) to O(τ 2/σ i), while a single global self-attention branch is re-tained to preserve the full receptive field. 3.4 Loss Functions Loss of Video Anomaly Detection. Following the standard weakly super-vised setting [43, 53, 67], only video-level annotations are available for training: given the video-level label Y ∈ { 0, 1}, 0 for normal and 1 for abnormal, we broadcast it along the temporal dimension as yi = Y for i = 1 , · · · , τ , so that no frame-level annotation is used. A binary cross-entropy loss La-nBCE is computed between the predicted scores P t and y. 4 Experiments 

4.1 Datasets and Evaluation Metrics 

For video anomaly detection we use UCF-Crime [43], over 1,900 surveillance videos covering 13 real-world abnormal events together with normal activities, and XD-Violence [52], about 4,700 videos from movies and social media cov-ering 6 categories of abnormal events. For temporal action detection we use THUMOS14 [16], 413 untrimmed videos with temporal annotations over 20 ac-tion categories, and ActivityNet [2], 19,994 videos with temporal annotations over 200 action categories. Following common practice, we report the frame-level AUC of the ROC curve on UCF-Crime and the frame-level Average Pre-cision (AP) on XD-Violence, which better reflects the precision–recall balance under class imbalance; for action detection we report the mean Average Pre-cision (mAP) averaged over multiple temporal IoU (tIoU) thresholds between predicted and ground-truth segments. 

4.2 Implementation Details 

We implemented our method on a single NVIDIA L40S GPU. Both pre-trained encoders fV (·) and fT (·) are frozen; only the projection layers, the learnable prompts, VTCA-Fusion, MLTC-Attention and the task head are trained, so all text-encoder variants share the same protocol and schedule. During the training process, we use the AdamW [28] optimizer and employ a scheduler that integrates linear warm-up and cosine annealing to adjust the learning rate, which is set to 1e-5. The batch size was set to 64. For video data input, we utilized pretrained encoder weights to extract video features, and the sequence length of video feature input is 512 frames. Each frame was resized to a resolution of 224 × 224 

pixels. For the text input with the enhanced text prompt design, where each textual class label is embedded with 10 learnable prompt sequence length. The maximum text sequence length is set to 79, following the configuration of CLIP [40]. The model was trained for 20 epochs.

4.5 Ablation Studies 

To better analyze the contribution of each component in our model, we con-duct ablation studies on the representative benchmarks UCF-Crime [43] and THUMOS14 [16]. 

Contribution of Our Proposed Modules. As shown in Table 3, we ab-late each module within the overall architecture and evaluate on representative datasets for each task. First, for the MLTC-Attention module, we replace it with standard attention. The results on both UCF-Crime and THUMOS14 datasets show a noticeable performance decline, demonstrating the superiority of MLTC-Attention in capturing temporal features. We also conducted experiments on the effect of the Top-k selection, which retains the top 1/8 of the compressed blocks at each scale, at least one block, following strategies in previous works [42]. Its removal led to a slight performance decrease on both datasets, further validating the effectiveness of this filtering mechanism in focusing on the most informative temporal segments. When the VTCA-Fusion module is removed, the correla-tion between video and text features weakens, resulting in a performance drop on both tasks. Finally, the latent component, which applies latent space atten-tion within both the MLTC-Attention and VTCA-Fusion modules, is crucial for understanding key semantic features in latent space; its removal degrades performance significantly. 

Alternative of Our Proposed Modules. We replace MLTC-Attention or VTCA-Fusion with several previously used temporal and cross-modal atten-tion modules. As shown in Table 4, substituting MLTC with Self-Attention, Soft-Attention, or MS-TCT consistently lowers performance, indicating that multi-scale temporal compression is crucial for modeling long-range dependen-cies. 5 Conclusion 

We tackle key problems in multimodal video action detection by our novel so-lutions. First, we propose the MLTC-Attention module, which utilizes a multi-scale temporal latent space compression attention. It is designed to adaptively capture important dynamic features across different temporal scales, effectively reducing the computational redundancy and inefficiency. Second, we present the VTCA-Fusion module, which employs cross attention between video content and enhanced multi-category text. This enables precise alignment and complemen-tary integration of visual and textual features, addressing limitations in existing multimodal feature integration methods. Extensive experiments on temporal ac-tion detection and video anomaly detection benchmarks demonstrate that our methods consistently improve accuracy and achieve superior overall performance.
