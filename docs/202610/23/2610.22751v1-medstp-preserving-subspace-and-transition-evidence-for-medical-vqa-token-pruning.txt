## Abstract 

Medical vision-language models (VLMs) for visual ques-tion answering (VQA) often pass long visual-token se-quences to the language model, making visual-token prun-ing attractive but risky for medical images, where answers may depend on localized boundary and texture evidence. We study training free pre-decoding pruning and treat prun-ing as a subspace stable evidence preservation problem. The retained tokens should remain close to the dominant vi-sual evidence subspace induced by the full token set. To this end, we propose Subspace-Transition Token Pruning (Med-STP), which estimates each token’s spectral support from principal component space and constructs a compact rep-resentation subspace. To better reflect medical image struc-ture, MedSTP further uses a bounded token-grid transition prior to promote boundary and texture sensitive tokens. We evaluate MedSTP on the closed-ended SLAKE and Omn-iMedVQA benchmarks and on RadImageNet-VQA, which provides both closed-ended and open-ended evaluations, using Lingshu-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B backbones. Med-STP delivers consistently strong performance on both the closed-ended and open-ended RadImageNet-VQA evalua-tions. These results show that MedSTP is an effective train-ing free pruning framework for medical domain VQA. 

## 1. Introduction 

Medical VQA is increasingly limited by the visual token budget rather than by the question text. Modern VLMs pass long image-token sequences into the language model, and recent backbones such as Qwen3-VL make this cost central to practical inference [6]. For medical images, this pressure matters: answers may depend on small boundary or texture 

Figure 1. Qualitative comparison under the same compact token budget. The dashed red circle marks the relevant abnormal region; MedSTP keeps boundary and texture evidence that generic prun-ing may miss. 

changes, not only on the dominant anatomy [3]. Visual token pruning is the natural compression tool. In ViTs, DynamicViT and EViT remove tokens with learned predictors or class-token attention [37, 42], while Token Merging (ToMe) merges similar patches at runtime [8]. In VLMs, FastV uses early visual attention [11], SparseVLM uses text relevance [49], VisPruner and PyramidDrop target duplicate or low-utility tokens [45, 48], BTP and PruneSID add diversity or grouping control [24, 35], and TransPrune and Photon use representation transitions or instruction-conditioned scheduling [23, 34]. This line makes fixed bud-get pruning practical. It also leaves open what the budget should protect in medical VQA. 1arXiv:2610.22751v1 [cs.CV] 15 Oct 2026 The open question is not whether to prune, but what a fixed budget selector should treat as evidence. Most med-ical token reduction so far lives in segmentation and vol-ume understanding, where dense masks, edge-aware halt-ing, or instruction-conditioned scheduling can exploit task structure during training [23, 25, 50]. Frozen-VLM medical VQA is more constrained: before language decoding, the selector must choose K visual tokens with no dense super-vision, no task-specific retraining, and no repeated subset decoding. Under that setting, a selector driven by generic saliency or redundancy can keep the dominant anatomy and still discard boundary or texture tokens that receive lower priority under generic scoring, as illustrated in Fig. 1. This failure mode has a representation-level reading. The full token set spans a visual evidence subspace, and the retained subset should stay close enough to it for the lan-guage model to receive the leading evidence directions. We take this subspace-stable evidence preservation as the se-lection target. Redundancy then becomes a budget error: a subset may keep high-energy tokens while spending most of its budget on one anatomical pattern. The harder case runs the other way. Subtle boundary and texture transitions can carry subspace support, yet a generic saliency or redun-dancy score may rank them below the dominant anatomy. Preserving the subspace is necessary, but a rule that scores tokens by global participation alone can still deprioritize these transitions, which can matter in medical images [3]. We propose Subspace-Transition Token Pruning (MedSTP) , a training free selector for the visual tokens of a frozen VLM. MedSTP has two coupled parts. The first forms a subspace-stable subset: token-side PCA defines the visual-evidence subspace, and the retained set is built pro-gressively by selecting, at each step, the token that adds the most previously unrepresented information to the current subspace. A short perturbation argument on the retained-token second moment matrix (Prop. 1) explains why high-participation, non-redundant tokens help keep the leading subspace stable. The second part adds local sensitivity. MedSTP reads a Sobel-style transition score from the VLM token grid [44] and applies it as a bounded multiplier on the spectral score, promoting a boundary- or texture-like token when it already carries subspace support. The bound keeps the transition term from overriding the subspace signal. We make the following contributions: • We cast fixed budget visual-token pruning for frozen-VLM medical VQA as subspace-stable evidence preser-vation : the retained tokens should keep the leading visual subspace induced by the full token set, a target we sup-port with a perturbation argument on the retained-token second moment matrix. • We introduce bounded token-grid transition subspace construction, which promotes boundary and texture to-kens when they retain spectral support, without letting the transition term override the subspace signal. • We realize both in MedSTP, a training free pre-decoding selector that needs no dense masks or retrain-ing, and evaluate it on SLAKE, OmniMedVQA and RadImageNet-VQA with Lingshu-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B under matched insertion points and fixed token bud-gets. 

## 2. Related Work 

2.1. Visual Token Pruning and Subspace Selection 

Compression can act on several different objects. Reweighted optimization compresses DNN weights [19], while federated pruning jointly considers model sparsity and training convergence [18]. Modality compression instead allocates a limited communication budget across multimodal inputs [22]. These settings provide a broader context for resource-constrained inference [12, 29, 30]; the object compressed by MedSTP is the visual-token sequence of a frozen VLM. Token reduction has been studied extensively for vi-sion transformers because image patches are often redun-dant. DynamicViT learns lightweight prediction modules to prune tokens progressively [42], EViT reorganizes attentive and inattentive tokens using class token attention [37], and Token Merging (ToMe) merges similar tokens at runtime without additional training [8]. These methods establish the basic efficiency trade-off, but their objectives are primarily designed for generic recognition rather than medical VQA. Recent VLM and MLLM pruning methods further ex-ploit long visual token sequences before or inside lan-guage decoding. Attention or relevance based methods score tokens by early layer attention, text-to-visual relation, or instruction guided attention, as in FastV [11], Sparse-VLM [49], and TransPrune [34]. Redundancy based meth-ods remove visually duplicate or low utility tokens, in-cluding VisPruner [48], PyramidDrop [45], and BTP [35]. PruneSID combines semantic grouping with intra group non maximum suppression to balance importance and di-versity [24]. These methods provide strong general VLM pruning baselines, but they are mostly domain agnostic: the retained subset is selected from generic attention, relevance, similarity, or grouping cues, without explicitly modeling the localized boundary, texture, and transition evidence empha-sized in medical images. Recent work has also moved beyond scalar saliency scores. SVD-Prune selects tokens by leverage scores to pre-serve dominant global variance [5]; ResPrune formulates pruning as text-conditioned subspace reconstruction [36]; and SPARE treats pruning as column subset selection with explicit subspace reconstruction error [33]. MedSTP draws on this broader subspace motivation but targets a different setting. We study fixed budget pruning 2before LLM decoding for frozen medical VQA backbones, where dense masks, task-specific retraining, and repeated subset decoding are unavailable. PCA provides a clas-sical tool for identifying dominant low-dimensional sub-spaces [1, 24, 27]. Building on this general subspace view, MedSTP uses token-side PCA to define the visual-evidence space. From this PCA representation, the retained medical-evidence subspace is constructed progressively according to the additional subspace information contributed by each candidate, with a bounded token-grid transition prior incor-porated into the same selection criterion. This construction allows global representation structure and localized bound-ary and texture evidence to jointly determine the final re-tained subset. We evaluate MedSTP under matched pre-LLM token budgets and report a subspace-drift diagnostic for the retained subset. 

2.2. Medical VLMs and Medical Token Compres-sion 

Medical VLMs adapt multimodal modeling to biomedical images and clinical questions [38, 52]. MedGemma 1.5 provides a medical multimodal instruction model for image and text understanding [43], while Med-Flamingo adapts Flamingo style few shot multimodal learning to medical im-age question answering [41]. Lingshu-7B provides a medi-cal MLLM backbone with a Qwen-compatible visual token interface [46]; Qwen3-VL-8B is our general VLM back-bone for testing whether the same selector transfers beyond a medical model [6]. SLAKE [39] and OmniMedVQA [26] provide image question answer pairs across radiology and broader medical modalities. RadImageNet-VQA [9] pro-vides CT- and MRI-based radiologic questions in open-ended and closed-ended formats. BrainTRACE extends medical VLM evaluation to longitudinal, multi-sequence, and volumetric evidence in brain MRI reasoning [31], be-yond the two-dimensional evaluation studied here. Medical learning also encompasses population-level time-series analysis, such as studies of vaccine rollout and mental health [47], and image-based biological infer-ence. In the latter setting, SIGMA transfers fine Golgi morphology cues from high-resolution images to a lower-resolution pathway-state classifier [21]. For volumetric medical images, Detail Consistent Distillation preserves wavelet-domain detail during teacher–student training for efficient MRI segmentation [20]. These biomedical appli-cations contextualize the importance of choosing evidence appropriate to a task [51]; they do not evaluate visual-token pruning. Medical token pruning has mainly been studied for im-age analysis models, segmentation, or volume understand-ing rather than fixed budget 2D medical VQA. TokenSparse learns score predictors for faster medical image segmenta-tion [50]; HRViT introduces edge aware token halting to preserve boundary sensitive regions [25]; Photon studies instruction conditioned token scheduling for volume under-standing [23]; and MedPruner proposes hierarchical token pruning for 3D medical image understanding in VLMs [40]. MedSTP targets a narrower point in this space: a training free selector that operates directly on frozen VLM visual tokens under a matched pre-LLM token budget for medical VQA. 

2.3. Compact Representations and Knowledge Transfer 

Representation selection and weighting also arise in dis-tributed learning. Gradient matching condenses raw data into representative points [15], and representative knowl-edge sharing combines compact datasets with adaptive dis-tillation weights [10]. Distributed energy distance measures feature-distribution heterogeneity [16], while PFedDST se-lects collaborating peers using loss, task similarity, and se-lection frequency [17]. CRAD weights peer supervision by class-specific reliability [7]. At the token level, influence calibration allocates on-policy self-distillation supervision according to its relation to the reinforcement-learning ob-jective [32]. These works address which information to transfer or emphasize during training [14, 28, 53]. Med-STP instead retains original visual embeddings at inference time, without constructing synthetic training data, selecting peer models, or updating backbone parameters. 

## 3. Method 

MedSTP is a training free, pre-decoding selector for the vi-sual tokens passed to a frozen language model. It makes one fixed budget decision from the encoded visual tokens be-fore language decoding, without dense masks, task-specific retraining, or trial decoding of candidate subsets. Figure 2 summarizes the pipeline. Subspace construction keeps to-kens that participate in principal evidence directions, while a bounded transition cue incorporates localized medical structure into the same construction process.

## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

We evaluate MedSTP on three medical VQA bench-marks with two VLM backbones: Lingshu-7B [46], amedical VLM, and Qwen3-VL-8B [6], a general VLM. SLAKE [39] provides 2,394 evaluated image question instances, the qt-ai of OmniMedVQA [26] provides 3,329 evaluated instances for anatomy-focused VQA, and RadImageNet-VQA [9] provides 1,000 paired Anatomy-MC and Anatomy-open questions over 2D CT and MRI images. This two-backbone setting tests whether the same fixed budget pruning behavior transfers across medical and general VLMs. All methods prune visual tokens after the vision lan-guage merger and before language model insertion. Seman-tic correctness of open-ended answers is evaluated indepen-dently by two method-blind open-source judges, Selene [4] 5and Phi-4 [2], using the same fixed rubric. All results are re-ported as the point estimate ± bootstrap standard error over the evaluated instances. All experiments are run on servers equipped with NVIDIA A100 GPUs. All pruning methods are executed in the same software environment with identi-cal prompts, insertion points, and token budgets. 

Compared methods. We compare against representa-tive pruning and token-selection baselines under the same frozen-backbone protocol. FastV [11] and SparseVLM [49] represent attention- or question-relevance based selection. VisPruner [48], BTP [35], and PruneSID [24] cover re-dundancy, diversity, and grouping based selection. HRViT is the medical ViT pruning method [25]. We compared adapted versions of SVD-Prune [5], ResPrune [36], and SPARE [33], and the results are provided in the Supplemen-tary. 

4.2. Main Results 

Across the three benchmarks and both backbones, Med-STP achieves the strongest aggregate performance among the evaluated pruning methods. Table 1 reports the Om-niMedVQA results. With Lingshu-7B, MedSTP reaches 70.83% average accuracy, exceeding the strongest compet-ing method by 3.57%. With Qwen3-VL-8B, it achieves 58.82%, improving by 2.89%. Table 2 shows a consistent advantage on SLAKE. MedSTP improves the average accu-racy over the strongest competing baseline by 1.55% with Lingshu-7B and by 1.67% with Qwen3-VL-8B, reaching 76.78% and 78.45%, respectively. Table 3 further evaluates this transfer on both closed- and open-ended RadImageNet-VQA questions. With Lingshu-7B, MedSTP improves by 3.50% on closed-ended average accuracy and by 5.20% on the method-blind open-ended judge average. With Qwen3-VL-8B, the corresponding gains are 1.60% and 1.25%.

## 6. Discussion 

Across three benchmarks and two backbones, the results support that MedSTP preserves diverse representation di-rections, while the bounded transition prior adjusts this se-lection toward localized feature changes. The pruning-rate and subspace-drift analyses are consistent with better repre-sentation preservation under aggressive compression. 

Limitations. The perturbation analysis motivates sub-space preservation but does not guarantee answer correct-ness or prove that the discrete selector minimizes the spec-tral objective. Moreover, the transition prior is not a lesion detector: as a multiplier on complementary spectral con-tribution, it cannot recover a local cue with zero contribu-tion to the current selection score. Our evaluation is also limited to two-dimensional medical VQA with two VLM backbones; extending the selector to volumetric images and broader clinical tasks remains future work. 

## 7. Conclusion 

We presented Subspace-Transition Token Pruning, a train-ing free visual token pruning rule for medical VQA. Med-STP combines spectral token influence, non redundant sub-set construction, and bounded token grid transition mod-ulation. Experiments on SLAKE, OmniMedVQA and RadImageNet-VQA show improved fixed budget pruning under matched token budgets.
