# Few Bits, One Law: Toward W2A4KV2 

Kai Yi , Tarek Elgamal , Sruthikesh Surineni , Vignesh Vivekraja , Soumyadeep Ghosh , Steven Li 

Meta AI Extreme low-bit LLM compression is most challenging when weights, activations, and KV caches are quantized together: their distributions differ, and quantization errors interact throughout the network. We introduce CanonQ , a unified quantization-aware training framework that addresses these challenges by separating source canonicalization from task-aware adaptation. Fixed rotations and energy normalization map heterogeneous tensor sources to canonical coordinates, enabling frozen Gaussian-reference codebooks to be reused across layers and models. Joint training then adapts the network to the coupled errors of weight, activation, and cache quantization within a common scalar/vector interface. We bound frozen-codebook transfer error and local task loss, and derive an exact normalization-aware straight-through Jacobian that links quantization distortion to gradient bias. The strongest gains arise under joint W2A4KV2 compression: across LLaMA3-1B/3B/8B , CanonQ-Omni achieves up to 14 .28 × lower WikiText-2 perplexity and up to 57 .9% higher mean zero-shot accuracy than prior state-of-the-art and representative quantization baselines. The benefits extend to 

Qwen3-1.7B , code generation, and mathematical reasoning: on instruction-tuned MobileLLM-Pro-1B 

at W2A16KV16 , CanonQ achieves relative improvements of 41 .7% in HumanEval pass@1 and 39 .1% in GSM8K exact match over the strongest evaluated quantization baseline.

# 1 Introduction 

LLM inference is increasingly limited by memory movement: weights dominate model storage and matrix-multiplication bandwidth, activations affect prefill, and the KV cache grows with batch size and context length. Quantization is therefore central to deployment, yet the most attractive regimes, which include W1/W2 

weights, A4 activations, and KV2 caches, remain difficult. Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) is effective at moderate precision but often collapses when several components are compressed simultaneously (Dettmers et al., 2022; Xiao et al., 2023; Frantar et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024; Shao et al., 2024; Egiazarian et al., 2024; Malinovskii et al., 2024). Quantization-Aware Training (QAT) can recover quality by adapting the model to the quantized computation (Liu et al., 2024a, 2025b; Chen et al., 2025; Li et al., 2026; Yi et al., 2026), but the result still depends on the discrete representation to which the model is trained. 1arXiv:2610.09202v1 [cs.AI] 6 Oct 2026 At such low precision, that representation is not a minor implementation choice. Uniform quantization uses equally spaced reconstruction levels, even though weights, activations, and KV states have different distributional shapes. A data-fitted codebook can accommodate those differences, but ties the representation to the tensors or calibration data used to construct it. Joint compression makes the choice harder: weights shape the activation distribution, and quantized activations generate the keys and values that must themselves survive a two-bit cache. This motivates a common quantization interface that supports adaptation across the coupled computation. We ask: can a small quantization interface be designed once, then reused while an LLM learns to operate through it? Our approach separates two decisions. Source-specific transforms and normalization determine the coordinates presented to the quantizer; a source-independent reference determines the reconstruction alphabet. QAT then adapts the network to the resulting fixed codec. We introduce CanonQ around this principle: canonicalize the source, then train the model to use the codec. A fixed source-specific Hadamard– diagonal–permutation (HDP) transform mixes each block, and energy normalization removes its magnitude. The codebooks and HDP maps stay fixed throughout QAT. CanonQ-W applies this interface to weights;  

> CanonQ-Omni

applies it to the complete W/A/K/V computation. Our central finding is that the benefit of canonical quantization becomes most pronounced when weights, activations, and KV caches are compressed together. Our contributions are: (1) A unified framework for joint W/A/KV quantization. We introduce CanonQ , which unifies scalar and vector quantization of weights, activations, and KV caches through a common canonical interface. Frozen reference codebooks are reusable across layers and models, while joint QAT adapts the network to interacting quantization errors across the full computation. (2) A unified theory linking source alignment to optimization. We establish a quantitative chain from source mismatch to codebook distortion, local task loss, and optimization error. Wasserstein bounds characterize when frozen reference codebooks transfer to actual tensor distributions, while an exact normalization-aware straight-through estimator (STE) Jacobian identity reveals how assignment distortion controls backward sensitivity and gradient bias. We further derive distortion-dependent stationarity guarantees for a smooth reference objective, providing a theoretical foundation for the canonical training interface. (3) Large quality gains under extreme joint compression. At 

W2A4KV2 , CanonQ-Omni achieves up to 14 .28 × lower WikiText-2 perplexity and up to 57 .9% higher mean zero-shot accuracy than strong quantization baselines across LLaMA3-1B/3B/8B . Further evaluations demonstrate transfer to Qwen3-1.7B , substantial coding and GSM8K improvements under W2A16KV16 ,and stable relative perplexity within the 8K continued-QAT training range. 

# 2 Related Work  

> Low-bit optimization and codebooks.

PTQ methods including GPTQ , AWQ , and OmniQuant improve low-bit reconstruction (Frantar et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024; Shao et al., 2024); LLM-QAT , EfficientQAT , ParetoQ ,

> WinQ

, and JacQuant adapt models through quantized training (Liu et al., 2024a; Chen et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025b; Li et al., 2026; Yi et al., 2026). Binary and ternary methods likewise make representation a first-order design choice (Wang et al., 2023; Ma et al., 2024; Shang et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2024).  

> Lloyd–Max

and Linde–Buzo–Gray provide classical scalar and vector constructions (Max, 1960; Lloyd, 1982; Linde et al., 1980); modern non-uniform, additive, and lattice approaches include NF4 , SqueezeLLM , AQLM ,

> PV-Tuning

, and QuIP# (Dettmers et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024; Egiazarian et al., 2024; Malinovskii et al., 2024; Tseng et al., 2024). LC-QAT is a close weight-only QAT comparison: it represents two-bit quantized weights through a learned affine map over discrete vectors and avoids explicit codebook lookup in the training forward pass (Wang et al., 2026). CanonQ instead freezes the reference table, retains nearest-codeword assignment, and trains all enabled W/A/K/V paths.  

> Rotations, canonicalization, and caches.

Randomized rotations support distributed compression and LLM incoherence processing (Vargaftik et al., 2021, 2022; Chee et al., 2023; Tseng et al., 2024). QuaRot and

exploit cache-specific structure (Liu et al., 2024b; Hooper et al., 2024). TurboQuant separates norm from direction and constructs scalar quantizers for rotation-induced marginals; its inner-product variant additionally quantizes a residual with QJL (Zandieh et al., 2025). This is the closest source-modeling connection. We study 2task-aware adaptation to a fixed canonical codec, allow joint tuple assignment, and quantify the implemented HDP map’s mismatch from its design reference. CanonQ targets the more aggressive W1/W2-A4/A8-KV2 regime through a reusable canonical interface and joint task-aware adaptation.

# 4 Theory: From Source Alignment to Optimization 

The fixed interface raises three questions: when do reference codebooks transfer, how do quantization residuals affect local task loss, and how does distortion influence training?

> Connection to evidence.

The analysis separates source mismatch, sensitivity-weighted reconstruction error, and assignment distortion. Scalar W1/KS diagnostics assess marginal alignment; zero-update/QAT comparisons test adaptation to the fixed codec; precision and dimension sweeps establish how these choices affect model quality. 

# 5 Experiments 

The evaluation first locates the benefit across compression settings, then examines source alignment and adaptation, and finally tests longer-context likelihood, code generation, and mathematical reasoning. 

5.1 Evaluation Setup 

We evaluate LLaMA3-1B/3B/8B and Qwen3-1.7B (Dubey et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2025), using 1B for the broad component study. Standard QAT uses FineWeb-Edu , sequence length 2048 , effective batch size 64 ,

AdamW , and up to 240K updates ( ≈ 31 .5B tokens). Learning rates are selected from {1, 2, 4} × 10 −5 on held-out validation data. The 8B CanonQ-Omni configuration reuses the selected 3B setting. The context study separately continues QAT at 8192 for 40K steps. We report WikiText-2 perplexity (PPL) (Merity et al., 2017) and the unweighted mean zero-shot accuracy over eight benchmarks. Accuracy is reported in percent; task definitions and evaluation details appear in Sec. A.1. The instruction-tuned extension reports greedy coding pass@1 and GSM8K final-answer exact match under a separate protocol (Sec. A.5) (Austin et al., 2021; Chen et al., 2021; Cobbe et al., 2021). 

ParetoQ denotes its weight quantizer combined with the uniform A/KV paths specified in Sec. A.2. Uniform 

A4/KV2 uses 15/3 active levels; canonical scalar tables use 16/4 at the same index rate. Comparisons evaluate complete trained configurations, including these differences. Full settings and per-task results appear in Secs. A and B.3. Each reported configuration uses one training seed.

Weight-only quantization gives modest differences. At 1B W2A16 , ParetoQ and CanonQ-W-d2 achieve similar quality: 12.66/55.53 and 12.86/55.45 (PPL/accuracy), respectively (Tab. 2). At W1A16 , CanonQ-W-d1

Activation compression produces the larger separation. At W2A8 , CanonQ-Omni-d1 improves ParetoQ ’s 18.22/51.09 to 13.25/55.60. At W2A4 , weight-only canonicalization remains near 35 PPL and 44% accuracy, whereas CanonQ-Omni-d1 reaches 13.84/54.71: 2 .35 × lower PPL and a 10.59-point gain over ParetoQ . 5.4 Canonicalization and QAT Play Complementary Roles 

We next examine the complementary roles of source canonicalization and model adaptation through distribution diagnostics, zero-update comparisons, and cache-path ablations. 

HDP aligns dynamic-source marginals. Layer-mean W1 distances to the Gaussian reference fall from 0.101 to 0.010 for activations, 0.060 to 0.026 for keys, and 0.030 to 0.018 for values; weights change from 0.015 to 0.017 (Fig. 2). The largest distributional change is in activations, which also show the strongest separation between weight-only and full-stack canonicalization at A4 . Keys and values move closer to the same reference, while weights need little marginal correction. These marginal measurements support the use of a shared reference construction for heterogeneous W/A/K/V sources. 

The fixed codec benefits from task-aware adaptation. At 1B W2A4 , applying the scalar canonical W/A codec with zero updates gives 7043.43/38.83, versus 13.84/54.71 after QAT (Tab. 7). PPL falls approximately 509 ×

while the table stays fixed. The codec supplies reconstruction choices, but the pretrained network must still adapt to using them throughout the computation. Marginal alignment and task-aware training thus address different parts of the problem. The KV2 zero-update comparison also changes the cache path and measures their combined effect. 

The cache path also matters. The uniform-KV CanonQ-Omni-d1 ablation reaches 22.82/47.72 at W2A8KV2 and 21.65/48.30 at W2A4KV2 , compared with 15.70/53.45 and 15.42/53.56 for canonical KV. The corresponding accuracy gains are 5.73 and 5.26 points (Tab. 8), so the cache contribution persists at both activation precisions. At 1B , moving from W2A4 to W2A4KV2 costs the complete canonical path 1.58 PPL and 1.15 accuracy points, compared with 58.00 PPL and 3.65 points for ParetoQ . The ablation evaluates the complete canonical cache path, combining transformation, active levels, normalization, and backward behavior.

# 6 Discussion and Limitations  

> A shared interface for coupled errors.

CanonQ separates reusable reconstruction choices from task-aware adaptation. Canonicalization makes a fixed reference alphabet useful across heterogeneous sources, while joint QAT adapts the network to their interacting errors. The larger gains under joint compression, together with the alignment and zero-update studies, support this division of roles. These results favor selecting the codec under the complete target precision configuration: methods with similar weight-only quality separate sharply when activations and caches are also quantized. Evaluating the intended W/A/KV format therefore captures interactions that weight-only comparisons leave untested.  

> Choosing scalar or vector assignment.

The interface supports both choices at a fixed index rate. Scalar tables give the strongest reported 1B W2A4KV2 result; vector assignment improves 3B accuracy with a small PPL tradeoff. Dimension is therefore a practical choice guided by validation quality and lookup cost. Joint tuple statistics and sensitivity-weighted residuals offer concrete quantities for explaining these differences. 

Deployment and further evaluation. The present study establishes model quality at extreme low bit rates. Deployment evaluation will complement these results with exported-model size, KV-storage, and prefill/decode throughput measurements for fused kernels, accounting for group metadata and unquantized modules (Sec. A.4). Evaluation beyond the 8K training range, reasoning benchmarks with independent hyperparameter selection, and repeated training seeds are directions for extending this evidence. 

# 7 Conclusion 

We introduced CanonQ , a unified framework for weight, activation, and KV-cache quantization using canonical coordinates and frozen reference codebooks. Our theory links source mismatch to reconstruction, task loss, and normalization-aware STE bias. Experiments establish substantial quality gains under W2A4KV2 across model scales, with further improvements in coding and reasoning and stable relative perplexity within the 8K 

training range.

# A Experimental Details 

A.1 Models, Training, and Evaluation 

We use pretrained LLaMA-3.2-1B/3B , LLaMA3-8B , and Qwen3-1.7B checkpoints with their native tokenizers (Dubey et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2025). The 1B model supports the broad precision and component study; the larger models test transfer of the same frozen-table construction. Standard QAT starts from the corresponding full-precision checkpoint and samples a continuous FineWeb-Edu stream. The architecture, vocabulary, and positional encoding are unchanged. Training excludes the evaluation datasets. 

Optimization. We train on eight H100/A100-80G GPUs, with eight sequences per GPU. The standard recipe uses 2048 -token sequences, effective batch size 64 , and at most 240K updates, or approximately 31.5B 

tokens. We use AdamW with β = (0 .9, 0.999), ϵ = 10 −8, zero weight decay, and global gradient-norm clipping at 1 .0. A cosine schedule follows 1000 linear-warmup steps and decays to zero. Learning rates are selected from {1, 2, 4} × 10 −5 on held-out validation data; enabled scale and gain parameters share the weights’ learning rate. For the reported 8B CanonQ-Omni configuration, we reuse the best training hyperparameters selected at 

3B to reduce tuning costs. The training seed is 42 . The 8K/40K continuation and instruction-tuned coding and reasoning extension use the separate protocols in Secs. A.5 and C. 

Language modeling and downstream tasks. For WikiText-2 , we concatenate tokenized test documents without added separators, split the stream into non-overlapping 2048 -token windows, and drop the trailing partial window. PPL is the exponential of mean next-token negative log-likelihood over the scored tokens. All methods use the same tokenized corpus and evaluation implementation. Zero-shot accuracy is the unweighted mean over ARC-Easy and ARC-Challenge (Clark et al., 2018), BoolQ (Clark et al., 2019), PIQA (Bisk et al., 2020), SIQA (Sap et al., 2019), HellaSwag (Zellers et al., 2019), OpenBookQA (Mihaylov et al., 2018), and WinoGrande (Sakaguchi et al., 2020), using common prompts and answer normalization across methods. Evaluation re-quantizes shadow weights and keeps the trained activation/cache operators enabled. Each result represents one training seed unless stated otherwise.

# C Context-Length Robustness after 8K Continued QAT 

Protocol. All context-length results use LLaMA3-1B W2A4KV2 , continued QAT with 8192 -token sequences for 40K updates, and evaluation at 2048/4096/8192 tokens. The FP reference is the base checkpoint evaluated at each length. Table 14 reports PPL and the same-length ratio rM (L) = PPL M (L)/ PPL FP (L). 

Relative quality remains stable across the tested lengths. CanonQ-Omni improves from 16.86 to 15.55 to 14.86 PPL as the evaluation window grows. Its reductions relative to ParetoQ are 2 .99 ×, 3 .00 ×, and 3 .07 ×; at 

8K , it also improves over CanonQ-W by 2 .66 ×. FP improves from 9.71 to 9.21 to 8.74, leaving CanonQ-Omni 

at 1 .69–1 .74 × FP across lengths, versus 4 .37–4 .52 × for CanonQ-W and 5 .07–5 .22 × for ParetoQ . The ordering is consistent at all three lengths. 

Interpretation. All evaluation windows lie within the 8K continued-QAT training range. The experiment measures language-modeling quality across these windows. Quantized models receive continued QAT, while the FP checkpoint serves as a fixed same-length reference; the resulting ratios combine training and quantization effects. Longer windows provide additional preceding tokens and can change the scored-token set. We therefore assess context-length robustness through the consistent method ordering and approximately stable same-length FP ratio.
