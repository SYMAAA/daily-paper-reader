## ABSTRACT 

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) enable powerful multi-image synthesis but incur substantial computation from long visual contexts. While token pruning offers a direct route to accelerating inference, existing methods are primarily adapted from understanding tasks, suffering from insufficient reference image ex-ploration, confused conditional guidance, and pooling-induced ambiguity that de-grades generation quality. To address these issues, we propose CATP, the first training-free context-compression method tailored to UMM generation, which 

Calibrates the discrepancies of general Attention-based Token Pruning for better quality-efficiency trade-off. Concretely, building on independent layer-wise prun-ing during denoising, we introduce Guidance-Consistent Pruning (GCP) to syn-chronize pruning decisions across conditioning branches, and a Taylor-Calibrated Attention Metric (TCAM) that derives a theoretically grounded second-order cor-rection for pooling-induced bias. In addition, a Fast Scoring Operator (FSO) fuses the calibration into tiled online Softmax to reduce pruning cost. Extensive ex-periments across representative UMMs and benchmarks demonstrate that CATP consistently outperforms state-of-the-art token-reduction methods, achieving up to 2.09 × attention-core speedup. Code will be released upon paper acceptance. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs), which integrate understanding and generation within a shared backbone (Deng et al., 2025; Diao et al., 2026), have emerged as a prevalent paradigm for enhancing both capabilities (Zhao et al., 2025). Leveraging their strength in handling complex inputs, multi-image synthesis, which generates a target image from multiple references (Chen et al., 2026; Wei et al., 2026), has recently attracted growing interest in broad applications including fashion design (Choi et al., 2024), personalized content production (Wang et al., 2025), etc. However, this typically requires concatenating visual tokens from multiple high-resolution images, yielding long sequences that incur substantial attention computation during denoising (Chen et al., 2026; He et al., 2025). In general, such long-context designs are originally intended to support diverse capabilities of foun-dation models (Deng et al., 2025), whereas a specific task may exploit only a subset of the available information (Zhang et al., 2025b). Therefore, reducing the effective sequence length is a direct route to improving inference efficiency (Bolya et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2024b). Among strategies, attention-based token pruning offers an effective and general baseline, leveraging native attention score to discard less informative tokens (Yao et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2026b). Nevertheless, existing schemes are primarily developed for understanding-oriented models (Chen et al., 2024b; Wu et al., 2026a), while the few direct adaptations to UMMs overlook generation-specific characteristics (Ke et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2026), potentially discarding visual regions critical to faithful synthesis. Specifically, we identify three key limitations: 1) Static pruning across timesteps and layers. 

Understanding tasks usually produce short responses (Liu et al., 2024), with most information ag-gregated into a few tokens at early layers (Wu et al., 2026a), making one-shot pruning throughout inference feasible (Chen et al., 2024b). In contrast, visual generation attends to complementary regions across denoising timesteps and layers, as in Fig. 1(a). Especially under aggressive token budgets, a fixed pruning fails to cover the full reference content, yielding insufficient exploration of visual cues. 2) Inconsistent compression across conditioning branches. Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) (Ho & Salimans, 2021), natively instantiated in UMMs (Deng et al., 2025), performs mul-tiple forward passes with different input sequences, naturally producing branch-specific attention

Ambiguous token selection under pooled query representations. Adapting pruning to varying context exploration requires frequent importance estimation, while exact attention-based metric with the still-lengthy denoising queries incurs substantial overhead, undermining the goal of acceleration. Average pooling on queries is commonly adopted to reduce this cost (Zhang et al., 2025a). However, as observed in Fig. 1(c), it smooths fine-grained attention responses to critical regions, obscuring their distinction from less relevant content and thus yielding less reliable context retention. To address these issues, we propose CATP, a novel training-free framework for efficient multi-image synthesis in UMMs. Starting from the general attention-based token pruning, CATP identifies generation-specific discrepancies and calibrates them at both conditioning-branch and importance-estimation levels, thereby improving generation fidelity. Specifically, CATP prunes reference-image tokens in the KV cache across all layers at predefined timesteps, adapting compression to varying reference explorations. Building on this, we introduce Guidance-Consistent Pruning (GCP), which formulates the cross-branch mismatch of independent compression and synchronizes pruning deci-sions with the full-condition branch to preserve clean guidance. A second-order Taylor expansion then characterizes the bias of attention metric with pooled-query, leading to a Taylor-Calibrated At-tention Metric (TCAM) for more reliable identification of critical reference tokens. Additionally, a Fast Scoring Operator (FSO) is developed with a custom Triton kernel. It integrates the calibration into tiled online Softmax computation, substantially reducing the scoring overhead. In summary, our main contributions are three-fold: 1) We analyze discrepancies in general attention-based token pruning for UMM multi-image synthe-sis and propose a training-free acceleration framework, dubbed CATP. To the best of our knowledge, CATP makes the first investigation of token pruning specifically tailored to UMM generation. 2) We develop Guidance-Consistent Pruning (GCP), Taylor-Calibrated Attention Metric (TCAM) and Fast Scoring Operator (FSO), which address the rigidity, inconsistency, and ambiguity of general schemes, jointly enabling a flexible, accurate and efficient online context compression. 3) We conduct extensive experiments on representative UMMs and benchmarks, showing that CATP delivers superior generation quality, with up to 2.09 × attention-core acceleration. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

2.1 UNIFIED MULTIMODAL MODELS AND MULTI -I MAGE SYNTHESIS 

Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) enable understanding and generation within a single architec-ture (Zhao et al., 2025). Recent prevalent paradigm adopts a Large Language Model (LLM) as the backbone (Zhao et al., 2025), where text generation remains autoregressive process, while images are synthesized via iterative flow matching in latent (Deng et al., 2025) or pixel space (Diao et al., 2026). For image generation, conditioning inputs are encoded and concatenated into a token se-quence, cached as fixed KV states, and repeatedly attended to by generated visual tokens throughout denoising (Deng et al., 2025; Cao et al., 2025). Multi-image synthesis leverages UMMs’ strengths 2Under review in complex instruction understanding and high-quality generation (Wei et al., 2026; Oshima et al., 2026). However, multiple reference images substantially lengthen the input sequence, underscoring the practical necessity of efficient inference, especially in attention computation (Chen et al., 2026). 2.2 EFFICIENT INFERENCE VIA TOKEN REDUCTION 

Token Pruning. Token pruning has presented clear efficiency gains for long-context tasks (Yao et al., 2026), exploiting the task-irrelevant redundancy inherent in input sequences (Wu et al., 2026a). Initially explored in understanding tasks, methods develop various criteria for token selection (Yang et al., 2025; Alvar et al., 2025). Among them, attention-based pruning offers a strong baseline across LLMs (Wu et al., 2026a), Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) (Chen et al., 2024b), and the understanding branch of UMM (Ke et al., 2026), accelerating either prefilling through early token removal (Chen et al., 2024b) or decoding via KV cache pruning (Wan et al., 2024). 

Token Reduction for Visual Generation. Efficiency in visual generation has been studied from multiple perspectives, such as timestep reduction (Fan et al., 2026) and cross-step cache reusing (Liu et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2026), while token reduction is orthogonal to them. Most methods orig-inally target Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) (Peebles & Xie, 2023), typically reducing the com-puted sequence by merging and subsequently unmerging denoising tokens (Bolya & Hoffman, 2023; Fang et al., 2025). ToPi (Lin et al., 2026) more closely follows the aforementioned token pruning paradigm, but shares pruning decisions across layers, overlooking layer-wise differences in informa-tion demand. The few attempts on UMMs largely transfer understanding-oriented techniques (Chen et al., 2026), neglecting generation-specific characteristics. In this work, we systematically analyze these distinctive properties and develop token pruning specifically tailored to UMM generation.

## 4 EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS AND ANALYSIS 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETTINGS 

Models. Following recent advances in UMMs, we evaluate methods on two representative models: Bagel (Deng et al., 2025) and SenseNova-U1 (Diao et al., 2026), with generation performed in latent and pixel spaces, respectively. For Bagel, we use the multi-image fine-tuned checkpoint from (Chen et al., 2026), while SenseNova-U1 adopts its official checkpoint with native multi-image support. 

Datasets and Evaluation Metrics. We conduct experiments on three widely used multi-image syn-thesis benchmarks, following their official splits and metric aggregation rules. Concretely, we report the geometric mean of Image Consistency and Prompt Following scores on the MacroBench (Chen et al., 2026) Customization subset across four reference-number groups (1–3, 4–5, 6–7, and 8–10), the Weighted-Ref-VIEScore for Object-Centric, Human-Centric, Human-Object Interaction (HOI), and Decomposition-and-Recomposition (De&Re) tasks on MICo-Bench (Wei et al., 2026), and the geometric mean of Prompt Following and Subject Consistency scores for the Single/Multiple/Scene categories of OmniContext (Wu et al., 2025a), each further divided into Character (Char.)/Object (Obj.)/Char.+Obj. subcategories. We also include the average score (Avg.) for MacroBench and OmniContext, and the Overall score for MICo-Bench. For metrics involving MLLM-based evalua-tion, we utilize the open-source Qwen3-VL-32B-Instruct (Bai et al., 2025a) as the evaluator.

## 5 CONCLUSION 

In this work, we conduct a pioneering investigation of visual token pruning for efficient UMM multi-image synthesis, and develop a novel training-free method, namely CATP. By performing layer-wise pruning on KV cache tokens, CATP enables adaptive utilization of different reference information. Furthermore, we explicitly preserve clean conditional guidance through Guidance-Consistent Prun-ing (GCP) and improve the discriminability of critical regions with a theoretically derived Taylor-Calibrated Attention Metric (TCAM), together with a Fast Scoring Operator (FSO) that reduces the scoring cost.
