# FedFit: Federated Fine-Tuning of LLMs via Vector-Bank Parameterization and Quantization 

Hang Zou, Chao Zhang, Yuzhi Yang, Yu Tian, Samson Lasaulce, and M´ erouane Debbah 

Abstract —Federated Learning (FL) enables privacy-preserving fine-tuning of Large Language Models (LLMs), yet the massive communication overhead remains a critical bottleneck. Further-more, applying Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) in FL faces a fundamental “aggregation dilemma” between the accurate Sum-of-Products (SoP) and the communication-efficient Product-of-Sums (PoS) implementations. To tackle these challenges, we propose FedFit. First, to significantly reduce communication overhead, we introduce a disjoint shared vector-bank parame-terization that reconstructs high-dimensional adapter matrices from two compact and disjoint global vector banks. Second, to address the aggregation dilemma, we devise an alternating optimization schedule . By cycling between decoupled single-bank updates (which allow for accurate aggregation) and joint updates corrected by a Residual Spectral Aggregation mechanism, we resolve the conflict between SoP and PoS. Additionally, we integrate blockwise quantization with client-side error feedback to further compress the transmitted vectors. Furthermore, we establish theoretical convergence guarantees for the proposed algorithm. Extensive experiments on Qwen2.5 models demon-strate that FedFit achieves perplexity performance comparable to standard federated LoRA methods, while providing compression ratios up to 100 × higher. 

Index Terms —Large language models, distributed learning, federated learning, low-rank adaptation, fine-tuning, model quan-tization. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

A. Motivation 

Large language models (LLMs) have achieved remarkable success across a wide range of natural language processing tasks [1], and fine-tuning LLMs for downstream tasks has become standard practice across many application domains. However, in privacy- or security-sensitive areas such as health-care and finance, centralized fine-tuning is often infeasible because it requires uploading raw user data to a central server [2]. In addition, in remote Internet-of-Things (IoT) or mobile scenarios, transmitting local data to the cloud and then broadcasting updated models back to devices can consume excessive wireless resources, which is particularly problematic in power- or bandwidth-limited environments [3–5]. Federated learning (FL) addresses these issues by training models col-laboratively across distributed clients using their local data, while only communicating local model updates to a central

Applying FL to LLM fine-tuning, often referred to as FedLLM, has attracted increasing attention [7–12], yet the extremely large model size of LLMs makes full-parameter FedLLM prohibitively expensive in both computation and communication [13]. Low-rank adaptation (LoRA), which consists in perturbing the weight matrix by a low-rank product, is a representa-tive parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) method that can substantially reduce local computation, storage, and commu-nication costs when fine-tuning LLMs [14, 15]. Instead of updating the full weight matrix of each layer, LoRA introduces a low-rank adapter of the form ∆W = BA ⊤, where A and 

B are two low-rank matrices [15]. While this design works well in centralized fine-tuning, directly deploying LoRA in FedLLM raises a fundamental aggregation dilemma [16–20]. In FedLLM, client k learns its own adapters (Ak, B k) and uploads them to the server. The server must then combine these client-side updates into a single global adapter. If the server averages the products ∆Wk = BkA⊤

across clients (Sum-of-Product, SoP), the resulting update generally loses the exact low-rank structure and cannot be represented by a single pair of low-rank matrices, thereby increasing the downlink commu-nication cost. Alternatively, if the server first averages Ak and 

Bk separately and then multiplies the two averaged matrices (Product-of-Sum, PoS), the LoRA format is preserved, but the aggregation becomes inaccurate because all cross-client cross terms are discarded. Recent works observed that these two aggregation rules can lead to different performance in LoRA-based FL [16–18, 20–22], but a systematic investigation or theoretical analysis of the SoP vs PoS discrepancy for LoRA-based FedLLM, especially under vector-bank parameterization and aggressive quantization are missing. Although LoRA significantly reduces the computation and communication cost of FedLLM relative to full-parameter fine-tuning, LoRA adapters are still highly redundant across layers and clients [23–25]. Recent studies show that LoRA weights can be represented in a shared latent vector space with sparse indexing and low-rank structure, enabling further compres-sion [24, 25]. However, these vector-bank LoRA techniques have so far been studied only in centralized or single-client settings. To the best of our knowledge, there is currently no work that applies such vector-bank LoRA representations to a decentralized setting such as FedLLM. Communication efficiency remains a critical bottleneck in realistic federated systems [3, 26, 27]. While LoRA already reduces the number of parameters to be transmitted, repeatedly communicating dense adapter matrices or large latent banks can still dominate the cost in wireless or edge environments [5, 19, 20]. Quantization has also been widely adopted in FL to arXiv:2610.01537v1 [cs.LG] 1 Oct 2026 2

alleviate communication overhead [26–29], and some recent methods have started to explore quantized LoRA in federated settings [21, 22]. Nevertheless, there is still no work that investigates quantized vector-bank LoRA in FedLLM, where both the latent bank structure and the SoP/PoS aggregation trade-off must be handled jointly. To make the bandwidth pressure concrete: even after LoRA has reduced the trainable parameter count by two orders of magnitude relative to full fine-tuning, a single per-round upload of a ∼15 MB adapter still takes roughly 24 seconds over a 5 Mbps cellular uplink, and proportionally saturates the link in cross-silo deployments with hundreds or thousands of participating clients. Communi-cation, not the local compute imposed by quantized weights, therefore remains the binding constraint in the wireless and cross-organization FedLLM regimes targeted by this work, motivating further compression on top of LoRA. These observations motivate the work in this paper: we design a FedLLM framework that (i) resolves the SoP–PoS aggregation dilemma, (ii) shares vector-bank parameterization across layers and clients, and (iii) supports aggressive quanti-zation for ultra-low communication cost. 

B. Related Works 

LLMs and Federated Learning: Transformers and LLMs have become the dominant backbone for modern AI systems [1]. FL provides a standard paradigm for privacy-preserving distributed training, enabling multiple clients to collaboratively train a global model without sharing raw data [2, 3, 6]. Recent surveys and position papers further discuss the opportuni-ties and challenges of combining FL with LLMs, including system heterogeneity, communication bottlenecks and privacy requirements [4, 7, 9, 30]. A rapidly growing body of work studies federated fine-tuning of foundation models and LLMs (often called FedLLMs), such as wireless-network oriented fine-tuning of foundation models [5], federated instruction tuning [11], mobile-device FwdLLM [12], and full-parameter tuning under tight communication constraints [13]. 

Parameter-Efficient and Federated LoRA for LLMs: 

PEFT methods, such as adapter modules and LoRA, greatly reduce the number of trainable parameters needed to adapt LLMs [14, 15]. Many LoRA variants further improve effi-ciency, expressiveness or robustness, including orthonormal LoRA [23], vector-bank [24] and Uni-LoRA [25], and FFA-LoRA [31] for privacy-preserving FL. In the federated setting, several works integrate LoRA into FL for resource-constrained and heterogeneous clients. Flex-LoRA [16] proposes a flexible aggregation scheme for clients with heterogeneous computa-tion and data, while FLoRA [17] stacks heterogeneous low-rank adapters to improve aggregation robustness. FedIT [11] and FwdLLM [12] focus on instruction tuning and billion-parameter models in realistic systems. More recently, FedTT [19] and EcoLoRA [20] introduce tensorized and energy-aware federated fine-tuning of LLMs. A unified convergence analysis of aggregation–broadcast operators for LoRA-based FL is given in [32], including a comparison of Sum-of-Products (SoP) and Product-of-Sums (PoS) aggregation. How-ever, [32] does not consider shared vector-bank parameteri-zation or communication-constrained FedLLM settings with quantized updates. Another line of work studies exact or quantization-aware aggregation for LoRA in FL, including FedEx-LoRA [18], Quantized LoRA [21], and FedQLoRA [22]. Our method falls into this FedLoRA family, but targets extremely communication-constrained settings and couples LoRA with quantization based encoding of local adapters. A closely related line mitigates the SoP–PoS bias by al-ternating optimization or server-side correction of the LoRA factors. RoLoRA [33] alternates the adapters across rounds, freezing a globally synchronized factor in each round so that averaging the other factor is exact at the product level, with convergence guarantees. LoRA-FAIR [34] introduces a closed-form server-side residual correction for both aggregation bias and initialization drift. LA-LoRA [35] alternates A and B

at every local step and applies a low-pass Gaussian filter to gradients. These methods operate on raw LoRA adapters. 

Communication-efficient and Quantized Federated Learning: Communication-efficient FL has been widely ex-plored via gradient quantization, sparsification and error-compensation. Representative methods include QSGD [26], FedPAQ [27], TernGrad [36], signSGD [37] and its majority-vote [38] and error-feedback variants [28], as well as a recent analysis of error feedback with biased compression in FL [29]. Other communication-efficient schemes compress model updates using binary neural networks or (quantized) com-pressed sensing techniques [39–42]. Product quantization (PQ) [43] and optimized PQ [44] are powerful vector quantizers widely used in approximate nearest-neighbour search, and are natural tools for compressing high-dimensional model updates. Compared with these works, we focus on quantization and aggregation of LoRA adapters rather than full-precision gradients, and design a PQ-based encoding scheme tailored to federated LoRA for LLMs.

While the standard LoRA formulation in Section II sig-nificantly reduces computational demands compared to full fine-tuning, its deployment in wireless FL faces two critical bottlenecks. First, the communication overhead remains pro-hibitive for bandwidth-constrained edge networks. Second, the product structure of low-rank matrices induces an aggregation discrepancy under conventional averaging (see Section IV). To address these challenges, we propose FedFit (see Fig. 1), a communication-efficient framework comprising two integrated components: a disjoint shared vector-bank parameterization, and an alternating optimization schedule.

Inspired by recent LoRA variants such as Uni-LoRA [25], which show that adapter weights are highly redundant, we leverage the insight that sharing parameters across layers’ adapters can largely reduce computational and communication complexity while preserving performance. B. Alternating Optimization Strategy 

Directly optimizing both banks simultaneously induces a bilinear coupling in the weight updates. This coupling not only doubles the communication overhead but also leads to aggre-gation errors when averaging local updates. To mitigate these issues, we introduce a coordinate-descent training schedule governed by a phase indicator ϕt ∈ { A-only , B-only , A+B }.This approach allows us to selectively freeze parameters to achieve exact aggregation and reduced complexity, or update jointly to ensure convergence. At each round t ∈ { 1, . . . , T }, the server broadcasts the current global banks (vAt , vBt ) to the selected clients. The clients then perform local training updates restricted to the coordinates specified by ϕt:

IV. A GGREGATION APPROACHES 

The alternating optimization schedule introduced in Section III is not merely a heuristic for communication costs reduction and training stability, it is also structurally designed to resolve the fundamental conflict arising when aggregating distributed low-rank updates. While standard federated aggregation rules assume linearity in the parameter space, the low-rank factor-ization in LoRA introduces a bilinear coupling that breaks this assumption. In this section, we formalize this aggregation dilemma, then demonstrate how our decoupled single-bank phases achieve accurate aggregation, and finally present the residual spectral correction mechanism used to rectify aggre-gation errors during the joint update phases.

A. Blockwise Round-To-Nearest (RTN) quantizer 

By projecting the heavy adapter weight matrices into two disjoint vector banks, we can further quantize them before sending them. This is not merely a sequential pipeline of two independent compression stages: the vector-bank parameteri-zation is what makes aggressive low-bit quantization effective, following the classical transform-coding pattern of a decorre-lating transform followed by a scalar quantizer, e.g., in JPEG and H.264/HEVC. Raw LoRA adapters have heterogeneous per-layer statistics that a uniform blockwise quantizer handles poorly; the bank instead represents the entire transmitted state as a single 1-D vector whose seeded-projection entries are homogeneous and near-Gaussian, so block-wise RTN with one per-block scale is the natural quantizer. The bank parame-terization thus makes quantization cheap rather than lossy ,justifying the two mechanisms as a single co-design rather than an X+Y stack. Each element of the vector bank is reused across many adapter entries via the projection matrices P A, P B and can be viewed as a representative (or “cluster center”) for a group of similar values. This leads to a structural compression before any quantization is applied.

VIII. C ONCLUSIONS 

This work addresses the critical communication and ag-gregation bottlenecks in Federated LLM fine-tuning, offering three key takeaways for future edge intelligence systems. First, we demonstrate that the high dimensionality of LoRA adapters is largely redundant for LLMs; by using a disjoint shared vector-bank parameterization, we show that distinct client models can be effectively constructed from a shared, ultra-compact parameter space without sacrificing expressivity. Second, we resolve the fundamental “aggregation dilemma” (Sum-of-Products vs. Product-of-Sums) not only through ap-proximation, but also through a novel alternating optimization schedule. This finding suggests that sequential updating of low-rank factors is a viable strategy to achieve accurate ag-gregation in distributed settings. Finally, the success of FedFit proves that extreme compression (reducing communication costs to just 1–3% of standard methods) is achievable when structural parameter sharing is coupled with post-training quantization. These results constitute important first steps to contribute to deploy billion-parameter models over highly constrained wireless networks. Other issues not addressed in this paper would need to be investigated separately. These include a deeper analysis of the LLM model’s effect on the results, the study of scenarios with client heterogeneity and partial participation, accounting for transmission errors and delays, and the acquisition of new data.
