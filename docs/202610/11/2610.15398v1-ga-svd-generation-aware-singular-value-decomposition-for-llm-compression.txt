ABSTRACT 

Large language models (LLMs) demonstrate strong generalization across a broad range of reasoning and generation tasks, but their large parameter counts impose substantial memory and computation costs, hindering deployment in practical resource-constrained settings. One-shot low-rank compression is a promising solu-tion since it reduces model size and inference cost effectively. However, existing SVD-based methods primarily target multiple-choice benchmarks and often fail to preserve performance on generation tasks. Moreover, their rank allocation strate-gies are typically heuristic. We propose GA-SVD , a generation-aware low-rank compression framework for LLMs. First, we introduce Generation-Aware Weight Whitening (GAWW), which leverages gradients from on-policy knowledge dis-tillation to construct whitening transformations aligned with generative behavior. Second, we develop an evolutionary global rank allocation method that searches for effective layer-wise rank allocation under a given global compression ratio. Across extensive experiments on Mistral-7B, Qwen3 series, and Llama-3.3 models, eval-uated on both multiple-choice and generative benchmarks, GA-SVD consistently outperforms existing SVD-based baselines and achieves state-of-the-art (SoTA) results. We further demonstrate scalability by applying our method to models with up to 70B parameters. Code will be released. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Recently, large Language Models (LLMs) (Yang et al., 2025; Team, 2026; AI@Meta, 2024) can generate fluent, human-like content and generalize across a wide range of reasoning and generation tasks. However, the powerful performance comes from the large-scale model size, typically billions and even trillions of model parameters. The high computation and memory cost hinders the real-world application of LLMs, especially in memory-limited and latency-sensitive scenarios. A line of work explores one-shot methods for compressing LLMs, including numerical quantiza-tion (Frantar et al., 2022; Egiazarian et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2024), network pruning (Frantar & Alistarh, 2023; Kurti´ c et al., 2023; Tang et al., 2026) and weight low-rank compression Wang et al. (2025c;b). Among these techniques, low-rank compression typically applies singular value decomposition (SVD) to reduce model size and computational cost by truncating singular values and factorizing large weight matrices into products of smaller low-rank matrices. Unlike quantization and unstructured pruning, low-rank compression does not require custom inference kernels and can provide acceleration across standard hardware platforms. Moreover, low-rank compression is orthogonal to other compression techniques, such as quantization, and can therefore be combined with them for more aggressive model compression. A series of SVD-based compression methods obtain satisfactory performance while reducing the model computation cost. For example, SVD-LLM (Wang et al., 2025c) connects singular value with compression loss by input data whitening, which decomposes the input-whitened weights and truncates the weights with the identical ranks. SVD-LLM v2 (Wang et al., 2025b) further improves the performance by analyzing the sensitivity of each layer and allocating the different compression ratios for each weight. However, there are still critical limitations of existing methods. Moreover, current adaptive rank allocation methods (Li et al., 2025; Gao et al., 2024) are based on heuristics, which may be suboptimal under a global compression budget. To address the limitation of existing works, we propose GA-SVD , a generation-aware singular value decomposition framework, inspired by on-policy knowledge distillation (Agarwal et al., 2024). Technically, we introduce Generation-Aware Weight Whitening (GAWW), which guides the weight matrix whitening by the gradient of on-policy KD. The central idea is to make the whitening geometry reflect the model’s generative behavior, rather than relying only on offline calibration statistics. To achieve this, we estimate layerwise whitening statistics from trajectories generated by the compressed student model itself. Unlike prior input-whitened SVD methods, our method introduces an additional output-side sensitivity covariance, which measures how strongly perturbations in a layer output affect a generation-aware objective. These two statistics define a bi-sided compression metric, and under this metric the compressed layer is obtained by applying truncated SVD to a transformed weight matrix. Moreover, to obtain effective rank allocation under a global compression budget, we formulate rank selection as a search problem and introduce evolutionary global rank allocation via evolutionary search (Tang et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026). Given a global compression ratio, we randomly mutate rank allocations to generate a set of offspring candidates at each generation using predefined mutation steps. Based on the fitness evaluation, the best candidate is selected as the final rank allocation. Empirically, we validate our method on a wide range of models, including Mistral, Qwen, and Llama series models. Our compressed models are evaluated on both MCQ and generative benchmarks. Compared with existing methods, our method achieves the SoTA performance on both MCQ and generative benchmarks. Specifically, on Mistral-7B with 20% compression, GA-SVD achieves the best coding average, GSM8K accuracy, MCQ average, and perplexity among compressed models. Compared with SVD-LLM, GA-SVD outperforms all existing baseline methods on both MCQ and generative benchmarks. The gains remain consistent under more aggressive 40% compression, demonstrating strong robustness. Across Qwen3 models from 8B to 32B, GA-SVD provides substan-tial improvements, especially on generation-oriented benchmarks. Moreover, our method is highly scalable and we further scale our method on compressing larger models up to 70B parameters. We summarize our contributions as follows: • We propose GA-SVD , a generation-aware low-rank compression framework for LLMs. In particular, we introduce Generation-Aware Weight Whitening (GAWW), which incorporates on-policy knowledge distillation signals into the whitening process, enabling compressed models to better preserve generation capabilities. • We introduce an evolutionary global rank allocation method for low-rank compression. Instead of relying on heuristic layer-wise allocation rules, our method formulates rank allocation as a global search problem and discovers effective rank configurations under a target compression budget. • Extensive experiments across the Mistral-7B, Qwen3, and Llama-3.3 families, evaluated on both multiple-choice and generative benchmarks, demonstrate that GA-SVD achieves 2Preprint state-of-the-art performance among existing SVD-based LLM compression methods, while also scaling to models with up to 70B parameters. 

2 RELATED WORK 

Post-Training SVD-Based LLM Compression. Early work (Hsu et al., 2022) on language models introduced Fisher-weighted SVD (FWSVD), which weights singular value decomposition by pa-rameter importance to preserve task-relevant components and achieves higher accuracy than vanilla SVD at aggressive compression. A closely related method, Activation-aware SVD (ASVD) (Yuan et al., 2023), further adjusts weight matrices based on input activation statistics and layer sensitivity. ASVD applies a per-channel scaling to absorb activation outliers and then assigns different ranks per layer according to sensitivity. SVD-LLM (Wang et al., 2025c) identifies that naive SVD causes high perplexity and instead uses a truncation-aware whitening preprocessing and sequential low-rank updates. SVD-LLM V2 (Wang et al., 2025b) further optimizes singular value truncation by assigning heterogeneous compression ratios according to theoretical truncation loss. SAES-SVD (Hu et al., 2026) proposes Cumulative error-aware layer compression to jointly consider local reconstruction and accumulated error compensation, and adaptive collaborative error suppression to automatically adjust the compensation weight. AA-SVD (Sinha & Fleuret, 2026) compresses LLMs by anchoring each compressed layer to the original model outputs while adapting to input distribution shifts caused by upstream compression, These methods are attractive due to their simplicity and lack of retraining, but they often rely on uniform or heuristically determined ranks, which may be suboptimal. In practice, one must often tune ranks to avoid large performance drops. Moreover, the generation ability is ignored during compression. 

Rank Selection Methods. Rather than fixing ranks by rule, recent works (Ji et al., 2024) learn the optimal truncation. For example, Adaptive Rank Selection (Gao et al., 2024) treats truncation as a differentiable process. AdaSVD (Li et al., 2025) adaptively compensates truncation errors and assigns layer-specific compression ratios based on layer importance. D-Rank is proposed by (Mi et al., 2025), which is an SVD-based compression method that assigns different ranks to different layers according to their information density. LLRC (Sundrani et al., 2025), which uses a small calibration set and trainable binary masks over singular values to select ranks in a fine-tuning-free way. Similarly, Dobi-SVD (Wang et al., 2025a) formulates SVD truncation as a differentiable step with analytically optimal update rules, enabling gradient-based refinement of the decomposition. However, these works generally require extra training cost for rank selection. 

3 METHOD 

Overview. Our method starts from the student warm-start initialization, which is required by the on-policy distillation. After that, given a calibration dataset, we collect the input covariance and generation-aware sensitivity covariance, which are applied in the weight bi-whitening. We further utilize evolutionary search to find the optimal rank allocation setups. The overall pipeline can be found in Figure 2.

For token position t, we denote by xl,t the input activation of layer l, and by hl,t = W ⋆l xl,t the corresponding layer output. ri ← ri − δ, rj ← rj + δ, (11) where the updated ranks must remain within their admissible rank sets and δ refers to the mutation step. This mutation preserves the global budget approximately while enabling non-uniform redistribution of rank across weights.

4 EXPERIMENTS 

Model & Dataset & Baselines. We conduct our methods on a range of different LLMs. We compare our method with baselines on Mistral 7B (Jiang et al., 2023) and Qwen3 (Yang et al., 2025) series, including 8B, 14B and 32B models. To further show the scalability and validate our method on instruction-tuned model, we test our method on Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (AI@Meta, 2024) models. For the calibration dataset, we use wikitext (Merity et al., 2016) training set, Evol-codealpaca-v1 (Luo et al., 2023) and tulu-3-sft-personas-math (Lambert et al., 2024) to assess both general and domain knowledge calibration. We compare GA-SVD with various baselines including ASVD (Yuan et al., 2023), SVD-LLM (Wang et al., 2025c), SVD-LLM v2 (Wang et al., 2025b), Dobi-SVD (Wang et al., 2025a) and AA-SVD (Sinha & Fleuret, 2026). As the closest work, we compare with SVD-LLM on each following analysis experiment. We apply the official implementation of each method with the same calibration set.

For on-policy generation-aware whitening, we use 256 samples with the prompt length of 128 and rollout length 512. The temperature is 1.0. We compress the model with 0.2 and 0.4 compression ratio. For evolutionary rank allocation, we apply 32 samples for fitness computation. We utilize KL divergence as the fitness function. For each offspring generation, 16 candidates are generated with 10 times mutations randomly. The rank mutation step is 128 and the boundary window w is 32 for faster searching. We search the rank allocation setup for 100 generations. All experiments, including main results and analysis are conducted on a 4 ×H100 machine. 4.1 MAIN RESULTS

We provide the ablation study in Table 5. The results verify the contribution of each component in GA-SVD. Removing GAWW decreases performance on all generative benchmarks, indicating that generation-aware whitening helps preserve generation ability during compression. This suggests that relying only on input-side statistics is insufficient for generation-oriented tasks, since it does not capture how layer-output perturbations affect the model’s generated responses. Removing ERA causes a larger drop on coding tasks, reducing HumanEval from 20.7 to 16.7 and MBPP from 34.1 to 30.9, which shows the importance of global rank allocation beyond heuristic rank assignment. This also indicates that different weights and layers have heterogeneous compression sensitivity, and that automatically redistributing the rank budget is critical for preserving task performance under a fixed compression constraint. We also compare different objectives for gradient-based whitening. Both LM loss and prefix KD underperform the proposed on-policy generation-aware distillation signal, confirming that gradients collected from the student’s own generated trajectories provide more effective compression guidance for generation tasks. Compared with teacher-forced or next-token prediction objectives, the on-policy signal reflects the distribution encountered by the compressed model during autoregressive decoding, where early errors can influence subsequent tokens. 

5 CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we propose GA-SVD, a generation-aware low-rank compression framework for LLMs. GA-SVD introduces Generation-Aware Weight Whitening, which uses on-policy distillation gradients to align the compression geometry with generative behavior, and an evolutionary global rank alloca-tion strategy to search for effective layer-wise ranks under a fixed compression budget. Experiments across Mistral, Qwen3, and Llama-3.3 models show that GA-SVD consistently outperforms existing SVD-based baselines on both generative and multiple-choice benchmarks. The method also reduces perplexity, improves inference efficiency, and scales to large models up to 70B parameters. These results demonstrate that generation-aware low-rank compression is an effective and practical direction for preserving LLM capabilities under substantial compression. 9Preprint

A.2 LIMITATION 

Although GA-SVD achieves strong performance on both MCQ and generative tasks, several limitations remain. First, the actual inference speedup is lower than the nominal compression ratio. This is an inherent limitation of SVD-based compression, as each dense matrix is replaced by the product of two smaller matrices, introducing additional computation and memory access overhead. This limitation may be mitigated through system-level co-design and optimized kernels. Second, a noticeable generation performance gap still remains, particularly when compressing smaller models or under aggressive compression ratios. We leave further improving generation preservation and inference efficiency to future work. A.3 MORE EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS 

Results on CNN/DailyMail. To complement the structured generation benchmarks, we evaluate open-ended summarization using Mistral-7B on 200 samples from CNN/DailyMail. These results suggest that the benefits of generation-aware compression extend to summarization, helping preserve the content captured by ROUGE beyond tasks evaluated through code execution or exact-answer matching.

The Results on Qwen3-8B. Qwen3-8B exhibits pronounced sensitivity to low-rank compression, particularly on generation tasks. We provide results of different compression ratio on Qwen3-8B in Table 12. At 20% compression, the coding average drops from 78.4 to 0.0 with SVD-LLM and to 16.5 with GA-SVD, while GSM8K accuracy falls from 88.7 to 1.4 and 8.4, respectively. A possible explanation is that Qwen3-8B has less parameter redundancy than its larger counterparts, making the same relative rank reduction more disruptive to the capacity needed for autoregressive reasoning. The larger degradation on coding and math tasks than on MCQ benchmarks is consistent with this hypothesis, although it does not establish the underlying mechanism. Reducing compression to 10% substantially alleviates the degradation: coding averages recover to 20.8 and 35.3, and GSM8K accuracies to 20.3 and 28.2, for SVD-LLM and GA-SVD, respectively. These results show that GA-SVD mitigates, but does not eliminate, the sensitivity of Qwen3-8B to compression, highlighting the need to adapt compression ratios to model capacity and task requirements. A.4 MORE VISUALIZATION OF RANK ALLOCATION 

Figures 4 and 5 provide more detailed layer-wise rank allocation results for Mistral-7B and Qwen3. The learned ranks are highly non-uniform across both layers and weight types, further confirming that different parts of the transformer exhibit distinct sensitivity to low-rank compression. For example, MLP projections such as Down Proj, Gate Proj, and Up Proj generally receive much larger ranks than attention projections such as K Proj and V Proj, suggesting that MLP weights require more rank capacity to preserve model behavior under compression. The allocation patterns also differ across model families. Although both Mistral and Qwen3 show strong rank variation, the peaks and valleys occur at different layers and projection matrices, indicating that compression sensitivity is architecture-dependent. This supports the need for a model-specific global search strategy rather than using a fixed rank schedule shared across models. In addition, the sharp rank changes in some projections, such as Q Proj, K Proj, and V Proj, show that even weights within the same module can have substantially different redundancy.
