## ABSTRACT 

Structured pruning is typically formulated as identifying which model dimensions to remove, often within the model’s original representation. However, the effect of coordinate deletion fundamentally depends on the basis in which pruning is per-formed. This raises a more fundamental question: how should the basis be chosen specifically for coordinate deletion? We introduce WUSPruner , a transform-based framework for structured pruning of large language models. We formulate basis selection as a local linear reconstruction problem that directly minimizes the re-construction loss induced by pruning. Inspired by the second-moment transfor-mation in WUSH (Chen et al., 2026), we consider a WUS transform constructed jointly from activation and weight statistics. We show that under positive-definite second moments, WUS transform minimizes the local reconstruction loss over all invertible changes of basis. In the resulting basis, the activation and weight second moments are simultaneously diagonalized and balanced, causing the dele-tion error to decompose into independent spectral contributions and yielding a principled criterion for coordinate selection. Guided by this result, we develop a scalable pruning method based on blockwise WUS transformations and compact input projections. Experiments on Llama and Qwen models from 8B to 72B pa-rameters show consistent improvements over structured pruning baselines across language modeling, knowledge, commonsense reasoning, mathematics, and code generation benchmarks, while also delivering practical reductions in inference la-tency and memory usage. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Large language models achieve strong performance at substantial computational and memory cost, motivating compression techniques that reduce the cost of inference. Among them, structured prun-ing (Sun et al., 2024; Ashkboos et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2026; 2025) is particularly attractive because it removes entire dimensions or channels, yielding smaller dense operators that can translate into practical reductions in latency and memory usage regardless of the hardware specification. However, structured pruning is also inherently destructive: removing a representation dimension eliminates its contribution to the computation rather than merely zeroing individual parameters without constraint. As a result, even moderate pruning can substantially distort the outputs of the original model. The central challenge is therefore to remove model dimensions while preserving the performance of the original dense model as much as possible. In this work, we study this problem through the local reconstruction of linear mappings, where the intermediate dimension is reduced while minimizing the resulting output distortion. A key observation is that structured pruning is inherently basis-dependent. Many existing prun-ing heuristics operate directly in the model’s native representation, assigning importance scores to weights or dimensions and removing those deemed least important. Methods such as Wanda (Sun et al., 2024) perform pruning in the model’s original representation, whereas transform-based ap-proaches such as SliceGPT (Ashkboos et al., 2024) modify the basis before deletion. This highlights that pruning quality depends not only on coordinate importance, but also on the choice of represen-tation basis. However, the PCA-based transformation of SliceGPT is chosen according to activation statistics rather than the reconstruction error induced by pruning itself. This leaves a fundamental question open: how should the basis be chosen specifically for coordinate deletion? Our goal is 1arXiv:2610.15394v1 [cs.LG] 11 Oct 2026 Preprint therefore to identify a transformation that minimizes the reconstruction loss induced by coordinate deletion under a fixed pruning budget. In this work, we formalize this objective as a local linear reconstruction problem. Given calibration activations and the corresponding weight matrix, we optimize over invertible changes of basis to minimize the reconstruction loss after deleting a fixed number of transformed coordinates. We show that this objective admits a theoretically optimal solution given by a WUS transformation inspired by the second-moment construction of WUSH (Chen et al., 2026). The transform is constructed jointly from activation and weight statistics and is obtained by removing the Hadamard component used for quantization. Under positive-definite activation and weight second moments, WUS attains the minimum reconstruction loss over all invertible transformations. In the WUS basis, the transformed activation and weight second moments are simultaneously diagonalized and balanced, eliminating cross-coordinate interactions in the deletion error. As a result, the reconstruction loss decomposes into independent coordinate-wise contributions determined by a singular-value spectrum, providing a principled criterion for coordinate selection. This characterization further connects transformed coordinate deletion to optimal rank-constrained approximation. The theoretically optimal construction, however, is not directly suitable for large-scale Transformer pruning. Applying a full transformation to high-dimensional representations incurs substantial con-struction and storage costs, and practical deployment requires the transformed representation to in-tegrate efficiently with existing Transformer modules. To bridge this gap, we develop WUSPruner ,a scalable approximation to the unrestricted WUS construction. We partition each input space into blocks and compute the WUS transformation locally within each block, substantially reducing the cost of constructing the pruning basis. The retained transformed coordinates are then implemented through compact input projections followed by smaller dense linear maps. Together, these designs make the theoretically motivated basis construction practical for large-scale Transformer pruning while keeping the additional memory and computational overhead manageable. Empirically, WUSPruner consistently improves structured pruning quality across model families and scales. We evaluate models ranging from 8B to 72B parameters on language modeling, com-monsense reasoning, knowledge, mathematics, and code generation benchmarks. Across the evalu-ated models, WUSPruner achieves higher average downstream accuracy than the structured pruning baselines and consistently improves perplexity on PTB and C4. The gains persist when scaling from 8B models to Qwen3.8-27B and Qwen2.5-72B, indicating that the proposed basis construc-tion remains effective at larger model scales. Beyond pruning quality, WUSPruner also translates structural compression into practical efficiency gains, including both inference latency and memory consumption reduction. In summary, our main contributions are: • We formulate structured pruning as a basis-selection problem : instead of pruning coor-dinates only in the model’s original representation, we optimize the change of basis with respect to the reconstruction loss induced by coordinate deletion. • We provide a theoretical characterization of the optimal basis for this objective. Under positive-definite activation and weight second moments, we show that the WUS transfor-mation minimizes the local reconstruction loss over all invertible transformations. In this basis, the deletion error decomposes into independent spectral contributions, connecting coordinate pruning to optimal rank-constrained approximation. • Building on this result, we develop WUSPruner , a scalable structured pruning method based on blockwise WUS transformations and compact shared input projections. Experi-ments on Llama and Qwen models from 8B to 72B parameters show consistent improve-ments over structured pruning baselines across language modeling and downstream tasks, together with practical reductions in inference latency and memory usage.

Structured pruning instead removes entire coupled components to obtain hardware-friendly com-pression. LLM-Pruner (Ma et al., 2023) identifies dependent structures using gradient-based impor-tance and restores performance through low-rank adaptation, ZipLM (Kurti´ c et al., 2023) explicitly optimizes the trade-off between predictive loss and runtime on a target inference platform, and FLAP (An et al., 2024) combines activation fluctuation statistics with adaptive structure selection and bias compensation. A complementary line of work studies whether pruning becomes easier after changing the representation basis. SliceGPT (Ashkboos et al., 2024), for instance, exploits orthogo-nal invariances in Transformers to rotate hidden representations using activation-derived PCA bases and subsequently remove low-importance dimensions. Our proposed WUSPruner derives the basis from a different objective: minimizing the local output reconstruction error induced by coordinate deletion while jointly accounting for the second moments of both activations and weights. To trans-late this principle into a practical structured compressor, our WUSPruner employs local blockwise projections and reduced linear maps while retaining the original residual-stream dimensionality, and explicitly includes the projection operators in the compressed-model cost. 

Activation-aware low-rank compression. Low-rank compression (Hu et al., 2021; Dettmers et al., 2023; Chavan et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2024; Hayou et al., 2024; Tian et al., 2024) approximates a dense linear operator by the product of smaller matrices, reducing both parameter count and compu-tation. Classical SVD provides the optimal approximation under an unweighted Frobenius objective, whereas recent LLM compression methods incorporate activation statistics to better reflect the actual output distortion induced by compression. ASVD (Yuan et al., 2023) rescales weight matrices ac-cording to activation distributions before decomposition, mitigating the effect of activation outliers and adapting compression to layer sensitivity. SVD-LLM (Wang et al., 2025), for instance, intro-duces truncation-aware data whitening to connect singular-value truncation with calibration-set re-construction loss and further performs sequential updates of compressed weights. This viewpoint is closely related to our formulation. After an appropriate change of basis, retaining only r coordinates produces an operator of rank at most r. Under the unrestricted local formulation considered in this work, the optimal coordinate-deletion solution achieves the same minimum output reconstruction error as the best activation-weighted rank-r approximation. This yields a spectral characterization that connects structured coordinate pruning with activation-aware low-rank compression. Our WUS-Pruner translates the unrestricted theoretical construction into a practical blockwise representation with shared input projections and spectral coordinate selection.

The unrestricted construction above provides a theoretical solution to the local deletion problem, but applying a full transformation to high-dimensional Transformer representations is expensive. We therefore develop a scalable approximation based on local input projections, shared transformations, and blockwise WUS construction. 

Integration into attention and MLP modules. We replace each selected linear map with an input projection followed by a smaller dense linear map while preserving its output width. The projection is applied immediately before the linear map, allowing the compressed module to retain the original interfaces with normalization, nonlinearities, and residual connections. In particular, the residual-stream width and input embeddings remain unchanged. Each transformation is constructed from the activations entering its associated linear map and the corresponding weights. Maps operating on different intermediate representations use separate transformations, whereas maps sharing the same input can share both the basis and projected activations. The same construction can also be applied to the language-model output head while preserving the vocabulary dimension. 

Sharing projections across linear maps. For linear maps W (1) , . . . , W (J) sharing the same input, we construct a common basis from the concatenated weight matrix Wgroup = [ W (1) · · · W (J)].Its weight second moment is ΣW, group = PJj=1 W (j)W (j)⊤. The resulting reconstruction objec-tive is the sum of the individual map errors, allowing the same input projection to be shared across the group. We use this construction for the gate and up projections. Each map retains its own com-pressed weight, while the shared projection is stored and evaluated only once. This sharing amortizes the additional parameter cost introduced by the transformation across multiple linear maps, reducing the storage overhead of the compressed representation.

## 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Models and Datasets. We use the WikiText (Merity et al., 2016) training set as the calibration data for most experiments, sampling 128 sequences with a sequence length of 2048. For the mathematics and code generation experiments, we instead use a mixed calibration set of 256 samples, consist-ing of 128 samples from theblackcat102/evol-codealpaca-v1 (Luo et al., 2023) and 128 samples from allenai/tulu-3-sft-personas-math (Lambert et al., 2024). All base-line methods use the same calibration data and calibration configuration as WUSPruner to ensure a fair comparison. We evaluate our method across multiple model families, including Llama3-8B (Grattafiori et al., 2024), Qwen3-8B (Yang et al., 2025), and Qwen3.5-9B (Team, 2026). To further evaluate scalability, we extend the experiments to Qwen3.8-27B and Qwen2.5-72B. 

Baselines. We compare WUSPruner with three representative compression baselines: Wanda-sp (Sun et al., 2024), SliceGPT (Ashkboos et al., 2024), and SVD-LLM (Wang et al., 2025). Wanda-sp is a structured variant of Wanda that ranks removable dimensions using weight magnitude and activation statistics. SliceGPT applies activation-based PCA rotations before dimension deletion. SVD-LLM performs activation-aware low-rank compression using truncation-aware whitening and singular value decomposition. For all comparisons, baseline methods use the same calibration data and calibration configuration as WUSPruner .

Evaluation. We evaluate the pruned models from three complementary perspectives. For language modeling, we report perplexity on WikiText-2 (Merity et al., 2016), PTB (Marcus et al., 1993), and C4 (Raffel et al., 2020). For commonsense reasoning and knowledge, we report accuracy on PIQA (Bisk et al., 2020), HellaSwag (Zellers et al., 2019), ARC-Challenge (Clark et al., 2018), MMLU (Hendrycks et al., 2021), OpenBookQA (Mihaylov et al., 2018), and CMMLU (Li et al., 2024). We further evaluate mathematical reasoning and code generation on GSM-8K (Cobbe et al., 2021), GSM-Platinum, HumanEval (Chen et al., 2021), HumanEval-Plus, MBPP (Austin et al., 2021), and MBPP-Plus. Unless otherwise specified, all downstream evaluations are conducted using the lm-evaluation-harness package (Gao et al., 2024), and HumanEval and MBPP are evaluated using the EvalPlus framework (Liu et al., 2024).

WUSPruner consistently achieves the high-est average accuracy among the pruning methods, obtaining 42.5%, 49.5%, and 51.1% on Llama3-8B, Qwen3-8B, and Qwen3.5-9B, respectively. These results correspond to improvements of 4.5, 7.5, and 9.5 percentage points over the strongest baseline for each model. Moreover, WUSPruner 

achieves the highest accuracy on five out of six individual benchmarks for each model, demonstrat-ing its effectiveness across different model architectures and evaluation tasks. Notably, WUSPruner

## 5 CONCLUSION 

We presented WUSPruner , a transform-based framework for structured pruning of large language models. By formulating pruning as coordinate deletion under a change of basis, we show that the WUS transformation minimizes local reconstruction error over invertible transformations and yields a principled spectral criterion for coordinate selection. We further develop a practical block-wise WUS approximation with compact shared projections for Transformer LLMs. Experiments on models from 8B to 72B demonstrate consistent improvements over structured pruning baselines, together with practical reductions in inference latency and memory usage. These results highlight basis optimization as a principled and effective direction for structured LLM compression. 9Preprint
