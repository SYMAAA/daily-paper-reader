Abstract 

Large Language Models (LLMs) demand efficient inference, where the multiply-accumulate (MAC) operations in linear projections dominate compute and energy cost. Post-training quantization (PTQ) reduces this cost by mapping weights and activations to low-bit integers. Power-of-Two (PoT) weight quantization promises efficient multiplier-free LLM inference via shift-add logic, but suffers significant accuracy degradation at low bitwidths due to its coarse base-2 quantization grid. We introduce Fractional PoT (FPoT) , a base-√2 (half-octave) fractional PoT grid that achieves practical 4-bit weight quantization while preserving a fully integer shift-add datapath. The method combines (i) the base-√2 weight grid with uniform integer activations, (ii) a calibration-time grid-alignment refinement absorbed into the static per-channel scale, and (iii) a lightweight 1-adder √2

approximation whose error is absorbed during weight calibration via approximate-grid quantization, yielding a processing element (PE) with significantly fewer gates than a 4-bit integer multiplier. On Llama-3-8B with 4-bit weight and activation quantization, FPoT improves zero-shot commonsense reasoning performance by 

8.72% relative to the PoT baseline, while maintaining performance close to the uniform integer baseline, with only a negligible 0.4% p degradation. These results hold across various configurations spanning seven models and three bit-widths. Furthermore, FPoT can be integrated with state-of-the-art LLM PTQ methods, demonstrating framework-agnostic applicability. 

1 Introduction 

The rapid proliferation of Large Language Models (LLMs) [Touvron et al., 2023, Grattafiori et al., 2024, Yang et al., 2024, Jiang et al., 2023] has transformed natural language processing, yet their deployment on resource-constrained edge devices remains challenging. One of the dominant costs in LLM inference is the multiply-accumulate (MAC) operation within Transformer [Vaswani et al., 2017] linear projections, which account for virtually all parameters and a majority of inference energy [Wang et al., 2020, Sze et al., 2017]. Recent post-training quantization (PTQ) [Ashkboos et al., 2024, Liu et al., 2025, Hu et al., 2025, Lin et al., 2024a] has pushed integer (INT) quantization

to 4-bit weights, activations, and key-value (KV) cache at near-lossless accuracy, making 4-bit the practical operating point: 8-bit pays with costly multipliers, while lower bits collapse LLM accuracy. Power-of-Two (PoT) quantization, also known as logarithmic quantization [Elhoushi et al., 2021, Li et al., 2020], offers a multiplier-free pathway and further reduces computation: constraining weights to ±2n allows MAC operations to be substituted with bit-wise shifts and an integer addition, substantially reducing the per-MAC energy. Register-Transfer Level (RTL) synthesis on Samsung 5 nm shows a PoT weight MAC at 0.42 × the energy of an equivalent INT MAC [Przewlocka-Rus et al., 2022]. Though efficient, accuracy remains the central obstacle of PoT quantization. Standard PoT places representable levels at geometric intervals of factor 2 (one octave), yielding a worst-case rounding error of 41 .4% (21/2 −1) at the midpoint under a 4-bit round-to-nearest strategy. While this coarseness is tolerable for Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) [Elhoushi et al., 2021, Li et al., 2020, 2023], it causes catastrophic degradation in LLMs: perplexity (PPL) degradation of +9 .5 to +107 .5 when PoT is applied to both weights and activations, or +1 .43 when restricted to weights alone. Standard PoT is not a viable drop-in replacement for multiplication operations in LLMs. 

In this work, we show that the accuracy barriers of 4-bit PoT stem from a grid mismatch with the LLM weight statistics, and introduce Fractional PoT (FPoT) to address this. On Llama-3-8B at 4-bit, applying FPoT to weights reaches on-par performance with the uniform INT baseline (only 

0.4% p 0-shot degradation), while delivering an 8.72% relative gain over standard PoT. Throughout this paper, we use near-lossless to denote configurations whose perplexity increase remains within 

+0 .5 and whose downstream accuracy drop remains within 1.0%p of the uniform INT baseline. Our contributions are as follows. • Theoretically grounded base-√2 grid for 4-bit LLMs : We conduct a Gaussian-proxy analysis to identify base-√2 as the optimal integer-resolution PoT grid at 4-bit, halving the worst-case relative error of standard PoT (41.4% →18.9%). • Approximate-grid quantization with low computational overhead : FPoT quantizes weights to the actual hardware grid using a 1-adder √2 approximation. The resulting processing element (PE) requires significantly fewer gates than a 4-bit integer multiplier. • Near-lossless performance over uniform INT baselines : FPoT integrates with state-of-the-art LLM PTQ frameworks at comparable overhead (Appendix J), achieving performance on par with uniform INT baselines. 

2 Related Work 

2.1 Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) for LLMs 

Uniform INT weight quantization methods such as GPTQ [Frantar et al., 2023] and AWQ [Lin et al., 2024b] achieve competitive accuracy at 4-bit via Hessian-weighted error compensation and activation-aware weight scaling. Based on these works, a line of research on quantizing activations to low-bits emerged for more efficient calculation. However, activation quantization is more challenging due to the high-magnitude outliers concentrated in a small subset of channels [Dettmers et al., 2022]. Extreme outliers cause uniform INT4 activation quantization to collapse most activations to zero under a per-tensor scale dominated by the outlier channels. Gradually, attempts to mitigate such outliers emerged. Early approaches such as SmoothQuant [Xiao et al., 2023] migrate the quantization difficulty from activations to weights via per-channel scaling. Recent methods resolve this via rotation-based preprocessing that redistributes outliers across dimensions: QuaRot [Ashkboos et al., 2024] applies randomized Hadamard rotations, SpinQuant [Liu et al., 2025] learns globally optimal rotations, OSTQuant [Hu et al., 2025] jointly trains rotation with per-channel smooth scales for tighter control, and DuQuant [Lin et al., 2024a] adds dual block-diagonal transforms and channel permutation. These approaches collectively enable 4-bit weights, 4-bit activations, 4-bit KV cache (W4A4KV4) uniform quantization at near-lossless accuracy. While the aforementioned methods enable practical 4-bit activation quantization, they mainly focus on uniform quantization grids. In this work, we apply logarithmic weight quantization to the SoTA quantization methods and demonstrate that it can reach comparable performance with better efficiency. 22.2 Power-of-Two (PoT) and Shift-based Quantization 

PoT quantization [Elhoushi et al., 2021] is a family of methods that constrains weights to ±2n values and replaces multipliers with barrel shifters. Prior works on CNN include DeepShift [Elhoushi et al., 2021], APoT [Li et al., 2020], and DenseShift [Li et al., 2023]. These methods are either not directly applicable to LLMs, or represents weights as sums of multiple PoT terms, requiring n sequential shift-adds per MAC. For LLMs, PoT-PTQ [Wang et al., 2025] is a base-2 quantization that achieves 2–3-bit via data-agnostic scale initialization plus residual fine-tuning. Concurrent with our work, LogART [Xu et al., 2026] extends logarithmic PTQ with learnable rounding and dynamic base allocation between base-2 and base-√2. However, both methods retain floating-point (FP) activations at 16-bit precision (FP16). Since the activations are stored in high bitwidths, the main operation of the previous works is 

FP16 × PoT , requiring FP16 accumulators that cannot be simplified to an integer shift-add unit. Our FPoT pairs a fixed half-octave grid with uniform INT activations, enabling a fully integer shift-add PE. 

Position of this work. Between the two distinct lines of work—activation quantization, which enables fully INT-only arithmetic, and PoT quantization, which replaces multipliers with shift-add PE—a gap remains. Our method, FPoT, aims to address this gap: a multiplier-free LLM quantization method that retains uniform INT activations and composes with recent PTQ frameworks. State-of-the-art uniform INT quantization methods [Ashkboos et al., 2024, Liu et al., 2025, Hu et al., 2025, Lin et al., 2024a] achieve near-lossless W4A4 but retains integer multipliers. PoT-based methods are either CNN-targeted [Elhoushi et al., 2021, Li et al., 2020, 2023], or retain FP activations [Wang et al., 2025, Xu et al., 2026]. FPoT can pair base-√2 weight quantization with SoTA uniform INT activation quantization to achieve efficient and effective W4A4 quantization. √2 is approximated with 1-adder, where the approximation cost is absorbed via approximate-grid quantization (§3). FPoT realizes a fully integer shift-add PE at near-lossless W4A4KV4 accuracy. 

3 Methodology 

Our method applies base-√2 quantization to all linear projection weights of each Transformer block while maintaining uniform INT quantization for activations and the key-value (KV) cache. This design isolates the logarithmic grid to static weights, where calibration can absorb quantization error, while preserving hardware-friendly uniform INT formats for dynamic activations and KV-cache. Weight distributions are redistributed by state-of-the-art LLM PTQ preprocessing (Appendix A) prior to GPTQ calibration, and a calibration-time weight grid-alignment further refines the per-channel scale. In brief, such methods apply learned rotations and per-channel scaling to redistribute channel-concentrated outliers, producing smoother distributions amenable to the logarithmic grid. The transform adds negligible overhead ( ≈0.02% of weight MACs).

21/2 − 1 ≈ 41 .4% at the midpoint between adjacent levels. This coarse resolution is a fundamental accuracy bottleneck for LLM weight quantization using PoT. Approximate-grid quantization. The odd-e path above requires a ×√2 coefficient. A 1-adder realization ( x + x≫1 = 1 .5x) deviates from √2 ≈ 1.414 by 6.1% , which would normally degrade accuracy under naive post-hoc substitution—quantizing to the exact FPoT grid and only replacing the coefficient at dequantization. Our solution is approximate-grid quantization : we quantize weights to the nearest level in the actual hardware grid (i.e., using 1.5 for odd e instead of √2), so that weight calibration absorbs the approximation as part of the quantization grid rather than as post-hoc distortion. Empirical validation of this scheme is reported in §5.3, with the resulting hardware cost analyzed in §4.2.

4 Analysis 

We analyze why the FPoT base-√2 grid is optimal for 4-bit LLM weight quantization (§4.1) and quantify its hardware cost relative to INT and standard PoT baselines (§4.2). 

4.1 Grid-Matching Analysis Gaussian proxy. Raw LLM weights are far from Gaussian: they exhibit heavy-tailed, leptokur-tic distributions with high-magnitude outliers concentrated in a few channels. Recent LLM PTQ preprocessing redistributes channel-concentrated outliers across dimensions, reducing kurtosis and equalizing per-channel dynamic range. We analyze a zero-mean symmetric distribution whose log-magnitude is unimodal—a property that holds for the preprocessed weights, with the Gaussian 

w ∼ N (0 , σ 2) serving as a tractable analytical proxy. Under this proxy, u = log 2 |w| is approx-imately Gaussian in log-space with standard deviation 1/(√2 ln 2) ≈ 1.02 octaves (derivation in Appendix B.2), so ∼95% of probability mass concentrates within ∼4 octaves around the mode. Empirically, the preprocessed weight distributions of all seven models studied closely match this Gaussian-proxy prediction (Appendix C), validating its use for 4-bit grid design. 

Optimal base under the Gaussian proxy. A PoT quantizer with step ratio β = 2 1/k and N

levels covers a dynamic range βN −1; with N =15 , k=1 yields a 16 ,384 × range that far exceeds the ∼4-octave preprocessed weight spread, k=2 yields 128 × (aligned), and k=4 yields 11 × (tail clipping). Numerically minimizing the MSE on w ∼ N (0 , 1) with 15 levels while jointly optimizing the scale s yields β⋆ ≈ 20.55 (effective k⋆ ≈ 1.8), very close to k=2 (Fig. 2): FPoT k=2 achieves MSE = 1 .27 × INT4, while PoT wastes levels near zero ( 3.21 ×) and k≥3 suffers dynamic range starvation ( 2.87 × at k=3 , 5.37 × at k=4 ). This optimality is bit-width specific—at 8-bit the optimal shifts to k⋆ ≈ 19 —so the k=2 choice is specifically motivated by the 4-bit constraint (8-bit analysis and full numerical table in Appendix B.3). 

Normalized Bin Entropy. To quantify how effectively a grid utilizes its fixed bit budget under a given weight distribution, we introduce the Normalized Bin Entropy (NBE). For a quantizer Q with 

N representable levels and level-occupancy probabilities pi = Pr[ Q(W ) = ℓi], we define 

NBE( Q; pW ) = H(p)log 2 N , H(p) = − PNi=1 pi log 2 pi, (5) where N is the budget-determined number of representable levels (e.g., N =15 for the 4-bit symmetric grids studied here: 7 positive + 7 negative + 1 zero). NBE = 1 iff data mass is uniform across all levels; NBE → 0 as mass concentrates on a single level. Information-theoretically, log 2 N · NBE is the expected Shannon code length per weight, so NBE measures the fraction of the fixed bit budget that carries information (Appendix B.1). Implicit magnitude pruning. The k-adaptive zero threshold τk = 2 −1/(2 k) (Eq. 2) interacts with the Gaussian-proxy distribution to map a k-dependent fraction of weights to zero. At k=2 ,

τ2 ≈ 0.841 causes ∼22% of preprocessed weights to be zeroed— implicit magnitude pruning 

that is fully compensated by the 2× finer grid resolution. Empirical measurement on real LLM weights confirms this ratio (Appendix C). These zeroed weights further skip their MACs entirely via zero-gating (§3), providing additional compute reduction beyond the shift-add substitution. Prior PoT methods (DeepShift, APoT) adopt ad-hoc zero strategies without recognizing this k-dependent effect. 

4.2 Hardware Cost Analysis 

We evaluate the hardware benefits of FPoT weight quantization with two complementary metrics: compute-unit (CU) cost from a naive component model and energy from published RTL synthesis data. FPoT is applied to all linear projection layers, converting all weight MACs (6.98 B per token) into shift-accumulate operations (per-projection breakdown in Appendix D). 

Compute Unit Cost. We model hardware cost with a naive component-based analysis (Ap-pendix E.1): an INT multiplier grows quadratically ( W ×A gate-equivalents), while a PoT barrel shifter and FPoT shifter + adder grow linearly. FPoT adds one adder for the √2 approximation (x+x≫1 = 1 .5x, 6.1% error absorbed by approximate-grid quantization, §3). At W4A4, PoT costs 0.38 × and FPoT costs ∼0.50 × the INT multiplier; the O(W A )-vs.-O(W +A) scaling gap widens at higher bit-widths (Table 6). Critically, the additional 1-adder over standard PoT costs only ∼0.12 × extra CU at W4A4 while reducing perplexity overhead by 67% –94% , placing FPoT at the knee of the accuracy–efficiency Pareto frontier (Fig. 3) in the compute-bound prefill regime. Open-source logic synthesis (Yosys [Wolf and Glaser, 2013]) is consistent with the structural ordering INT > FPoT > PoT and the bit-width scaling predicted above (Tables 6–7, Appendix E.2). 

Energy. We anchor the energy comparison to RTL synthesis by Przewlocka-Rus et al. [2022] on Samsung 5 nm (250 MHz, TT corner, 0.6 V, 25°C, 50% toggle rate), which reports a PoT-to-INT energy ratio of 0.42 × at W4A8—a 2.4× reduction per weight MAC. FPoT with the 1-adder √2

unit adds minimal overhead (one adder, active for ∼50% of MACs); we report the FPoT energy ratio as 0.42 –0.46 × at W4A4 ( 0.84 –1.00 × at W4A8; 1.68 –2.00 × at W8A8)—a 2.2–2.4× energy reduction per weight multiply versus INT, with the √2 unit contributing at most 0.04 × overhead for a 50% duty cycle on odd exponents. Appendix E.4 provides per-model accuracy–cost trade-off curves. 

Scope of hardware claims. The 0.42 –0.46 × energy and 0.50 × CU ratios are per weight MAC ,anchored to cell-level RTL synthesis [Przewlocka-Rus et al., 2022]; end-to-end inference energy reduction scales with the fraction of total energy attributable to weight MACs, which is largest in the compute-bound prefill regime and diminishes under memory-bound decode (Limitations, §6).

6 Discussion 

Design rationale and comparison with prior PoT methods. FPoT’s three design choices—a regular geometric grid amenable to GPTQ (vs. APoT’s irregular additive decompositions [Li et al., 2020]), uniform INT activations eliminating runtime log 2 conversion [Elhoushi et al., 2021, Li et al., 2023], and 4-bit grid-resolution targeting (vs. PoT-PTQ’s 2–3-bit scale-calibration focus [Wang et al., 2025])—collectively position FPoT as the only multiplier-free PTQ achieving near-lossless W4A4KV4. LogART [Xu et al., 2026] reports strong W4A16 accuracy but lacks LLM W4A4KV4 evaluation due to absent PTQ preprocessing (Appendix I). 

Broader impact. Multiplier-free LLM inference can reduce the energy consumption and carbon footprint of large-scale deployment. However, cheaper inference may also lower barriers to harmful applications; this work does not introduce new capabilities and applies to existing public models with slightly degraded accuracy. 

Limitations. Efficiency projections use analytical cost models anchored to published RTL syn-thesis [Przewlocka-Rus et al., 2022]; open-source logic synthesis (Yosys [Wolf and Glaser, 2013]) confirms the structural ordering and bit-width scaling (Appendix E.2), and end-to-end RTL/ASIC validation is left for future work. Preprocessing training requires float32 weight copies, limiting single-GPU (80 GB) experiments to ≤14 B parameters. The k=2 optimum is 4-bit specific (8-bit shifts to ≈19 ). All results combine state-of-the-art LLM PTQ preprocessing with GPTQ; compatibility with AWQ or round-to-nearest (RTN) is untested. 

7 Conclusion 

We introduced FPoT—a half-octave fractional PoT weight quantization scheme that, combined with uniform INT activation quantization and state-of-the-art LLM PTQ preprocessing, achieves near-lossless accuracy while enabling multiplier-free shift-add inference. Three contributions enable this result. First, a Gaussian-proxy analysis identifies base-√2 as the optimal integer-resolution grid at 4-bit, halving the worst-case relative error of standard PoT ( 41 .4% →18 .9% ). Second, approximate-grid quantization absorbs the 1-adder √2 hardware error via weight calibration, yielding a PE whose 9compute unit is ∼0.50 × the INT4 multiplier gate count (vs. ∼0.38 × for standard PoT). Third, FPoT achieves consistent near-lossless results across twenty-one configurations spanning seven models and three quantization settings (W4A4KV4 / W4A8KV8 / W8A8KV8), recovering zero-shot accuracy to within 1.0% p of the uniform INT baseline in 20 /21 configurations (mean gap −0.17% p; all 14 

W4 configurations within 0.5% p), and integrates with state-of-the-art LLM PTQ frameworks at comparable overhead (Appendix J). These results suggest that FPoT is a promising pathway toward shift-based LLM inference hardware with near-lossless accuracy, with future work including scaling to larger models, exploring higher bit-widths, and end-to-end RTL/ASIC validation.

B Grid-Matching Theory 

B.1 Normalized Bin Entropy: Properties and Proofs 

We establish five properties of Normalized Bin Entropy (NBE, Eq. (5)) that justify its use as a grid-level design metric. 

Property 1 (Bounds). For any probability vector p ∈ ∆N −1, 0 ≤ NBE( p) ≤ 1, with NBE = 1 

iff pi = 1 /N for all i (uniform occupancy), and NBE = 0 iff pi = 1 for some single level i.

Proof. By the non-negativity of Shannon entropy, H(p) ≥ 0 with equality iff p is a point mass. The upper bound H(p) ≤ log 2 N is the maximum-entropy property of the uniform distribution over N

outcomes, proven by Jensen’s inequality applied to −x log x. Dividing by log 2 N yields the stated bounds. 

Property 2 (Information-theoretic interpretation). Let L⋆(p) denote the expected code length of an optimal prefix-free (Shannon–Fano or Huffman) code for the occupancy distribution p. Then 

log 2 N · NBE( p) = H(p) ≤ L⋆(p) < H (p) + 1 .

That is, log 2 N · NBE is the tightest achievable expected code length per weight, and NBE measures the fraction of the fixed-length budget log 2 N that carries information under the data distribution. 

Proof. Shannon’s source coding theorem states that for any distribution p, H(p) ≤ L⋆(p) < H (p)+1 

bits per symbol. Dividing by log 2 N and rearranging yields the claim. 

Property 3 (Affine invariance of level values). NBE depends only on the occupancy probabilities 

p, not on the level values {ℓi}. Consequently, affine relabeling ℓ′

Property 5 (Correlation with MSE for log-concave distributions). For log-concave symmetric weight distributions (satisfied by the Gaussian proxy and, after PTQ preprocessing, empirically by LLM weights), the quantizer base β = 2 1/k that maximizes NBE lies within a narrow neighborhood of the base that minimizes MSE. Specifically, for N (0 , 1) weights with 4-bit budget ( N = 15 ), both optima satisfy β⋆ ∈ [2 0.50 , 20.60 ] (equivalently, k⋆ ∈ [1 .67 , 2.00] ). 

Empirical justification. The MSE of a log-base-β quantizer on pW decomposes into a tail-clipping term (decreasing in β, as a larger βN −1 reduces clipped tail mass) and an in-range rounding term (increasing in β, as coarser bins accept larger within-bin errors); the interior optimum balances these effects. NBE exhibits the analogous trade-off: too-small β concentrates mass in tail-proximal bins, too-large leaves high-density bins over-populated. Numerical sweep on N (0 , 1) with N =15 confirms both optima in the stated interval (Table 3, Fig. 2). A formal rate-distortion analysis is left to future work. 

Remark. Properties 1–4 are exact; Property 5 is empirical for our operating regime and serves to justify why NBE-based grid design yields near-MSE-optimal quantizers in practice.

D.3 Design Implications 

Per-projection sensitivity guides future aggressive compression designs. The Value projection requires uniform INT activations: full FPoT on WV incurs ∆PPL = +0 .30 versus +0 .04 for weight-only FPoT (Table 5), a 7× gap driven by post-softmax activation noise. For future full-PoT activation 21 designs, the Output projection is the safest candidate, followed by FFN projections; Value should be last.

E.1 Naive Component-Based Cost Model 

We adopt a simple component-counting model with two unit costs: cfa = 1 .0 (full-adder equivalent) for arithmetic logic, and cmux = 0 .5 × cfa for 2-to-1 multiplexer stages (reflecting the lower transistor count of a pass-gate MUX vs. a full adder). All costs are per-bit of the datapath width A (activation bits). 

INT multiplier. A W ×A signed multiplier generates W ×A partial products and reduces them through an adder tree. Complexity grows quadratically :

CINT = W × A. 

PoT barrel shifter. A barrel shifter with (W −1) -bit shift control implements (W −1) cascaded MUX stages, each containing A two-input multiplexers: 

CPoT = ( W −1) × A × cmux = ( W −1) × A × 0.5.

Complexity grows linearly in both W and A, which is the fundamental source of PoT’s hardware advantage. 

FPoT shifter + adder. FPoT uses a barrel shifter with one fewer stage (shift amount = ⌊e/ 2⌋,requiring W −2 control bits), plus a 1-adder √2 approximation unit ( x + x≫1 = 1 .5x, 6.1% error). The constant right-shift ( ≫ 1) is a hard-wired reconnection requiring zero logic gates. The 6.1% coefficient error is absorbed by approximate-grid quantization (Section 5.3):

To corroborate the structural claims of the naive model, we synthesize the three compute units using Yosys 0.9 [Wolf and Glaser, 2013] with ABC generic-gate mapping ( abc -g gates ). E.3 MAC Energy Ratio: Published Synthesis Anchor 

The energy estimates in §4.2 are anchored to the only published RTL-level ASIC synthesis comparing INT and PoT MAC units at identical conditions: Przewlocka-Rus et al. [2022] (Samsung 5 nm, 250 MHz, TT corner, 0.6 V, 25°C, 50% toggle rate). Relative to an 8 ×8 uniform MAC baseline, they report W4A8 uniform MAC at 2.5× energy reduction and W4A8 PoT MAC at 6.0×. The PoT-to-INT 23 ratio for the same W4A8 precision is therefore: 

EPoT,W4A8 

EINT,W4A8 

= 2.56.0 ≈ 0.42 ×.

FPoT adds the ×√2 unit (one adder, active for ∼50% of MACs). We report the FPoT/INT energy ratio as a range: 0.42 –0.46 ×, where the lower bound assumes negligible √2 switching energy and the upper bound assumes proportional contribution. 

Applicability to LLMs. Although Przewlocka-Rus et al. [2022] synthesize CNN-oriented MAC units (ResNet/VGG benchmarks), the reported per-MAC energy ratio is model-agnostic: the syn-thesized block—a 4×8 shift-add unit vs. a 4×8 uniform multiplier—is structurally identical to the per-weight MAC in our LLM datapath. Model-level differences (sequence length, attention, layer count) affect only the aggregate MAC count, not the per-MAC energy, so the 0.42 × ratio transfers directly to LLM inference. Note that this anchor reports per-MAC energy of the arithmetic unit alone; total LLM inference energy additionally includes memory-access, accumulator, and activation-path contributions, the relative weight of which varies with batch size and sequence length. The per-MAC ratio therefore sets the achievable upper bound on weight-arithmetic energy savings rather than the total system-level reduction.

Figure 19: Accuracy–cost Pareto trade-off: Qwen2.5-14B. 27 F Fused Triton Kernel for FPoT Training 

The FPoT fake-quantization pipeline (quantize → dequantize → STE) generates ∼8 intermediate tensors per weight matrix when implemented in standard PyTorch—sign, absolute value, log 2 result, exponent, zero/kill masks, and dequantized output—each the same size as the weight. During gradient-checkpointed backward passes, these intermediates are regenerated, creating a memory peak that exceeds single-GPU capacity for models above ∼13 B parameters on 80 GB GPUs. We implement a fused Triton kernel that performs the entire pipeline in a single kernel launch: 1. Quantize : compute |x/s |, apply zero threshold ( 2−0.5/k ), compute round (log 2(·) × k),clamp exponents, determine sign and kill masks. 2. Dequantize : compute s · sign · 2e/k .3. STE : output xfake ; backward passes gradient through unchanged via a custom 

torch.autograd.Function .All intermediate values reside in GPU registers and are never materialized as global-memory tensors. The kernel handles per-channel broadcast of scale, n, and m parameters natively through stride-based indexing, avoiding expand as().contiguous() copies that would otherwise duplicate these tensors to full weight size.

5,120 ), the fused kernel reduces peak intermediate memory from 1.07 GB to 0.19 GB—an 82% reduction . This enables FPoT training of 14B-parameter models on a single A100-80GB with batch size 4, previously infeasible due to OOM during gradient checkpoint recomputation. 

Numerical fidelity. The kernel performs all arithmetic in float32, matching the precision-critical 

log 2 and 2(·) operations. On a full-size weight tensor, 99 .6% of elements match the PyTorch reference exactly; the remaining 0.4% differ by at most one quantization level due to bf16 →float32 promotion order differences, with the Triton path being strictly more precise. A pure-PyTorch fallback is provided for environments without Triton.

G.5 Rounding Mode 

The standard FPoT rounding uses nearest-neighbor in log-space: e = round (log 2(|w/s |) · k), which places decision boundaries at the geometric mean of adjacent PoT levels. LogART [Xu et al., 2026] is a concurrent logarithmic quantization method that also targets multiplier-free inference via power-of-two weight representations. Both methods share the same hardware motivation—replacing integer multipliers with barrel shifters—but differ in scope and design: • LogART : Dynamic base assignment (per-channel base-2 or base-√2), learned rounding (500 iterations/layer), asymmetric quantization. Full W/A/KV quantization within a unified framework.

However, when INT4 activation quantization is applied, LogART’s weight-quantized model degrades catastrophically (PPL > 3900 or ∞; 0-shot 9 drops to ∼34 %, near random), while FPoT maintains stable accuracy (PPL ≤ 7.60 , 0-shot 9 ≥ 61 .9%). This confirms that distribution redistribution via state-of-the-art LLM PTQ preprocessing is a prerequisite for stable joint W4A4KV4 quantization . Without Hadamard rotation, channel-concentrated activation outliers dominate the per-token scale, effectively quantizing the majority of activations to zero. FPoT’s compatibility with state-of-the-art LLM PTQ preprocessing resolves this at the source by redistributing outliers prior to quantization, enabling stable W4A4KV4 deployment that weight-only methods do not support.

Integration. We replace DuQuant’s round-to-nearest (RTN) weight quantizer with a column-wise weight calibration backend that supports both uniform INT and FPoT grids. Activations and the KV-cache remain on the uniform INT grid in all configurations; only the weight grid is swapped. We use symmetric per-channel weight quantization, no learnable weight clipping (epochs = 0), and a single calibration seed (WikiText-2, 128 samples, seqlen 2048). The weight calibration backend performs its own MSE-based clip-ratio search per channel, independent of DuQuant’s smooth/rotation transforms.

Several observations are worth highlighting: • FPoT overhead is small and framework-invariant. Across all completed model–config pairs, replacing uniform INT weights with FPoT increases perplexity by +0.05–0.34 in the DuQuant pipeline, comparable to the +0.08–0.42 range observed with OSTQuant (Table 1). The overhead is consistently smallest at W4A4KV4, where the quantization grid is already coarse and the additional granularity of the FPoT half-octave grid compensates well. • DuQuant matches OSTQuant on absolute quality. For Llama-2-7B, the two frameworks produce nearly identical perplexity under both uniform and FPoT grids (within ±0.15 PPL across all bit-widths). For Llama-3-8B the gap is slightly larger ( ≈0.3 PPL at W4A4), reflecting differences in the preprocessing strategy rather than the weight grid. • Zero-shot accuracy tracks perplexity. 1.1%p when switching from uniform to FPoT—within the noise margin of single-seed runs—confirming that the perplexity overhead translates to negligible downstream impact. These results demonstrate that FPoT’s near-lossless multiplier-free property is not tied to a specific PTQ framework: any pipeline that performs channel-level weight calibration (e.g. column-wise MSE search or OSTQuant’s learned scaling) can adopt FPoT weights with minimal additional quality loss.
