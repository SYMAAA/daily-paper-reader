Abstract 

With the rapid development of vision-language models (VLMs), Web-GUI agents have achieved promising performance in interacting with real-world interfaces through screenshot-based grounding. However, high-resolution screenshots and multi-turn histories introduce excessive visual tokens, causing substantial inference overhead. Existing token prun-ing methods, mainly designed for natural im-ages, often select tokens independently based on importance scores, which can fragment the local visual structures required for rec-ognizing small GUI elements such as but-tons, icons, and hyperlinks. In this work, we propose STP-GUI (Spatially-aware Token 

Pruning for GUI agents), a query-guided to-ken pruning framework that preserves fine-grained instruction-conditioned token scoring while performing spatially coherent window-level selection under aggressive token reduc-tion. A lightweight selector is optimized through policy learning while the VLM back-bone remains frozen. Experiments on Online-Mind2Web show that STP-GUI achieves a fa-vorable efficiency-performance tradeoff under low token budgets. At 21% token retention, it preserves 77.7% of full-token performance while reducing inference latency by 31% and FLOPs by 57%. Results on Android in the Wild further suggest that the method transfers to mobile GUI environments.

The cost is a large visual to-ken sequence: for instance, Qwen3-VL (Bai et al., 2025a) encodes a single 1920 ×1080 screenshot into approximately 2,040 visual tokens. Further-more, as is a common practice in Web-GUI agent systems (Fu et al., 2025; Qin et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025; Bai et al., 2024), agents receive multi-ple interaction history—prior screenshots, actions, and observations—at each step, which can eas-ily push the total visual token count beyond 10K. Reducing visual token load is therefore a practical challenge. arXiv:2610.20390v1 [cs.CV] 14 Oct 2026 A straightforward strategy is to prune visual to-kens before input the LLM modules, as has been studied for VLM (Shen et al., 2025; Shang et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025). Web-GUI screenshots, however, pose distinct challenges that make stan-dard pruning strategies unreliable. First, informa-tive regions are sparse, localized, and often tiny—a single icon or hyperlink may occupy only a handful of visual tokens—while most of the page consists of blank background, repeated layout blocks, or decorative structure. Second, the spatial continuity of these small regions is essential: losing a few adjacent patches can destroy the visual evidence needed to identify a clickable element. These char-acteristics make pruning strategies designed for natural images (Jizhihui et al., 2026; Xing et al., 2024), where foreground objects are large and con-tinuous, a poor fit for web interfaces. Despite these challenges, prior pruning paradigms in the VLM literature—attention-based selection (Jizhihui et al., 2026; Xing et al., 2024; Shang et al., 2025), similarity-based filtering (Cai et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2025; Jeddi et al., 2025), and learnable summarization (Cha et al., 2024) may remain suboptimal for GUI screenshots. Existing pruning methods (Jizhihui et al., 2026; Sun et al., 2026) involves two related decisions: estimating instruction relevance and organizing the retained evidence. Token-wise selection offers fine-grained relevance ranking, but may retain disconnected fragments of interface elements. Window-wise selection imposes local spatial structure, yet its effectiveness also depends on whether the underlying scores identify useful regions. We therefore study scoring and selec-tion granularity jointly. Our matched-budget comparison shows that pooling Attention scores into windows yields modest improvements, while window-wise selection provides larger gains with the learned STP-GUI scorer. This motivates a lightweight instruction-conditioned selector that estimates token relevance and retains complete spatial windows before LLM processing. As illustrated in Fig. 1, we propose STP-GUI 

(Spatially-aware Token Pruning for GUI agents), a query–guided token pruning method that se-lects spatially contiguous windows rather than iso-lated tokens. Concretely, STP-GUI first computes token-wise importance scores using a lightweight query–conditioned scoring module, aggregates the scores within W ×W windows, and then uses a Plackett–Luce policy during training to learn which windows to retain. At inference time, windows are selected greedily by score rank under a fixed token budget, while the VLM backbone remains frozen throughout. This design preserves fine-grained to-ken scoring while reducing spatial fragmentation of the retained set, thereby helping the model pre-serve local evidence around GUI elements such as buttons, icons, and label-field pairs. Experi-ments on Online-Mind2Web (300 tasks across 136 live websites) (Xue et al., 2025) show that STP-GUI is particularly competitive at low retention budgets. By evaluating on Android in the Wild (AitW) (Rawles et al., 2023), we further show that the same design transfers beyond web screenshots to mobile GUI settings. At 21% token retention, it achieves the strongest success rate among the com-pared methods reported at their evaluated operating points while reducing inference latency by 31% and FLOPs by 57% relative to the dense baseline. Our main contributions are summarized as: • We identify a previously overlooked failure mode of GUI-agent token pruning: coupling importance estimation and selection granu-larity causes spatial fragmentation of action-relevant visual evidence. • We introduce STP-GUI, a granularity-decoupled selection paradigm that scores to-kens precisely while selecting contiguous win-dows to preserve spatial coherence. • We formulate GUI visual token retention as a budget-constrained discrete selection problem and design a lightweight query-conditioned selector trained with policy learning, while keeping the VLM backbone frozen. • Experiments on Online-Mind2Web show that STP-GUI provides a favorable tradeoff be-tween accuracy and efficiency under token pruning, reducing inference latency by 31% and FLOPs by 57%. Results on AitW also show the generalization for mobile GUI envi-ronments. 

2 Related Work 

Web-GUI Agents. Early GUI agents relied on modular pipelines combining text extraction, rea-soning, and memory components (Hurst et al., 2024; Xie et al., 2024), which can be brittle un-der changing environments (Xia et al., 2024). Re-cent VLM-based agents instead directly ground actions from screenshots and achieve stronger gen-eralization on benchmarks such as Mind2Web and AitW (Wang et al., 2024b; Hong et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2025). Meanwhile, mobile GUI agents fur-ther improve grounding through specialized train-ing pipelines (Tang et al., 2025). However, regard-less of agent architecture, high-resolution visual inputs introduce substantial token overhead, mak-ing efficient visual token selection an important problem for deployment. 

Token Pruning in VLMs. Token pruning re-duces the inference cost of VLMs by removing redundant visual tokens. Existing approaches can be broadly categorized into training-based and training-free methods (Bolya et al., 2022). Training-based methods, such as ATP-LLaVA (Ye et al., 2025), learn adaptive pruning policies or ratios, while STP-GUI instead freezes the VLM backbone and optimizes a lightweight query-conditioned selector for spatially coherent pruning. Training-free methods mainly rely on importance-based scoring (e.g., FastV (Chen et al., 2024), Spar-seVLM (Zhang et al., 2024), MustDrop (Liu et al., 2024), PDrop (Xing et al., 2024)) or redundancy-based filtering (e.g., DART (Wen et al., 2025), Di-vPrune (Alvar et al., 2025)). However, these meth-ods are primarily designed for natural images and select tokens independently, which may fragment the spatial evidence required for GUI grounding. STP-GUI addresses this limitation by decoupling token relevance estimation from evidence reten-tion granularity, enabling spatially coherent token selection under constrained budgets. 

Visual Grounding for GUI Agents. Recent works improve GUI grounding through richer su-pervision or region-aware representations. For ex-ample, Gao et al. (Gao et al., 2024) enhance vision-language alignment with region-level supervision, YORO (Ho et al., 2022) develops efficient visual grounding models, and R-VLM (Park et al., 2025) introduces region-aware visual representations for GUI grounding. Different from these approaches, STP-GUI addresses visual token reduction for a frozen GUI agent rather than modifying the ground-ing backbone. Its selector uses action-level su-pervision and/or full-token model feedback, with-out requiring token-retention masks or UI-element bounding boxes.

M ∈ { 0, 1}Lv is the binary retention mask and N = ⌊βL v⌋ is a fixed retention budget. We refer to β as the keep ratio , i.e., the fraction of vi-sual tokens retained after pruning. For multi-turn GUI agent interaction where input history concate-nated with current observation yields multiple im-age frames, STP-GUI applies the window selection mechanism frame-wise under a uniform keep ra-tio β. This ensures that each temporal visual step retains a spatially contiguous and coherent local representation before entering the LLM. 

3.2 Method Overview 

STP-GUI prunes adapter-projected visual tokens before they enter the LLM. Given visual tokens and an instruction, a lightweight scorer produces token-level relevance scores. These scores are aggregated within non-overlapping spatial windows. During training, windows are sampled under a fixed bud-get and the selector is optimized using action-level feedback from the frozen VLM. At inference, the highest-scoring windows are retained deterministi-cally. 

3.3 Instruction-Guided Token Scoring 

We define a lightweight instruction-conditioned scorer fθ(V, T) : RLv ×d × RLt×d → RLv that maps visual tokens V and text tokens T to per-token importance scores si = fθ(V, T)i. V de-notes the adapter-processed visual token sequence from the vision encoder, and T denotes the em-bedding sequence of the current instruction. Fol-lowing (Feng et al., 2025), T is restricted to the action-relevant portion of the instruction, excluding chain-of-thought reasoning steps. The scorer performs lightweight cross-modal in-teraction between projected visual and textual fea-tures, conditioning each visual token on the instruc-tion to estimate its task relevance. The resulting per-token scores are subsequently aggregated for window-level selection. Per-token Scoring

STP-GUI shifts the selection unit from individ-ual tokens to spatial windows while keeping token-level scoring for fine-grained importance estima-tion. The two operations are decoupled: token scores provide local precision, and window-level selection provides spatial coherence. Specifically, given visual tokens on a spatial grid of size Hg × Wg (where Hg and Wg are the grid height and width in tokens, respectively, with 

Hg · Wg = Lv), we partition the grid into non-overlapping spatial windows of size W × W , in-dexed by w ∈ { 1, . . . , ⌊Hg/W ⌋ · ⌊ Wg/W ⌋} . The per-token scores within each window are aggre-gated into a window-level score: 

sw = ϕ({si}i∈w) (2) where ϕ(·) aggregates the token importance scores within window w. These aggregated window scores parameterize a Plackett–Luce distribution over candidate windows. During training, we sam-ple window subsets from this distribution so that the expected token budget matches the target keep ratio β, and optimize the resulting policy with PPO. At inference time, no policy optimization is in-volved: windows are ranked by the aggregated scores produced by the trained scorer fθ, and the top-ranked windows are selected under the same budget. Because every retained region is a com-plete W ×W patch, the downstream VLM receives local evidence instead of isolated token fragments. 

3.4 Training the Window Selector 

We do not have ground-truth token-retention masks. We therefore optimize the discrete window-selection policy using action-level feedback from the frozen VLM. We therefore sample window sub-sets with a Plackett–Luce policy induced by the aggregated window scores, and use PPO to train the selector toward window choices that produce ac-tion responses consistent with the full-token model while respecting the token budget. 

Reward design. We use two complementary re-ward signals: rgt , which encourages actions con-sistent with task-level supervision when available, and rfull , which encourages the pruned-input policy to remain consistent with the action preference in-duced by the same model under the full, unpruned input. In other words, rfull pushes the selector to retain window subsets that elicit action responses close to those of the full-token model.

4 Experiments 

Benchmarks. Online-Mind2Web (Xue et al., 2025) is a live web agent benchmark comprising 300 tasks across 136 real websites, using task-level success rate (SR) as the primary metric. Tasks re-quire end-to-end interaction with live pages, mak-ing them sensitive to spatial-localization errors in-troduced by aggressive token pruning. Android in the Wild (AitW) (Rawles et al., 2023) is a large-scale mobile GUI dataset spanning diverse app cat-egories. Following (Bai et al., 2024), we evaluate on the General and Web Shopping test splits to assess cross-domain transferability of STP-GUI. 

Baseline model construction. We used Qwen3-VL as the backbone model for our experiments. We construct the Web-GUI supervised training data fol-lowing prior work (Qin et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025; Fu et al., 2025). For Online-Mind2Web and AitW, we separately obtain the corresponding back-bones by supervised fine-tuning(SFT) Qwen3-VL on their training data. STP-GUI and all pruning baselines are evaluated on top of these SFT back-bones. We only performed SFT as the baseline and developing advanced RL method to achieve a higher accuracy is beyond the scope of this paper. 

Implementation details. We fine-tune Qwen3-VL as the base model using LLaMA-Factory (Zheng et al., 2024) on 8 ×A100 (80G) GPUs for 3 epochs with an initial learning rate of 

1e-5 and a cosine decay schedule. During SFT, the ViT encoder is frozen, while the adapter and LLM parameters are updated. After SFT, the VLM backbone is completely frozen for token pruning. STP-GUI only optimizes a lightweight 2M-parameter selector for window retention, without updating any VLM parameters. Unlike pruning methods that adapt VLM parameters during optimization, STP-GUI introduces only a small selection module that can be trained offline and deployed on top of the frozen backbone. The detailed tuning cost comparison with adapted prun-ing baselines is provided in appendix. Training the selector takes approximately 10 hours on 8 GPUs. This additional cost is incurred only once offline, while the learned selector reduces inference latency by 31% and FLOPs by 57% during deployment. with entropy regularization. PPO is an optimiza-tion choice rather than a separate novel algorithmic component. Nevertheless, the optimizer affects the achieved performance, as evaluated in Table 6. For comparison methods, we re-implement rep-resentative pruning approaches (Xing et al., 2024; Jizhihui et al., 2026; Sun et al., 2026; Cai et al., 2025) on Qwen3-VL using their official imple-mentations. To ensure fair comparison, we eval-uate both their original training-free settings and fine-tuned variants adapted on the same SFT data. Layer-sensitive baselines are evaluated using their best-performing configurations, with detailed sen-sitivity analyses provided in the appendix. Since Online-Mind2Web is evaluated on live websites, we repeat experiments 3 times under the same pro-tocol and report the mean SR.

Analysis of Spatial Coherence. Beyond task success rate, we further analyze the spatial struc-ture of the retained tokens. This analysis is mo-tivated by the window-size ablation in 4: when 

W =1 degenerates to token-wise selection, perfor-mance drops at the same keep ratio, suggesting that how retained tokens are organized in space may matter. Ideally, one would also measure whether the retained tokens continue to cover the action-critical target region at each interaction step. However, such a dataset-level evaluation is not directly available in either Online-Mind2Web or AitW: these benchmarks do not provide dense per-step UI-element annotations for complete trajecto-ries, and successful interaction paths are not unique. We therefore focus on a trajectory-level analysis of the binary retention masks produced by differ-ent pruning methods at the same 21% budget, us-ing 4 connected-component statistics (8-connected-component statistics are shown in appendix) as a quantitative measure of spatial fragmentation, and complement this with manually curated case studies in the appendix, where we annotate step-level targets for representative successful Online-Mind2Web episodes and compare them with the retained token regions. 

Ncc is the number of connected components. A smaller value means less fragmentation. Rlargest 

is the fraction of retained tokens that belong to the largest connected component. A larger value means that most retained evidence stays in one dominant region. Amean is the mean area of connected com-ponents (in tokens). A larger value means the re-tained regions are larger and more complete. Hcc 

is the entropy of component sizes. A smaller value means the retained tokens are less evenly scattered across many small regions. In short, better spatial coherence corresponds to lower Ncc and Hcc , and higher Rlargest and Amean .

5 Conclusion 

In this paper, we present STP-GUI, a spatially-aware visual token pruning method for Web-GUI agents. By combining query-guided scoring with window-level selection, STP-GUI preserves action-relevant local evidence as contiguous patches under constrained token budgets. On Online-Mind2Web, it retains 93.8% of full-token performance at 50% retention and 77.7% at 21% retention, while reduc-ing latency by 31% and FLOPs by 57%. These results show that lightweight pre-LLM pruning is a practical design for GUI agents operating under aggressive token constraints. 

Limitations. Our current study focuses on Web-GUI benchmarks and model scales up to Qwen3.5-9B. Additionally, STP-GUI uses a fixed token bud-get and predefined non-overlapping window gran-ularity, which may be less suitable for interface elements that span window boundaries. Neverthe-less, high-resolution encodings preserve sufficient token signals, and our overlap ablation (Tab. 5) sug-gests that coarse locality outweighs strict boundary alignment in practice.

A.1 More Implementation Details 

For comparison methods, we re-implement each method (Xing et al., 2024; Jizhihui et al., 2026; Sun et al., 2026; Cai et al., 2025) on top of Qwen3-VL using their official codebases. To ensure a fair comparison, we evaluate both their original training-free configurations and fine-tuned variants adapted on the same SFT data. Layer-sensitive baselines are evaluated using their best-performing configurations. Detailed sensitivity analyses are provided in appendix. Because Online-Mind2Web is conducted on live websites, we further assess evaluation stability by repeating all experiments 3 times under the same protocol and report the mean success rate in the main results. Across runs, the difference between the highest and lowest suc-cess rates never exceeds 5 successful tasks out of 300 (0.017 absolute SR). Since the performance gap between STP-GUI and the strongest compet-ing baseline is larger than this fluctuation range, the main conclusions are unlikely to be explained by run-to-run evaluation noise alone. We use the action space defined in the agent prompt, which includes discrete action types with optional text or spatial arguments, such as clicking, hotkey invoca-tion, scrolling, and termination.

A.9 Step-wise Visualization of Fragmentation Patterns 

Fig. 5 shows a qualitative visualization of STP-GUI’s token selection across 4 sequential steps of a representative Online-Mind2Web task. Be-cause Online-Mind2Web and AitW do not pro-vide dense per-step target annotations for complete trajectories, and successful interaction paths are not unique, we do not treat such examples as a dataset-level metric. Instead, we manually curate several successful Online-Mind2Web episodes as case studies, annotate the step-level ground-truth targets for those representative steps, and compare them with the retained token regions. In these cases, the selector concentrates the token budget on spa-tially coherent regions (navigation menus, product cards, and pricing tables), while discarding large uniform backgrounds. Token-wise pruning risks fragmenting contiguous GUI elements across the retention boundary; window-level selection instead preserves the local structure that accurate action grounding requires. To further investigate how different pruning strategies affect the spatial structure of retained vi-sual evidence, we provide additional visualizations for 5 representative methods: FlashVLM, PDrop, HiPrune, Attention, and IVC. Figures 6, 10, 7, 8, and 9 show the retained-token patterns of these methods across all execution steps of the same GUI interaction episode(In fact, this example task can be completed in just 4 steps.). Instead of presenting only a single-step snapshot, each large figure visu-alizes the full step-by-step pruning behavior of one method throughout the entire interaction process. These full-process visualizations are useful for examining whether a pruning strategy preserves spatially coherent local evidence or gradually de-generates into fragmented token retention over time. This distinction is particularly important for GUI grounding, where accurate action prediction often depends not only on the target region itself, but also on nearby contextual cues such as surrounding text, icons, and local layout structure. As a result, methods that retain visually scattered fragments may fail to preserve the structured local evidence needed for reliable grounding, even when using a similar token budget. Overall, these visualizations provide comple-mentary qualitative evidence to the quantitative re-sults in the main paper. By comparing the retained-token patterns across multiple execution steps, we can more clearly observe the different fragmenta-tion behaviors induced by different pruning strate-gies and better understand how preserving local spatial coherence may benefit GUI agents.
