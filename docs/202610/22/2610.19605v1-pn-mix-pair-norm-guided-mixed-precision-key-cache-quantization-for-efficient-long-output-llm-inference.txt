# PN-Mix: Pair-Norm Guided Mixed-Precision Key Cache Quantization for Efficient Long-Output LLM Inference 

Gil Jae Song Sungjoo Yoo *

Seoul National University 

jkoi632@snu.ac.kr, sungjoo.yoo@gmail.com 

Abstract 

Long-output inference in large language mod-els suffers from severe KV cache memory and bandwidth bottlenecks. While KV cache quan-tization alleviates this issue, low-bit quantiza-tion (e.g., 2-bit) severely degrades model accu-racy. Recent mixed-precision key-cache quanti-zation methods mitigate this degradation by al-locating higher precision to important key chan-nels, but estimate channel saliency using only key statistics. Through attention logit perturba-tion analysis at the RoPE channel-pair level, we show that quantization errors in channel pairs with large query–key pair-norm products con-tribute significantly to logit variation. Based on this insight, we propose PN-Mix, a mixed-precision key-cache quantization method for RoPE-based decoder-only LLMs, which allo-cates higher precision to salient channel pairs identified once during the prefill stage and reused throughout decoding. Across standard reasoning, code-generation, and AIME bench-marks, PN-Mix with 2.26-bit average precision achieves comparable or better average accuracy than prior mixed-precision methods using 2.32– 2.39 bits. Furthermore, in our Pareto sweep, PN-Mix degrades gracefully down to 1.5 bits, whereas the compared baselines exhibit sharper performance drops below 2.25 bits. 

1 Introduction 

Long-output inference has become a central capa-bility of large language models (LLMs), enabling applications that require extended generation such as code generation and complex problem solving. However, long-output decoding exposes a severe systems bottleneck in the key-value (KV) cache, which stores previous keys and values but grows linearly with sequence length and batch size. KV cache quantization alleviates this bottleneck by storing cached keys and values at lower pre-cision while retaining all token positions and the original cache structure. Yet this compression is inherently lossy: aggressive low-bit quantization (e.g., 2-bit) can introduce errors that substantially degrade reasoning accuracy over long chains of thought (Liu et al., 2025). Thus, effective KV cache quantization requires not only lowering precision, but also allocating the limited bit budget to the most critical components. Recent mixed-precision methods allocate higher precision to salient key channels, motivated by the greater quantization sensitivity of keys than val-ues (Li et al., 2025; Xia et al., 2026; Hariri et al., 2026). However, their saliency estimates rely on arXiv:2610.19605v1 [cs.LG] 14 Oct 2026 key statistics alone and operate at the individual-channel level, overlooking query–key interactions and the channel-pair structure induced by RoPE. In this work, we propose PN-Mix, a pair-norm guided mixed-precision key-cache quantization method designed for RoPE-based decoder-only LLMs. Our key observation is that key-cache quan-tization should be guided by its effect on attention logits, rather than by key statistics alone. Through attention-logit perturbation analysis at the RoPE channel-pair level, we show that quantization errors in channel pairs with large query–key pair-norm products can contribute substantially to logit varia-tion. PN-Mix therefore assigns higher precision to such salient RoPE channel pairs. We further find that salient channel pairs estimated from prefill tokens are predictive of full-sequence saliency, en-abling PN-Mix to identify them once during prefill and reuse them throughout decoding. As a result, PN-Mix remains input-adaptive without offline cali-bration and avoids repeated saliency recomputation during decoding. Overall, this work makes three main contribu-tions: • We derive a query-aware RoPE channel-pair saliency criterion based on query–key pair-norm products from attention-logit perturba-tion analysis. • We propose PN-Mix, a mixed-precision key-cache quantization method that input-adaptively selects salient RoPE channel pairs once during prefill and reuses the resulting selection throughout decoding. • We show that PN-Mix improves long-output inference by reducing average key-cache pre-cision while preserving accuracy and avoiding decoding-time saliency recomputation.

3 Related Work 

Prior work has explored several directions for re-ducing KV cache memory, including token evic-tion (Zhang et al., 2023; Li et al., 2024; Cai et al., 2025), low-rank approximation (Chang et al., 2025; Lee et al., 2025), merging (Zhang et al., 2024b; Wang et al., 2024), pruning (Xu et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2025), and quantization. Among them, KV cache quantization reduces memory by low-ering the representation precision of cached tensors while retaining the cache structure. We focus on KV cache quantization, which can be broadly di-vided into uniform-precision and mixed-precision approaches. 3.1 Uniform-Precision Quantization 

Uniform-precision KV cache quantization assigns the same bit-width to all quantized cache elements. Prior methods reduce low-bit quantization error through key/value-specific grouping (Liu et al., 2024), distribution-aware quantization (Hooper et al., 2024), residual correction (Kang et al., 2024), outlier-token preservation (Su et al., 2025a), Walsh– Hadamard channel rotations (Su et al., 2025b), projection-based 1-bit sign quantization (Zandieh et al., 2025), and polar-coordinate quantization of RoPE channel pairs (Wu et al., 2025). How-ever, because they assign the same bit-width regard-less of cache-element sensitivity, uniform-precision methods may insufficiently control errors in impor-tant components, which can accumulate over long chains of thought in reasoning models (Liu et al., 2025). 

3.2 Mixed-Precision Quantization 

Mixed-precision quantization instead allocates a limited bit budget according to estimated impor-tance. Existing methods differ in allocation gran-ularity, including token-level (Yang et al., 2024), layer-level (Li et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2026), and channel-level allocation. Since key-cache quan-tization importance is highly non-uniform across channels due to channel-wise outliers (Liu et al., 2024; Hooper et al., 2024), recent channel-level methods focus on assigning higher precision to salient key channels. ChanMix (Liao and Wen, 2026) uses an offline calibration set to identify retrieval-head channels, assigns them 4-bit preci-sion, and allocates 3/2/1-bit precision to the remain-ing channels via min–max range-based clustering. Kitty (Xia et al., 2026) dynamically re-estimates channel saliency for each quantization group using average key magnitude, assigning 4-bit precision to salient channels and 2-bit precision to the rest. Although these methods improve over uniform precision, they estimate saliency from ˜K alone and assign precision at the individual-channel level. This misses two structures that directly affect atten-tion: attention logits are determined by the interac-tion between ˜Q and ˜K, and RoPE couples channels into pairs. In contrast, PN-Mix estimates saliency from query–key interactions at the RoPE channel-pair level and allocates mixed precision to the key cache accordingly. 

4 Preliminary Analysis 

To achieve accuracy-preserving ∼2.25-bit KV-cache compression for long-output inference, we investigate three design questions: (1) which cache (key or value) to refine; (2) how to define an impor-tance score for assigning higher precision within the key cache; and (3) how to exploit this impor-tance score efficiently during long-output infer-ence. We examine these questions sequentially and use the resulting observations to guide our mixed-precision key-cache quantization design. 

4.1 Sensitivity of Key and Value Caches to Quantization 

Under a 2.25-bit budget 1, prior work indicates the key cache is more sensitive to quantization than the value cache (Li et al., 2025; Xia et al., 2026). We verify this by quantizing either ˜K or V to 2.25 bits and comparing against an FP16 baseline on AIME datasets. We evaluate DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B (R1-Llama-8B) and Qwen3-8B, generating up to 32K tokens per response. Accounting for at-tention sinks (Xiao et al., 2024), we also evaluate variants preserving the initial 32 tokens in FP16, following prior work (Xia et al., 2026). Table 8 shows that V -only quantization causes less degradation than ˜K-only quantization, even without sink-token protection. While preserving the first 32 tokens in FP16 improves both, V -only quantization approaches the FP16 baseline whereas 

˜K-only quantization still exhibits non-negligible degradation. These results lead to two design choices. First, sink tokens should be preserved in full precision for both caches. Second, key-cache quantization should be refined more carefully than value-cache quantization under low-bit compression.

4.3 Efficient Importance Estimation for Long-Output Inference 

With σh,i established as the importance score, the remaining challenge is to estimate it efficiently dur-ing long-output inference. ChanMix relies on of-fline profiling, eliminating online selection over-head but sacrificing input-adaptive channel allo-cation, whereas Kitty performs input-adaptive se-lection for each quantization group. However, this requires repeatedly identifying salient channels and storing a separate set of channel indices for each group. As decoding proceeds and more groups ac-cumulate, the resulting index storage and loading overhead can grow substantially. To retain input-adaptive importance while avoid-ing repeated salient-pair selection and index man-agement during long decoding, we choose to com-pute σh,i once from the prefill tokens and reuse the selected channel pairs throughout decoding. For this prefill-only strategy to be effective, the prefill-based importance score should satisfy two properties. First, it should be predictive: channel pairs identified as important during prefill should re-main salient throughout subsequent decoding. Sec-ond, it should be selective: the importance should be concentrated on a subset of channel pairs so that assigning higher precision to the selected pairs remains effective under a limited bit budget. We first evaluate the predictiveness of prefill-based selection by comparing its quantization qual-ity against a full-sequence oracle that computes importance scores using the complete sequence. Using the same H3(50%)L1 simulation setup as in §4.2, Figure 2 shows that the prefill-based ˜Q ˜K

To further understand why prefill-based selection remains effective, Appendix C compares the chan-nel pairs identified from prefill with those that are salient during decoding. Among the evaluated crite-ria, the prefill-based ˜Q ˜K pair norm yields the high-est average Jaccard overlap with decoding-time salient pairs. Moreover, Appendix L shows that this overlap does not systematically decrease as decoding progresses, including in long-generation and multi-topic settings. We next examine selectivity. Approximately 97– 99% of the total importance is concentrated in the top 50% of channel pairs (Figure 5); these values denote the median across layers, KV heads, and prompts. Together, these results indicate that σh,i can be computed once during the input prefill stage and that the selected channel pairs can be reused throughout long-output decoding.

7 Conclusion 

We presented PN-Mix, a pair-norm-based mixed-precision key-cache quantization method for RoPE-based decoder-only LLMs, designed to support ef-ficient long-output inference. PN-Mix allocates higher precision to RoPE channel pairs with large query–key pair-norm products and estimates these salient pairs once during prefill, avoiding offline calibration and repeated decoding-time saliency re-computation. Across standard reasoning, code gen-eration, and AIME benchmarks, PN-Mix achieves comparable or better average accuracy than prior mixed-precision baselines with lower average key-cache precision. It also remains robust in the sub-2.25-bit regime and expands the feasible serving region for long-output inference. 

Limitations 

PN-Mix is most directly applicable to RoPE-based decoder-only LLMs, as its saliency score is defined over RoPE channel pairs. Extending the same for-mulation to models without RoPE-style channel pairs may require additional adaptation. Our efficiency results are measured on a single NVIDIA A100-SXM4-40GB GPU with our current Triton-based implementation. Since wall-clock la-tency depends on hardware, kernel optimization, batching strategy, and serving infrastructure, the exact speedup of PN-Mix may vary across deploy-ment environments.

G Kernel Implementation 

We implement all kernels in Triton, including quan-tization and bit packing of the key and value caches, as well as the fused dequantization and attention operations during decoding. The kernels are implemented for the configu-ration used in our downstream task evaluation, namely bH = 3 , bL = 1 , and bV = 2 . After mixed-precision quantization of the key cache, salient channel pairs are represented by integers in the range [0 , 7] , while the remaining channels are rep-resented by integers in the range [0 , 1] . Since the least significant 1 bit is shared by all channels and the upper 2 bits are meaningful only for salient channel pairs, our implementation packs these two parts into separate uint8 tensors. Specifically, we first extract the LSBs of all channels and pack every 8 consecutive token values of each channel into one byte, producing packed_low , which is stored for all channels. We then extract the upper 2 bits and pack every 4 consecutive token values of each chan-nel into one byte, producing packed_hi , which is stored only for the salient channel pairs. For the value cache, after quantization, every 4 consecutive channel values of each token are packed into one byte and stored as a uint8 tensor. During the decoding-stage attention computa-tion, we launch two Triton kernels: one for com-puting ˜Qh,t ˜K⊤

For the quantized re-gion, the dequantized key or value cache is never written back to global memory. Instead, each quan-tization group is loaded into on-chip registers, de-quantized, and immediately consumed by the dot-product computation.

L Step-Wise Stability of Prefill-Based Saliency Selection 

Appendix C shows that channel pairs selected from the prefill states exhibit high overlap with decoding-time salient channel pairs on average. However, this averaged overlap does not reveal whether the overlap between the channel pairs selected during prefill and decoding-time salient channel pairs re-mains stable throughout generation or decreases at later decoding steps. We therefore examine how this overlap changes over the course of decoding under long-generation and multi-topic stress-test settings. For each causal query–key pair (t, s ) at decod-ing step t, we compare the top-r% channel pairs selected from the prefill states with the top-r%

salient channel pairs identified for that query–key pair. To prevent the overlap from being dominated by prefill keys, we restrict s to keys generated dur-ing decoding up to step t. We use two reference scores to identify the decoding-time salient chan-nel pairs: the pair-norm score, ∥qti∥2∥ksi ∥2, and the angle-aware absolute pair dot product, |⟨ qti, ksi ⟩| .For reporting, we group the decoding steps into four consecutive ranges and average the overlap over all eligible query–key pairs, heads, and lay-ers within each range. We additionally report the difference between the maximum and minimum overlap across 1K-step bins for the long-generation setting and across the four reporting bins for the multi-topic setting to summarize temporal varia-tion. Table 11 shows that the overlap between the channel pairs selected during prefill and decoding-time salient channel pairs remains stable through-out decoding in both stress-test settings. In the long-generation setting, where R1-Qwen-7B gener-ates 32,672 tokens from only 96 prefill tokens, the overlap for the ˜Q ˜K pair-norm criterion at r = 50% 

stays at approximately 0.72 across all four decod-ing ranges under the pair-norm score and at approx-imately 0.68 under the absolute pair dot product. This indicates that the overlap does not progres-sively decrease over the long decoding trajectory. The same pattern holds in the multi-topic setting containing 60 questions from 57 MMLU subjects. The overlap remains approximately 0.75 under the pair-norm score and approximately 0.71 under the absolute pair dot product across all four decod-ing ranges. The overlap therefore remains stable not only under long generation but also when the prompt spans many different topics. Across both stress-test settings, the ˜Q ˜K pair-norm criterion also maintains the highest overlap among the compared selection criteria. The small differences between the maximum and minimum overlap further con-firm limited temporal variation. A similar pattern at r = 10% shows that this behavior is not specific to the r = 50% selection ratio. Overall, these results indicate that the overlap between the channel pairs selected during prefill and decoding-time salient channel pairs remains relatively stable throughout decoding in the tested long-generation and multi-topic settings.
