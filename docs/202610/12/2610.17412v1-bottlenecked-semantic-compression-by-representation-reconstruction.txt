ABSTRACT
Vision Transformers incur high computational costs when processing long visual
token sequences. Existing reduction methods typically rank tokens independently
and discard those deemed unimportant or redundant. We propose a Reconstruction-
guided framework for visual token condensation(ReConV), which that summarizes
the complete visual sequence with a compact Summary Bank (SMB) and retains
tokens whose information is not adequately captured by the SMB, as indicated by
their reconstruction residuals. At each compression layer, a lightweight position-
conditioned decoder reconstructs the current tokens, leaving shared information
compressed in the SMB while explicitly preserving poorly reconstructed details.
Experiments on image classification and multimodal understanding demonstrate
favorable performance–efficiency trade-offs: ReConV surpasses the uncompressed
baseline with 48% fewer GMACs on ImageNet and enables reusable image-level
representations for efficient multi-turn inference. Code is available at TokenDrop.

INTRODUCTION
Vision Transformers (ViTs) have become a dominant architecture in modern computer vision, but
their self-attention cost grows quadratically with the number of input tokens. This quickly becomes
prohibitive for high-resolution images, multiple images, and videos. The problem is even more severe
in multimodal large language models (MLLMs), where visual tokens are concatenated with text
tokens and jointly processed by an LLM, leading to memory, latency, and throughput burdens.
The redundancy of visual representations has motivated token reduction methods that prune or
merge tokens under a constrained computational budget. In conventional visual recognition, token
reduction is motivated by the observation that image understanding progressively condenses local
patch information into higher-level semantic representations. Accordingly, existing methods estimate
token importance Rao et al. (2021); Yin et al. (2022), merge tokens with similar representations Bolya

methods, however, were developed for single-image classification and are often guided by object-

Consequently, visual token reduction in MLLMs has increasingly relied on text-to-image attention
from either the vision encoder or the LLM decoder to rank visual tokens Shang et al. (2024); Chen
et al. (2024b); Xing et al. (2024); Chen et al. (2024a; 2026). However, attention primarily reflects
local information routing for a particular layer, head, and query, with no guarantee that its magnitude
reliably captures token utility or the causal effect of removing a token. It may also be affected by
model-induced saliency priors and systematic preferences for particular tokens, and vary across layers
and heads. Moreover, accessing attention matrices can interfere with efficient kernels Dao (2024),
while decoder-side pruning cannot reduce computation in preceding LLM layers. We summarizes
representative visual token reduction paradigms in Table 1. To eliminate dependence on attention,
we introduce a learnable Summary Bank (SMB) that semantically aggregates the complete visual
sequence, and a lightweight position-conditioned decoder that reconstructs the original tokens from
this compact representation. As illustrated in Figure 1, this reconstruction-based criterion requires
neither attention distributions nor textual prompts and enables visual token reduction before the LLM.
A second limitation is that existing scoring methods evaluate tokens independently, potentially retain-
ing redundant high-ranking tokens while discarding subtle evidence. Although merging Shang et al.
(2024) accounts for redundancy, it may obscure details absent from the compressed representation.
ReConV instead encodes the complete visual sequence into the SMBs and uses reconstruction residu-
als to identify uncaptured information: well-reconstructed tokens are removed, while high-residual
tokens are retained as complementary evidence. Selection therefore depends on what the SMB has
jointly captured, bridging pruning and information aggregation. Our method retains 100.1% of the
baseline classification performance with 48% fewer GMACs and 89% of multi-turn performance
using only 5% of the visual tokens. Patch-to-SMB assignments also reveal emergent semantically
coherent aggregation (Figure 3).
Our main contributions are as follows:
1. We propose ReConV, a reconstruction-guided framework that compresses visual tokens
through a compact Summary Bank (SMB) and lightweight position-conditioned reconstruc-
tion.
2. We propose a set of learnable Summary Bank (SMB) tokens that aggregate the complete
visual sequence through asymmetric cross-attention. Reconstruction residuals measure
information not captured by this joint summary and guide token retention. Patch-to-SMB
assignments further reveal emergent semantic grouping.
3. ReConV generalizes across classification and multimodal dialogue, matching the uncom-
pressed classification baseline with 48% fewer GMACs and enabling efficient multi-turn
inference through reusable image-level representations.

RELATED WORK
Token reduction in ViTs is motivated by the observation that accurate recognition often requires only
a subset of the visual tokens. Early methods such as DynamicViT Rao et al. (2021), EViT Liang et al.
(2022), Evo-ViT Xu et al. (2022), A-ViT Yin et al. (2022), and ATS Fayyaz et al. (2022) estimate token
importance using attention signals or learned scoring functions and remove low-scoring tokens during
inference. To preserve information that would otherwise be discarded, subsequent methods such as
PPT Wu et al. (2023), ToMe Bolya et al. (2023), Token Pooling Marin et al. (2023), LookupViT Koner
et al. (2024), and STViT Chang et al. (2023) compress tokens through merging, pooling, or clustered
latent representations. Our method instead uses reconstruction-guided Summary Bank (SMB) tokens
as a latent bottleneck, avoiding direct importance scoring and irreversible merging.
Visual token reduction in MLLMs is often text-guided. SparseVLM Zhang et al. (2024) uses decoder
cross-attention and recycles pruned tokens through density-peak aggregation, while FastV Chen
et al. (2024b) and PDrop Xing et al. (2024) prune according to LLM or instruction-to-image at-
tention. PruMerge Shang et al. (2025) and VisionZip Yang et al. (2025) employ merging, whereas
AdaptVision Lin et al. (2025) selectively acquires high-resolution crops. In contrast, our vision-only,
reconstruction-guided framework requires neither prompts, attention heuristics, nor region-grounding
supervision, enabling reusable image-level compression across ViTs and MLLMs.
Despite their differences, these methods rely on two approximations: attention magnitude is a
reliable proxy for token utility, and independent top-K scoring approximates subset optimization,
although a token’s utility depends on what has already been preserved. Consequently, redundant
high-scoring tokens may be retained while complementary evidence is discarded (Fig. 1). We address
both limitations with an SMB that summarizes the complete sequence and reconstruction residuals
that measure each token’s uncaptured information relative to this summary. Sec. 3 details the method.

We evaluate image classification on ImageNet-1K Deng et al. (2009) using 224 × 224 inputs.
Following prior work, we adopt DeiT-S Touvron et al. (2021) as our primary backbone and insert

to λ = 0.05 for all experiments. When distillation is enabled, we apply a soft KL-divergence loss
with a DeiT-S model trained from scratch as the teacher. The overall objective is

4.1.2
MAIN RESULTS
Table 2 shows that our method achieves a stronger efficiency–accuracy trade-off than prior approaches.
We obtain 80.4% Top-1 accuracy while reducing GMACs by 38% relative to DeiT-S; even under
a more aggressive 48% reduction, our model still matches the backbone accuracy. We outperform
TokenPooling, which achieves 100.2% accuracy but reduces FLOPs by only 3.7% relative to DeiT-S.
In addition, we surpass methods that rely on importance-based pruning, such as Dynamic ViT Rao
et al. (2021), A-ViT Yin et al. (2022), and ATS Fayyaz et al. (2022), suggesting that hard token
dropping based solely on saliency can be destructive when the removed tokens might need to support
deeper semantic abstraction. Meanwhile, we achieve a better accuracy trade-off than EViT Liang
et al. (2022), Evo-ViT Xu et al. (2022), PPT Wu et al. (2023), and ToMe Bolya et al. (2023), which
preserve information through token merging or pooling. These results indicate that SMB can more
effectively absorb visual information into a compact global representation.
SMB Utilization and Semantic Partitioning
As shown in Fig. 3, patch-to-SMB assignments form
spatially contiguous, semantically related regions despite receiving no spatial or semantic supervision.
This suggests that the SMBs do not merely aggregate patches according to low-level proximity,
but naturally develop specialization for recurring visual structures. Figure 3 also shows that the
residual-norm dropping policy preferentially removes tokens from slowly varying regions, while
retaining tokens near object boundaries and other high-frequency details. This is consistent with
our design, which removes redundant structure while preserving visually informative content. We
provide quantitative ablations in Appendix B.1.1. Comparing SMB utilization maps from single- and
three-layer compression models reveals more pronounced aggregation in the latter; similar behavior
also emerges in multimodal tasks(see Appendix B.3).
We analyze the SMB usage distribution over the full validation set and find that the SMBs are
used approximately uniformly. For the A1A3A5 model, the corresponding entropies for the three
compressive layers are 2.7030, 2.7531, and 2.7418 nats. These results indicate that the model makes
effective use of the available SMB budget. For each SMB, we aggregate all tokens assigned to it
across the validation set and construct a class distribution using the labels of their source images. For
the 16-SMB model, the entropy values range from 6.1162 to 6.8889 nats, with a mean of 6.8219,
close to the maximum of 6.9078 nats for 1000 classes. This high entropy indicates that no SMB is
dominated by a small subset of classes. Together with the coherent assignment maps, this suggests
that SMBs capture class-agnostic mid-level visual structure rather than acting as class-specific experts.

4.2.2
MAIN RESULTS
Evaluation protocol.
Multi-turn dialogue is a natural setting for image-level compression because
multiple questions share the same image. ReConV compresses each image once and reuses its
visual representation across turns, amortizing visual encoding and compression costs. In contrast,
prompt-conditioned selection depends on the current query and must be recomputed whenever the
prompt changes. We evaluate this capability on MT-VQA-v2 Goyal et al. (2017) and ConvBench Liu
et al. (2024b). MT-VQA-v2 contains 3–14 questions per image, which we treat as successive inde-
pendent queries, whereas ConvBench comprises native conversations with cross-turn dependencies.
MetaCompress Wang et al. (2026) evaluates a similar setting using a manually constructed VQAv2
subset with fixed three-turn dialogues; however, neither the subset nor its implementation is publicly
available, precluding a direct comparison. For ConvBench, we report direct-grading scores from
Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct on a 0–10 scale, using the same judge and prompt for all methods.
At a 95% token reduction rate, ReConV achieves the best MT-VQA-v2 overall accuracy and Con-
vBench average among compressed methods, while providing 2.73× and 1.51× speedups, respec-
tively. This favorable accuracy–efficiency trade-off under extreme compression validates the SMB’s
effectiveness in absorbing information from the complete visual sequence.

CONCLUSION
In this paper, we introduced a reconstruction-guided framework for visual token compression. By
compressing token sequences into a compact set of learnable Summary Bank tokens and using recon-
struction residuals to guide token retention, our method enables hard token dropping without hard
semantic deletion. Experiments on image classification and multimodal understanding demonstrate
favorable performance–efficiency trade-offs across tasks.

semantically coherent aggregation than single-layer compression.

Implementation Details.
Following LLaVA-1.5, we use AdamW with a cosine learning-rate
schedule. The only modifications are the inserted compressive layers and the additional reconstruction
loss, which is applied during both pretraining and fine-tuning with different weights to balance
compression and task adaptation. We keep the rest of the training recipe unchanged and train the two
modules jointly for 2 epochs. For evaluation, we use greedy decoding with temperature set to 0 to
ensure deterministic outputs.
Datasets.
We use the 558K image-text alignment dataset and the 665K multimodal instruction
dataset released with the backbone Liu et al. (2024a) for pretraining and instruction tuning, respec-
tively. We evaluate our model on seven widely adopted benchmarks, including MMBench Liu et al.
(2024c), MME Fu et al. (2023), POPE Li et al. (2023), SQA Lu et al. (2022), VQAText Singh et al.
(2019), and VQA Goyal et al. (2017).
Multi-model Understanding
We evaluate the proposed compression framework on multimodal
reasoning and understanding benchmarks using LLaVA-1.5-13B Liu et al. (2024a) under retained
token budgets of 128, 64, and 32. We include ToMe Bolya et al. (2023) as a related baseline, although
its performance is relatively limited in the MLLM setting, since it represents an early attempt to bridge
ViT compression and multimodal models. For MMBench and MMBench(CN) Liu et al. (2024c), we
report dev-set results because test labels are not publicly available and the online evaluation server is
no longer accessible.
As shown in Table 7, SMB remains competitive across token budgets while substantially reducing
the visual prefix length. It is particularly effective on reasoning-heavy benchmarks such as MMB and
MMB-CN, and it continues to preserve most of the baseline capability with 64 or even 32 retained
tokens, indicating that the learned compression is robust and scalable. On POPE, the relatively
small gap on the adversarial split suggests that token compression has limited additional impact
on robustness to semantically plausible absent objects, while the lower recall indicates that the
compressed model remains conservative in predicting object presence.
Efficiency.
We measure prefill latency on POPE on a single A800 GPU, as reported in Table 8. In
the 64-token setting, our method achieves a prefill latency of 37.84 ms per sample, corresponding to
a 2.97× speedup over the backbone, while retaining 96% of the backbone performance on POPE.
Because compression shortens the visual prefix during both prefill and training, it also yields a
comparable reduction in training and fine-tuning time.
B.3
SEMANTIC PARTITION MAP IN MLLM
The visualization of SMB partitioning further shows that after two compression layers, the retained
visual tokens align well with the keywords in the question prompt, indicating that the kept tokens
preserve the fine-grained visual details relevant to downstream reasoning and remain semantically
grounded in the task-specific query. Meanwhile, SMB provides complementary global semantic
context. Together, keyword-level detail preservation and global semantic coverage help explain the
effectiveness of our method under visual self-driven signals, without relying on attention saliency.
