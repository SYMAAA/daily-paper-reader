QUERY-GUIDED VISUAL TOKEN PRUNING IN VIDEO
LARGE LANGUAGE MODELS

ABSTRACT
Video large language models (Video LLMs) have demonstrated strong multimodal
understanding across a range of tasks, including visual question answering and
video captioning. Yet, encoding each frame into hundreds of visual tokens makes
inference over temporal visual content expensive. Existing methods reduce this
cost by merging similar tokens or using text-to-vision attention to select task-
relevant tokens. Nevertheless, these methods still face two main challenges: (1)
Hierarchy-blind Pruning: many similarity-based methods rely on pairwise sim-
ilarity or training a lightweight neural network to predict importance scores with-
out explicitly considering the regional compressibility of a frame, which can mis-
guide the merging or removal of critical evidence. (2) Attention Drift: position-
dependent effects from Rotary Position Embedding (RoPE), which encodes token
positions in the decoder, together with preferences for nearby or recent tokens
and attention sinks can shift query-to-vision attention away from task relevance,
causing attention-based pruning to underweight and discard task-relevant visual
tokens. Motivated by these observations, we propose FoveaTree, a two-stage,
training-free visual-token pruning framework for efficient video inference. The
first stage estimates regional compressibility from the feature distortion incurred
when merging a region to construct a quadtree-derived density map, which guides
the dropping, merging, and retention of visual regions. Based on the surviving
units and inherited tree topology, the second stage introduces an efficient tree-
structured pruning mechanism in the middle layers of the LLM to mitigate At-
tention Drift and further reduce low-relevance visual content using text-to-vision
attention. We validate FoveaTree under different Video LLM architectures, i.e.,
InternVL3.5-8B and Qwen3-VL-8B, on five video question-answering bench-
marks and one captioning benchmark with token budgets of 50%, 30%, and 10%;
our method consistently achieves overall state-of-the-art performance across all
evaluated settings, demonstrating a superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off.1

INTRODUCTION
Recent video large language models (Video LLMs) (Wang et al., 2025; Bai et al., 2025a) exhibit
strong video understanding, delivering competitive performance on tasks such as video question
answering and video captioning (Li et al., 2025; Bai et al., 2025b). A Video LLM contains a vision
encoder, a modality projector, and an LLM (Liu et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024c);
the encoder represents each frame as patch tokens, which the projector maps into the language
embedding space (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021; Radford et al., 2021; Zhai et al., 2023; Oquab et al.,
2024; He et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2021). Since the visual sequence grows with video length and
resolution (Wang et al., 2024a; Xu et al., 2024), it inflates attention computation, prefill latency, and
key-value cache memory, motivating visual-token pruning for practical inference (Tu et al., 2025).

which we term Hierarchy-blind Pruning. Fig. 2 illustrates this limitation with FrameFusion, a
representative similarity-based method. FrameFusion computes cosine similarity only between spa-
tially corresponding tokens in adjacent frames and performs cascaded merging, but it neither esti-
mates within-frame regional compressibility nor constructs a nested spatial hierarchy.
Fig. 3 visualizes query-to-vision attention scores. Attention Drift appears as anomalously high
responses at spatial boundaries or early visual-sequence positions despite weak query relevance,
reflecting RoPE positional effects, nearby or recent-token preferences, and attention sinks (Su et al.,
2024; Xiao et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2025a; Zhang et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2024b). Flat top-k methods
such as LearnPruner follow these peaks; as retention falls from 50% to 30%, query-relevant regions
become increasingly fragmented or lost, whereas ATP uses the inherited tree to preserve relevant
regions together with their context, keeping the surrounding visual evidence intact during pruning.
Motivated by these observations, we propose FoveaTree, a two-stage, training-free visual-token
pruning framework. Merge-Gain Density Estimation (MGDE) builds a quadtree for each frame,
estimates compressibility from merge-induced feature distortion, labels terminal regions by density,
and performs drop, merge, and keep operations. Attention Tree Pruning (ATP) then uses middle-
layer text-to-vision attention to prune the surviving hierarchy to the target budget per frame.
Together, the two stages preserve core regions more precisely, address the challenges above, and
maintain spatial context during query-guided pruning. We validate FoveaTree under different Video
LLM architectures, InternVL3.5-8B and Qwen3-VL-8B, across five video question-answering
benchmarks and one captioning benchmark at 50%, 30%, and 10% visual-token retention. Across
all evaluated settings, FoveaTree achieves overall state-of-the-art performance and demonstrates a
superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off across both backbones and all retention budgets.
In summary, our main contributions are as follows:
• We propose FoveaTree, a two-stage, training-free visual-token pruning framework for ef-
ficient Video LLM inference. By combining compressibility-guided spatial pruning with
query-guided tree pruning within the LLM, FoveaTree efficiently preserves task-relevant
visual evidence at low visual-token retention without additional training or labels.
• We introduce MGDE, which builds a multiscale hierarchy and density map from merge dis-
tortion and gain, then adapts pruning to regional compressibility by dropping, merging, or
retaining tokens. We also introduce ATP to propagate attention scores through the inherited
quadtree, improving robustness to Attention Drift and preserving task-relevant regions.
• We conduct experiments on five video question-answering benchmarks and one captioning
benchmark to evaluate FoveaTree across different Video LLM architectures, while compo-
nent ablations on InternVL3.5-8B assess each proposed module. Notably, at 30% visual-
token retention on InternVL3.5-8B, FoveaTree achieves 100.62% performance of the full
model, with a 1.24× inference speedup and 53.2% lower TFLOPs.

2.3
MERGE-GAIN DENSITY ESTIMATION
Motivation.
As discussed above, similarity-based pruning can merge or discard tokens from infor-
mative regions because it does not explicitly represent the spatial hierarchy of a frame, and learned
selectors require training and repeated model evaluation. MGDE provides a training-free alternative
by measuring the feature distortion caused by regional merging. It uses this signal to construct a
multiscale partition, then derives a density map that assigns finer or coarser token resolution accord-
ing to the terminal region size before the visual tokens enter the language decoder for prefill.

Motivation.
After MGDE, the surviving units retain a spa-
tial hierarchy but can still contain content unrelated to the
query. To examine how attention-based pruning distributes
its selections, we run LearnPruner and ATP on Video-MME at
the same visual-token retention ratio using the original query
associated with each sample, then count how often the token
at each spatial position is retained across frames. Fig. 7 re-
ports position-wise selection frequencies and heatmaps. Un-
der Attention Drift, LearnPruner’s flat attention top-k tends to
retain peripheral tokens, producing scattered peaks that can
fragment relevant visual evidence. In contrast, ATP propa-
gates the same attention scores through the inherited tree and
selects spatially coherent branches, shifting retention toward
central regions where the primary visual content of a frame
is more frequently located. In this way, the tree structure lets
ATP evaluate each branch within its multiscale spatial con-
text, which is cheaper and more robust during pruning.

Implementation Details.
We evaluate InternVL3.5-8B and Qwen3-VL-8B with identical in-

uses layer-0 features, a root side length of 8, a per-frame leaf cap, and a merge-gain threshold of
0.005. The proposed ATP module is inserted at decoder layer 18. We run batch-one bfloat16 in-
ference with SDPA and greedy decoding on one NVIDIA A800 80 GB GPU, synchronize CUDA
timing, and exclude warm-up. Efficiency is measured on Video-MME with at most 64 frames. Exact
retained-token counts and results on additional backbones are provided in the Appendix, together
with full implementation details for all evaluated models, retention configurations, and benchmarks.

3.3
EFFICIENCY ANALYSIS
To measure the efficiency of FoveaTree, we record total inference time, peak memory, latency,
and TFLOPs on Video-MME together with Video-MME accuracy from the complete benchmark
evaluation, as reported in Table 3. At 30% retention on InternVL3.5-8B, FoveaTree reduces TFLOPs
by 53.2% relative to Full (322.0 to 150.8) with a 1.24× latency speedup (2.484 s to 2.006 s), while
peak memory stays within 4.4% of the unpruned model (20.26 to 19.36 GiB). Relative to the earlier
pruning methods (shown in gray), FoveaTree leads in Video-MME accuracy by a large margin;
relative to the more recent and stronger baselines, it attains the lowest inference time at both budgets
while remaining the most accurate, demonstrating the superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off.

ATP strategy.
Table 7 compares ATP with flat Top-k and debiased variants at 30% retention,
reporting both Avg. and latency. Following representation-difference calibration (Liu et al., 2024c;

4
CONCLUSION
We presented FoveaTree, a training-free framework motivated by Hierarchy-blind Pruning and At-
tention Drift in Video LLMs. MGDE reduces redundant visual units through a compressibility-
derived spatial hierarchy, while ATP propagates query-conditioned attention through the surviving
tree. Experiments on two Video LLMs and six benchmarks at three retention budgets demonstrate
stronger performance retention with competitive efficiency, establishing regional hierarchy as a prac-

tical bridge between query-independent compression and query-aware selection. The framework is
more complex than basic pruning methods and lacks explicit temporal processing; future work will
simplify the pipeline and extend the hierarchy across time to model motion and event dynamics.
ETHICS STATEMENT
This work does not involve human participants, human subjects, personally identifiable information,
or the collection of new personal data. We evaluate on publicly released video benchmarks, includ-
ing Video-MME, NExT-QA, MVBench, Valor32k, VNBench, and VDC, following their intended
evaluation protocols and applicable usage terms. The proposed method is an inference-time visual-
token pruning framework and does not generate new training data or modify the parameters of the
evaluated Video LLMs. As with other video-language systems, the evaluated models may inherit
biases, hallucinations, and errors from their underlying data and pretrained models. Token pruning
may additionally remove visual evidence in difficult cases, particularly for fine-grained, temporal,
or safety-critical content. We therefore view FoveaTree as an efficiency technique rather than a
substitute for human oversight in high-stakes applications.
REPRODUCIBILITY STATEMENT
We evaluate FoveaTree on four publicly available Video LLM backbones, six public video bench-
marks, and three visual-token retention budgets. The paper and supplementary material describe the
MGDE and ATP algorithms, regional distortion and attention-score formulations, quadtree construc-
tion, budget resolution procedure, model configurations, benchmark protocols, decoding settings,
hardware, timing methodology, and token-budget audits. The supplementary material also provides
additional backbone results, component and layer ablations, efficiency analyses, and implementa-
tion details for the compared baselines. Our code, configuration files, and evaluation scripts will be
released in the final version of the paper to ensure that all reported results can be reproduced.

MORE EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS
A.1
ADDITIONAL EXPERIMENTS ON QWEN2.5-VL
The main paper evaluates InternVL3.5-8B and Qwen3-VL-8B. To examine generality beyond these
two backbones, this subsection applies the same evaluation protocol to Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Table 8 re-
ports the full seven-metric protocol at 50%, 30%, and 10% retention budgets, following the identical
frame sampling and decoding configuration. These results quantify how each pruning method be-
haves across the QA benchmarks and the captioning score under each budget. Qwen2.5-VL-7B (Bai
et al., 2025b) uses a native dynamic-resolution vision transformer with window attention and 2D-
RoPE. Its spatial token count varies with input resolution, and MGDE and ATP operate on each
temporal unit’s native grid.
A.2
ADDITIONAL EXPERIMENTS ON LLAVA-ONEVISION
To examine generality beyond the two backbones used in the main comparison, we apply the same
evaluation protocol to LLaVA-OneVision-7B (Li et al., 2025). Table 9 reports the complete seven-
metric protocol at 50%, 30%, and 10% retention. Frame sampling follows the model’s official video
configuration, and all methods share identical inputs and decoding settings. FoveaTree maintains
competitive performance across all three retention budgets, with particularly strong results on the
Video QA benchmarks at 10% retention. Unlike the two main backbones, LLaVA-OneVision-7B
pairs a SigLIP-SO400M vision encoder with a Qwen2-7B decoder and encodes each sampled frame
(one native temporal unit) into a fixed low-resolution grid of 196 tokens, so the visual sequence
is uniform across units rather than resolution-adaptive. MGDE uses the same quadtree construc-
tion rule on each unit, and ATP is inserted at decoder layer 18, transferring the protocol without
architectural modification.

EVALUATION BENCHMARKS
We use the official test splits, subject to the subset specifications below.
Video-MME.
Video-MME (Fu et al., 2025a) evaluates video understanding across short, medium,
and long videos. Its questions cover perception and reasoning in diverse content domains. We eval-
uate without subtitles, report answer accuracy, and use the dataset for efficiency measurements with
a 64-frame cap. The fine-grained analysis examines OCR, counting, and object-related questions,
which place different demands on the retained visual content.
NExT-QA.
NExT-QA (Xiao et al., 2021) includes descriptive, temporal, and causal questions
about video events. In addition to recognizing the visible content, the model must connect actions
and explain their temporal or causal relationships. We report question-answering accuracy to assess
whether token pruning preserves the evidence required for these judgments.
MVBench.
MVBench (Li et al., 2024b) organizes video understanding into multiple tasks in-
volving actions, objects, spatial relationships, and temporal changes. Its diversity complements the
broad evaluation in Video-MME. We use the benchmark’s accuracy metric and retain the original
evaluation protocol when comparing the full model and compressed models. The official release
contains 4,000 samples, but 200 samples of the Fine-grained Pose task are missing from the version
we evaluate, so all reported numbers use the remaining 3,800 samples. Every method, including the
uncompressed reference, is evaluated on the identical 3,800-sample subset, ensuring a fair compari-
son.
Valor32k.
The VALOR dataset (Liu et al., 2025) supports vision, audio, and language understand-
ing. We evaluate on the visual subset of Valor32k using the visual question-answering setting. This
setting measures the model’s ability to answer questions using video content and complements the
action-oriented evaluation in NExT-QA and MVBench.
VNBench.
VNBench (Zhao et al., 2025b) evaluates retrieval, ordering, and counting through the
Video Needle In A Haystack construction. Relevant visual events are embedded within longer se-
quences, making the benchmark useful for assessing temporal localization and the retention of sparse
evidence. We report accuracy under the same input configuration for the full and compressed mod-
els.
VDC.
VDC, introduced with AuroraCap (Chai et al., 2025), evaluates detailed video captioning
using structured reference descriptions. We follow the official VDC QA evaluation pipeline, us-
ing Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct for semantic yes/no judgments. We report Acc. and Score, denoted
V DCAcc. and V DCScore, respectively. Detailed captioning requires the model to describe several
aspects of the scene within one response and provides a complementary evaluation to video question
answering. Subsequent work such as VDC-Agent (Wang et al., 2026) also uses this benchmark to
study detailed caption generation.

When Mt > Bt, ATP applies a unit-local budget resolver. (i) The
frontier is initialized with the unit-level routing node, to which the surviving roots of the restricted

Visual representations and video LLMs.
Vision Transformers (Dosovitskiy et al., 2021) and hi-
erarchical variants such as Swin (Liu et al., 2021) encode images as patch sequences; CLIP and
SigLIP (Radford et al., 2021; Zhai et al., 2023) use language supervision; MAE, DINOv2, and

2, Flamingo, and LLaVA (Li et al., 2022; 2023; Alayrac et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023; 2024a) con-
nect visual encoders to language models. Video-LLaVA, Video-ChatGPT, Chat-UniVi, TimeChat,
LLaMA-VID, and InternVideo2 (Lin et al., 2024; Maaz et al., 2024; Jin et al., 2024; Ren et al., 2024;

image resolutions and aspect ratios; and earlier InternVL releases and ShareGPT4V (Chen et al.,

Qwen3-VL-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and LLaVA-OneVision-7B (Wang et al., 2025; Bai et al., 2025a;b;
Li et al., 2025).
Token selection and compression.
TokenLearner learns compact visual tokens (Ryoo et al.,
2021), DynamicViT and ATS adaptively select tokens (Rao et al., 2021; Fayyaz et al., 2022), and
EViT and Token Pooling consolidate redundant information (Liang et al., 2022; Marin et al., 2023).
In VLMs, LLaVA-PruMerge combines selection and merging (Shang et al., 2025), HiRED allocates
image-partition budgets (Arif et al., 2025), and VisPruner balances saliency and diversity (Zhang
et al., 2025a); ZipVL performs dynamic token sparsification (He et al., 2024), and FocusLLaVA
and DeCo study coarse-to-fine or decoupled compression (Zhu et al., 2024; Yao et al., 2024). Pyra-
midDrop, SparseVLM, and ATP-LLaVA prune across decoder layers (Xing et al., 2025; Zhang
et al., 2025b; Ye et al., 2025c); MADTP uses multimodal alignment (Cao et al., 2024); and DART,
FEATHER, HoloV, and MustDrop target duplicate features, spatial coverage, holistic context, and
training-free reduction (Wen et al., 2025; Endo et al., 2025; Zou et al., 2025; Han et al., 2026). Our
baselines include ToMe’s similarity merging (Bolya et al., 2023), FastV’s decoder attention prun-
ing (Chen et al., 2024a), VisionZip’s dominant and contextual selection (Yang et al., 2025), Frame-
Fusion’s combined similarity and importance (Fu et al., 2025b), STTM’s multi-granular spatiotem-
poral merging (Hyun et al., 2025), FlashVID’s attention- and diversity-based tree merging (Fan et al.,
2026), and LearnPruner’s learned module with decoder attention pruning (Takezoe et al., 2026). At-
tention sinks (Xiao et al., 2024) and position-sensitive retrieval (Liu et al., 2024b) are documented
in LLMs; VLM debiasing methods address biased token importance (Zhao et al., 2025a; Zhang
et al., 2026). FoveaTree reuses distortion-based spatial quadtrees for query-guided pruning without
training.
Related analyses cover visual shortcomings and internal processing in multimodal models (Tong
et al., 2024; Neo et al., 2025), high-resolution processing (Li et al., 2026), and the Transformer
attention underlying decoder-side selection (Vaswani et al., 2017).
