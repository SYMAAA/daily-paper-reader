ABSTRACT 

Ultra-low-bit scalar quantization offers a regular arithmetic structure for effi-cient deployment of large language models, but preserving their predictive be-havior at extreme precision remains challenging. With only a few reconstruc-tion levels, accurate weight representation must translate into consistent model predictions using a small set of calibration samples. We propose FidelityQ , a scalar quantization framework for preserving predictive fidelity at weight preci-sions down to 2 bits. Specifically, we introduce Information-Aligned Analytic Coding (IAC ) for accurate quantization. IAC shapes each weight row toward a centered symmetric source through randomized Hadamard mixing, then fits a mirrored four-level minimum-distortion quantizer to its empirical weights. Ev-ery level set in this symmetry-aligned family decomposes exactly into 2 sign-valued bases with associated scales, combining source-adaptive reconstruction with lookup-free decoding compatible with scaled integer arithmetic. Our analy-sis further reveals that residual predictive discrepancies are heavy-tailed and con-centrate on tokens that the teacher predicts confidently, motivating Knowledge-Intensive Calibration (KIC ) for consistent quantization. KIC ranks sequences using the tail of token-wise teacher-student divergence and allocates a limited cal-ibration budget to the highest-scoring samples. It then restores predictive agree-ment by updating only per-row reconstruction levels under teacher supervision, leaving the pretrained weights frozen. Experiments on the LLaMA and Qwen families demonstrate the effectiveness of FidelityQ across weight-only and joint quantization settings. On LLaMA-2-7B, FidelityQ achieves a WikiText2 perplex-ity of 6.62 at W2A16 and improves the prior state-of-the-art W2A4KV4 result from 8.31 to 6.96, while reducing linear-layer weight memory by 8.0 × relative to FP16. These results support accurate LLM deployment with structured ultra-low-bit quantization. Our code is publicly available at https://github.com/ AI-Efficiency/FidelityQ .

1 INTRODUCTION 

Large language models (LLMs) support diverse applications, from code generation to embodied planning (Rozi` ere et al., 2023; Driess et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2026b), but their deployment re-mains constrained by the memory required to store and access their parameters (Liu et al., 2024b; Alizadeh et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2026a). Quantization reduces this burden by representing model parameters and intermediate tensors at lower numerical precision (Frantar et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024; Shao et al., 2024). Reducing weight precision to 2 bits offers substantial storage savings, while scalar representations with simple scaling operations preserve a regular arithmetic structure for deployment under tight memory budgets. However, each scalar weight then has only 4 possi-ble reconstruction values. Preserving a pretrained LLM’s predictive fidelity under this constraint requires accurate reconstruction of its weights.

Existing quantization methods improve reconstruction through different representations and opti-mization strategies. Scalar methods compensate quantization errors or adapt channel scales while retaining simple decoding operations (Frantar et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024; Shao et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024). Nevertheless, a fixed or restricted set of reconstruction values can remain poorly matched to the weight distribution at 2 bits. Codebook methods offer richer representations and competitive accuracy (Tseng et al., 2024; Egiazarian et al., 2024), but require specialized decod-ing to translate their representation advantages into efficient execution. Calibration and distillation can further recover predictive quality by optimizing quantization parameters or aligning model out-puts (Shao et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024; 2025). For example, on LLaMA-2-7B, group-wise round-to-nearest quantization increases WikiText2 perplexity from 5.47 to 431.97, whereas the published DB-LLM and QuIP# results reduce it to 7.23 and 6.66, respectively (Chen et al., 2024; 2025). We propose FidelityQ , a scalar quantization framework for preserving predictive fidelity at weight precisions down to 2 bits (fig. 2). Building on classical fixed-rate scalar quantization (Lloyd, 1982; Max, 1960), we view quantization as source coding under a fixed bit budget and analyze how re-construction accuracy relates to the structure of the code. Our analysis shows that the four-level minimum-distortion solution for the centered Gaussian reference lies in a mirrored family whose re-construction values admit an exact expression in 2 sign-valued bases and their scales. This connects source-adaptive level placement with lookup-free decoding compatible with integer arithmetic. We further consider how the remaining approximation affects model predictions. Weight reconstruction error alone does not identify the inputs on which the quantized model departs from its full-precision counterpart, motivating calibration data chosen directly from their predictive disagreement. Specifically, we introduce Information-Aligned Analytic Coding (IAC ) for accurate quantization. IAC first applies randomized Hadamard mixing, following rotation-based quantization (Ashkboos et al., 2024; Tseng et al., 2024), to obtain centered, approximately symmetric, near-Gaussian weight marginals. Guided by this source geometry, it restricts the 4 reconstruction levels to two mirrored pairs and determines their magnitudes by minimizing empirical squared error once for each trans-formed row (Lloyd, 1982; Max, 1960). Every such mirrored level set is represented exactly by 2 sign-valued bases and their amplitudes, allowing decoding through 2 scaled dot-product paths without per-element lookup. With quantized activations, the dot products use integer arithmetic while the amplitudes supply the reconstruction scales. Building on this representation, we introduce 

Knowledge-Intensive Calibration (KIC ) for consistent quantization. KIC measures token-wise di-vergence between the full-precision teacher and the quantized model, aggregates the largest diver-gences within each sequence, and selects the highest-scoring sequences under a fixed calibration budget. The selected sequences concentrate calibration on predictions that require closer agreement with the teacher. Teacher-guided calibration then updates only the per-row reconstruction levels, 2with the pretrained weights frozen. This procedure connects accurate weight reconstruction with targeted calibration, preserving predictive fidelity within a regular, lookup-free arithmetic structure. We evaluate FidelityQ on the LLaMA and Qwen families under weight-only and joint quantization settings. On LLaMA-2-7B at W2A16, FidelityQ achieves a WikiText2 perplexity of 6.62, compared with 8.89 for DB-LLM (Chen et al., 2024) at matched per-row granularity and 6.66 for QuIP# under the 2048-token reevaluation of Chen et al. (2025). At W2A4KV4 it achieves 6.96, compared with the reported 8.31 of RCP (Choi et al., 2025), while retaining lookup-free decoding. On an RTX A6000 at batch size 1, its linear stack takes 5.1 ms per token against 9.9 ms for AQLM, and the full model reaches 47.0 tokens per second in eager mode against 35.9 for FP16 (fig. 1). These results demonstrate strong predictive performance with 2-bit scalar quantization. 

2 RELATED WORK 

Post-training quantization. GPTQ and its extensions reduce reconstruction error through sequen-tial compensation (Frantar et al., 2023; Li et al., 2025; 2026). AWQ and OmniQuant adapt channel scales or equivalent transformations to improve scalar quantization (Lin et al., 2024; Shao et al., 2024). Rotation-based methods further reshape the source: QuaRot uses fixed Hadamard rotations, while SpinQuant and FlatQuant optimize rotations or more general transformations (Ashkboos et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2025). FidelityQ builds on source shaping and error compensation, keeps pretrained weights frozen during calibration, and adapts scalar reconstruction levels to the transformed weight distribution. 

Low-bit representations and decoding. Vector and codebook quantizers provide strong accuracy references at two bits. QuIP# uses a lattice codebook, while AQLM and VPTQ learn codebooks for weight reconstruction (Tseng et al., 2024; Egiazarian et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024a). Their indexed representations require decoding alongside matrix multiplication. Scalar alternatives retain arithmetic structure: PB-LLM leaves salient weights at higher precision and BiLLM binarizes them residually, while DB-LLM reconstructs two-bit weights through two binary branches (Yuan & Dong, 2024; Huang et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024). Classical scalar quantization determines reconstruction values by minimizing distortion (Lloyd, 1982; Max, 1960; Gray & Neuhoff, 1998), which Qift applies to rotated two-bit weights as one mirrored no-zero level set shared by all rows (Huang & Tsai, 2026). IAC instead fits the levels per transformed row and expresses them exactly through two sign planes, connecting source adaptation to regular arithmetic decoding. 

Calibration and data selection. Quantization parameters can be refined through end-to-end opti-mization, as in EfficientQAT’s step-size updates, or teacher supervision, as in BitDistiller and DB-LLM (Chen et al., 2025; Du et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024). Calibration data also affect compres-sion quality (Williams & Aletras, 2024); self-calibration obtains such data from model-generated text (Williams et al., 2025). Broader selection methods target pretraining distributions or down-stream capabilities (Xie et al., 2023; Xia et al., 2024). More closely related to compression recov-ery, PASER allocates instruction data according to capability degradation after pruning (He et al., 2026). KIC selects calibration sequences using the tail of token-wise disagreement between the full-precision and quantized models, then refines only per-row reconstruction levels under teacher supervision. Its selection criterion directly measures the predictive changes caused by quantization.

It ranks complete sequences by residual teacher-student disagreement, then refines only the quantization parameters on the selected sequences. Teacher-student divergence measures the prediction change caused by quantization, which local reconstruction MSE cannot locate after errors propagate through the model. Teacher entropy instead measures uncertainty in the original prediction and can be high even when quantization leaves that prediction unchanged. Figure 4 shows what selecting on this score buys: on the worst 1% of held-out tokens by pre-calibration divergence, tail perplexity falls from 96.7 to 79.8 against a random draw of the same size, while the two arms stay within 0.08% of each other on the corpus as a whole. Because a sequence may contain only a few strongly altered positions, the tail mean prevents their damage from being diluted by positions near agreement and reduces dependence on a single extreme value. This upper-tail average follows the conditional value-at-risk criterion (Rockafellar & Uryasev, 2000). Selecting the top k sequences, therefore, maximizes the sum of sequence scores under the calibration budget. KIC therefore concentrates a small calibration budget on the residual errors that most alter model predictions. Scoring requires one offline forward pass of each model over the candidate pool and adds no inference-time cost. On one shared LLaMA-2-7B pool, rankings select sets with 0.80 to 0.90 overlap across A16KV16, A4KV8, and A4KV4, compared with 0.125 for random selection. 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 SETUP 

Quantization. We evaluate weight-only quantization at W2A16KV16 and joint weight, activation, and KV-cache quantization at W2A4KV4, where W-A-KV denotes the three bit widths. FidelityQ quantizes weights to two bits with per-row reconstruction levels, while embeddings and the out-put head remain in FP16. The downstream checkpoints use 4096 KIC-selected sequences, except LLaMA-2-13B, which uses 1024. Each FidelityQ row comes from one calibration run on a mixed-domain pool. Calibration settings and implementation details are provided in Appendix B. 

Models and baselines. We evaluate LLaMA-2-7B/13B (Touvron et al., 2023b), LLaMA-3-8B (Grattafiori et al., 2024), Qwen3-8B-Base (Qwen Team, 2025), and LLaMA-30B (Touvron et al., 2023a). LLaMA-3-8B results are provided in Appendix E. Our primary controlled baseline, Base, uses randomized rotation, error compensation, and a uniform grid without finetuning. At matched W-A-KV configurations, we also compare with QuaRot (Ashkboos et al., 2024), ResComp (Li et al., 2026), and DB-LLM (Chen et al., 2024) as scalar baselines, and AQLM (Egiazarian et al., 2024) as a codebook accuracy reference. Additional comparisons are in Appendix E. 

Evaluation. We measure perplexity on WikiText2 (Merity et al., 2017) and the segment-sampled C4 (Raffel et al., 2020) validation split with a context length of 2048. Downstream evaluation includes zero-shot accuracy on PiQA (Bisk et al., 2020), ARC-e and ARC-c (Clark et al., 2018), HellaSwag (Zellers et al., 2019), WinoGrande (Sakaguchi et al., 2020), BoolQ (Clark et al., 2019), and OpenBookQA (Mihaylov et al., 2018), together with LAMBADA (Paperno et al., 2016) and 5-shot MMLU (Hendrycks et al., 2021). We report the seven-task average separately from LAMBADA and MMLU and use the LM Evaluation Harness (Gao et al., 2024) for task evaluation. 4.2 QUANTIZATION ACCURACY

For LLaMA-2-7B, FidelityQ reduces linear-weight storage by 8.0 × relative to FP16. At batch size 1 on an RTX A6000, its W2A16 linear stack decodes in 5.11 ms/token, against 8.83 ms for QuIP# E8P and 9.93 ms for AQLM 2 × 8 at matched two-bit storage, and 4.72 ms for the uniform grid whose perplexity is 9.56 rather than 6.62 (fig. 1 and table 8). Full-model decode throughput in HuggingFace eager mode is 47.0 tokens/s, versus 35.9 for FP16 (table 9). Appendix D gives per-method timings and measurement details. 

5 DISCUSSION 

At a fixed W2A4KV4 budget, further gains in predictive fidelity may come from jointly optimizing source shaping, reconstruction levels, and discrete assignments. Quantization loss could guide the source transform, while prediction feedback could refine both levels and assignments. Accounting for interactions among weight, activation, and cache errors as they propagate through layers and attention offers another direction for reducing loss within the same compact representation. Realizing the deployment benefits of W2A4KV4 also requires kernels adapted to its mixed pre-cisions. The evaluated GPU lacks native INT2-by-INT4 matrix multiplication; our current fused kernel accelerates two-bit weights with 16-bit activations at batch size one. Integrating sign-plane computation with four-bit activations and cache access, and extending fusion to prefill and larger batches, are directions for translating joint quantization into broader inference gains. 96 CONCLUSION 

FidelityQ preserves predictive fidelity at two bits by combining source-adaptive scalar reconstruc-tion with targeted calibration. IAC first shapes the weight distribution, then places reconstruction levels to minimize distortion, preserving the resulting level set through exact arithmetic decoding. KIC uses residual teacher-student disagreement to select calibration sequences and refines only the two reconstruction magnitudes per row. Experiments on LLaMA and Qwen show improved accu-racy over matched scalar baselines in weight-only and joint quantization. The fused implementation also realizes storage savings and faster decoding, demonstrating that source-adaptive reconstruction can retain the execution advantages of scalar quantization. 10 REFERENCES

D ARITHMETIC DECODING AND DEPLOYMENT 

D.1 FUSED ARITHMETIC DECODING 

At batch size 1, a persistent CUDA kernel computes the two sign-plane reductions and combines their row amplitudes: 

y = diag( a1)s1x + diag( a2)s2x.

Packed signs act directly on fp16 activation pairs through XOR before accumulation. This avoids per-weight conversion, indexed reconstruction lookup, and materialization of the decoded weight matrix. Activations are staged in shared memory, and pairwise fp16 accumulators are periodically accumulated into fp32. In a CUDA-event comparison of linear kernels, this implementation takes 5.18 ms/token versus 8.29 for our generic Triton path, a 1.60 × speedup for the same representation. D.2 MEASUREMENT PROTOCOL
