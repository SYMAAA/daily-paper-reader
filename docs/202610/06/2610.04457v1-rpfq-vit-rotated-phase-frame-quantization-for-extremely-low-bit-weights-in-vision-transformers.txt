# RPFQ-ViT: Rotated Phase-Frame Quantization for Extremely Low-Bit Weights in Vision Transformers 

Mengyuan Fan Bokai Huang JiaMing Pan Xiaokun Yuan Peizhuang Cong Zhewen Tan Tong Yang ∗

Peking University 

## Abstract 

Vision Transformers (ViTs) achieve strong performance on image recognition and mobile vision applications, but their high-dimensional linear projections and atten-tion computations still impose substantial storage and inference costs. Extremely low-bit quantization is a promising solution, yet ViTs often suffer severe accuracy degradation because conventional real-valued scalar codebooks are poorly matched to the directional geometry of Transformer projections. We present RPFQ-ViT, a Rotated Phase-Frame Quantization method that quantizes paired channels in two-dimensional phase planes, enabling low-bit codes to better preserve projec-tion directions while recovering magnitude with lightweight scaling. RPFQ-ViT serves as a drop-in QAT replacement for nn.Linear and does not modify the standard real-valued attention, normalization, or activation computation graph. On ImageNet-1K, RPFQ-ViT-B/16 reaches 79.33% Top-1 / 94.48% Top-5 under W2/A4, Swin-T reaches 79.30% Top-1 / 94.79% Top-5 under W2/A8, and DeiT-S reaches 77.41% Top-1 / 93.11% Top-5 under W2/A8. Ablations, phase-geometry analysis, and direction-preservation metrics show that channel pairing, learnable rotation, phase-anchor learning, and residual phase refinement each improve quan-tization quality. We further deploy RPFQ-ViT image-classification models on native iOS and Android runtime stacks; with 2-bit packed weights, model size shrinks by roughly 5.4–7.1× relative to FP32 and end-to-end on-device latency drops by 1.4–1.6×. All ImageNet results trained in our codebase use a matched 300-epoch recipe and are reported as mean accuracies over three independent runs. These results show that RPFQ-ViT provides a favorable trade-off among accuracy, compression, and practical mobile deployment for extremely low-bit ViTs. 

## 1 Introduction 

Vision Transformers, including ViT [Dosovitskiy et al., 2021], DeiT [Touvron et al., 2021], and Swin [Liu et al., 2021], achieve strong vision accuracy, but their attention and MLP projections remain expensive to store and run on resource-constrained devices. Extremely low-bit quantization is an appealing remedy, yet ViTs still degrade sharply at 4, 3, and especially 2 bits [Li et al., 2022, Liu et al., 2023, Huang et al., 2023]. Prior work has mainly traced this difficulty to attention distortion and unstable QAT dynamics, showing that low-bit errors in ViTs propagate through attention scores, token mixing, and deep residual blocks more severely than in CNNs. We take a complementary view: the problem is also geometric. Standard binary or ternary quantizers operate on each scalar independently, but ViT projections are highly sensitive to direction because attention depends directly on query-key inner products. At very low precision, scalar codebooks can therefore preserve magnitude poorly and distort the directions that matter most.

Our key observation is that many ViT linear layers have even channel dimension, which lets us introduce a simple deterministic half-split pairing without changing tensor shapes or the real-valued computation graph. Each pair defines a 2D phase plane, so a 2-bit codebook can represent four directions instead of a few scalar levels on the real line. This leads to a ViT-specific view of extremely low-bit quantization as direction preservation with lightweight magnitude recovery .Based on this formulation, we propose RPFQ-ViT , a rotated phase-frame quantizer for ViTs. RPFQ rewrites paired channels in an equivalent complex form, learns a group-wise rotated phase frame, and combines learnable anchors, least-squares scale fitting, and residual phase refinement in a drop-in QAT replacement for nn.Linear . The rest of the ViT graph remains unchanged. The contributions of this paper are as follows: 1. A ViT-tailored paired-phase formulation. We introduce a deterministic half-split pairing and an exact paired-complex reparameterization for even-dimensional ViT linear layers while preserving the original real-valued operator graph. 2. An adaptive rotated phase-frame quantizer. We combine group-wise rotation, learnable phase anchors, least-squares magnitude fitting, and residual phase refinement to adapt a compact phase codebook to layer- and head-specific ViT geometry. 3. Drop-in compatibility with standard ViT inference. RPFQ-ViT replaces nn.Linear 

during QAT while leaving attention, normalization, activations, and deployment toolchains unchanged. 4. Strong low-bit accuracy and deployment results. Across ViT-B/16, DeiT-S, and Swin-T on ImageNet-1K, plus ablations, geometry analysis, training-efficiency profiling, and mobile deployment, RPFQ yields a strong accuracy–compression–deployability trade-off in the 2-bit-weight regime. For reviewer convenience, an anonymized repository with code, configs, and reproduction scripts is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/RPFQ-neurips2026 .

## 2 Related Work 

We briefly review two lines of work most relevant for situating our design choices: low-bit quantization for vision Transformers and broader geometry-aware representations for low-bit models. 

2.1 Vision Transformer Quantization 

Early ViT quantization methods largely inherit CNN-oriented QAT techniques, such as LSQ [Esser et al., 2020], LSQ+ [Bhalgat et al., 2020], and PACT [Choi et al., 2018]. However, interactions among query, key, and value, the long-tailed statistics around LayerNorm, and the large expansion ratios of MLP layers make ViTs more sensitive to low-bit perturbations than CNNs. Q-ViT [Li et al., 2022] systematically studies attention-map distortion in fully quantized ViTs and introduces IRM and DGD to mitigate it. OFQ [Liu et al., 2023] instead emphasizes training dynamics, arguing that learnable scales can exacerbate weight oscillation and proposing StatsQ, CGA, and QKR. Variation-aware ViT Quantization [Huang et al., 2023] further analyzes low-bit ViT training from the perspectives of variation, outliers, and oscillation, and provides systematic comparisons among LSQ+, LSQ++KD, and its own method on DeiT-S and Swin-T. Broadly, these methods improve stability and attention fidelity within a real-valued quantization pipeline; by contrast, our work focuses on the geometry of the codebook itself and treats two-dimensional phase directions as the basic representation unit of extremely low-bit ViT weights. For empirical comparison, we prioritize baselines that are simultaneously QAT-based, targeted at ultra-low-bit ImageNet classification, and reported on standard ViT/DeiT/Swin backbones with enough public detail to support reproduction. Under this criterion, Q-ViT and OFQ remain the most directly comparable archival baselines for our W2 experiments. Recent published ViT quantization work has broadened the landscape, but much of it emphasizes PTQ or higher-bit settings rather than public 2-bit QAT configurations on ViT-B/16, DeiT-S, and Swin-T. For example, IGQ-ViT, FIMA-Q, and APHQ-ViT are PTQ methods for ViTs, APHQ-ViT explicitly focuses on 3-bit and 4-bit results, and GPLQ targets practical 4-bit quantization rather than a matched W2 QAT comparison [Moon 2et al., 2024, Wu et al., 2025a,b, Liang et al., 2025]. We therefore use Q-ViT and OFQ as the main direct baselines, while treating newer PTQ or higher-bit methods as adjacent context rather than identical-comparison settings. Although our focus is QAT, post-training quantization (PTQ) for ViTs offers a complementary view of the same distributional challenges. PTQ4ViT introduces twin uniform quantization and a Hessian-guided metric to handle the non-Gaussian activation distributions after Softmax and GELU [Yuan et al., 2022]. FQ-ViT identifies severe inter-channel variation in LayerNorm inputs and non-uniform attention-map distributions, and proposes Power-of-Two Factor and Log-Int-Softmax for fully quan-tized ViTs [Lin et al., 2021]. RepQ-ViT further decouples quantization from inference via scale reparameterization, with particular attention to post-LayerNorm and post-Softmax activations [Li et al., 2023]. These PTQ methods are complementary to our setting, but they reinforce the broader point that ViT quantization is shaped by structured layerwise statistics and module interactions rather than simple per-scalar clipping effects. 

2.2 Geometry-Aware Low-Bit Representations 

A smaller adjacent literature suggests that the representation space chosen before quantization can matter as much as the scalar rounding rule itself. Deep Complex Networks developed practical building blocks for end-to-end complex-valued deep networks and highlighted the usefulness of magnitude/phase-aware parameterizations [Trabelsi et al., 2018]; by contrast, RPFQ does not turn the ViT into a native complex architecture, but uses an exact paired-complex reparameterization only inside selected linear layers while leaving attention, normalization, activations, and the deployed operator graph real-valued. FrameQuant is closer in spirit because it also argues that the coordinate system before quantization matters [Adepu et al., 2024], but our setting is more specific: we target extremely low-bit ViT QAT rather than generic Transformer PTQ, and we use a deterministic half-split pairing plus learnable rotated phase frames that map exactly back to the original nn.Linear 

parameterization. In this sense, the novelty of RPFQ is not merely the use of a complex or transformed representation, but the use of a layout-preserving phase coordinate system that is tailored to ViT projections and directly compatible with standard ViT training and deployment. 

## 3 Method 

Overview. RPFQ has three steps. We first apply a simple deterministic half-split pairing to an even-dimensional ViT linear layer and rewrite the corresponding real-valued weight as an exactly equivalent widely-linear complex operator. This pairing is chosen to preserve the original tensor layout and avoid introducing extra learned matching or routing. We then quantize the resulting complex weights with a learnable rotated phase frame, fit group-wise magnitudes by least squares, and optionally apply lightweight residual phase refinement. Exact derivations of the paired complex reparameterization are deferred to Appendix A.1.

This section evaluates RPFQ-ViT from four angles: ImageNet-1K main results, ablations across training variants and weight bit-widths, training-efficiency and phase-geometry analyses, and mobile deployment. Because our focus is extremely low-bit weight quantization, we organize comparisons by effective weight bit-width rather than forcing methods with different forward quantization protocols, side metadata, and auxiliary training branches onto a single nominal quantization budget. 4.1 Experimental Setup Data, models, and training. All main experiments are conducted on ImageNet-1K [Russakovsky et al., 2015], reporting single-crop Top-1 and Top-5 accuracy at 224 × 224 resolution. We evaluate ViT-B/16, DeiT-S, and Swin-T to cover standard, data-efficient, and hierarchical vision Transformers. We use a timm-style 300-epoch AdamW recipe with cosine decay and a 5-epoch warmup; full augmentation, regularization, and reproduction details are deferred to Appendix A.2. Unless otherwise noted, every non-reported result in the main tables is the mean over three independent runs with different random seeds. For FP32, reproduced baselines, and RPFQ rows trained in our codebase, we keep the augmentation, EMA, regularization, training duration, and checkpoint-selection rule identical, and we report the final EMA checkpoint at epoch 300 rather than a single best-validation snapshot. 

Quantization protocol and baselines. By default, RPFQ replaces all even-dimensional nn.Linear

One residual phase stage corresponds to an effective storage cost of 1 bit per original real-valued weight; hence RPFQ-W2/W3/W4 denote 2/3/4-bit effective weight quantization. We compare against Q-ViT [Li et al., 2022], OFQ [Liu et al., 2023], LSQ+ [Bhalgat et al., 2020], LSQ+KD, and an FP32 baseline trained with the same recipe. For rows marked “reproduced”, we follow the released paper/repository recipe as closely as possible, including the original teacher setting, distillation loss, optimizer hyperparameters, quantization scope, and scale-initialization rule whenever these are public; Appendix A.5 summarizes the verification steps for reproduced rows that remain below the published numbers. 4.4 Training Efficiency and Memory Footprint 

Training cost is also important for extremely low-bit ViT QAT. RPFQ remains lightweight because it is a drop-in quantizer for nn.Linear and introduces no teacher branch, attention-level distillation loss, or multi-stage fine-tuning. On ViT-B/16, it completes a 300-epoch run in 46.55 hours on 8×H800-80GB, versus 160.21 projected hours for Q-ViT under the same global batch size, while also avoiding the large auxiliary memory required by DGD tensors. The full comparison table, profiling assumptions, and memory accounting are deferred to Appendix A.11. 

4.5 Phase-Geometry Analysis 

RPFQ is motivated by a simple hypothesis: at extremely low bit-widths, preserving projection direction matters more than minimizing element-wise reconstruction error alone. Real-valued scalar quantizers collapse each weight onto a one-dimensional codebook, whereas RPFQ quantizes deterministic half-split channel pairs in two-dimensional phase planes. We therefore analyze the quantized models at three levels: weight directions, attention-score perturbation, and intermediate-feature similarity. Given a full-precision weight matrix R and its quantized counterpart ˆR, we report weight MSE and angular error, where the latter directly measures directional distortion. For attention blocks, we measure score perturbation between QK ⊤ and ˆQ ˆK⊤. We also compute cosine similarity between intermediate block outputs to assess how well token representations are preserved after forward propagation. Beyond ImageNet accuracy, we ask whether RPFQ yields a practically deployable ViT representation on phones. 4.7 Experimental Summary 

Overall, RPFQ improves the accuracy-efficiency-deployability trade-off of extremely low-bit ViT quantization. On ImageNet-1K, its three-seed mean reaches 79.33%, 77.41%, and 79.30% Top-1 on ViT-B/16 W2/A4, DeiT-S W2/A8, and Swin-T W2/A8, respectively, outperforming prior real-valued low-bit baselines. Ablations identify the deterministic paired-phase parameterization, residual refinement, rotation, and anchor learning as the main contributors, while the training-efficiency, phase-geometry, and mobile studies show that these gains stem from better direction preservation without distillation-heavy training or changes to the standard ViT graph. Taken together, the results support our central claim: phase-direction codebooks provide an effective and deployment-friendly inductive bias for extremely low-bit ViT projections. 

## 5 Discussion and Limitations 

RPFQ suggests that extremely low-bit ViT quantization is limited not only by scalar approximation error, but also by a geometric mismatch between one-dimensional codebooks and Transformer projections. Pairwise phase quantization addresses this mismatch with a simple, layout-preserving change to standard nn.Linear , while learned rotation adapts the phase frame to layer- and head-specific dominant directions. This perspective is complementary to Q-ViT and OFQ, which mainly stabilize training within real-valued quantization pipelines [Li et al., 2022, Liu et al., 2023]. The present study still has clear limitations: we focus on ImageNet-1K classification, mobile latency depends on runtime support for packed weights, matched energy measurements are not yet available, and A2 activation quantization remains unstable. Even so, the results indicate that phase-direction quantization is a viable and deployment-compatible route toward extremely low-bit ViTs, while also underscoring the need for robustness, fairness, and privacy evaluation in downstream deployments. 9References

The released training configurations for the three main RPFQ checkpoints enable activation quan-tization through the public flags enable_act_quant=true and act_num_bits=4 . For the main W2/A4 checkpoints, b = 4 , so qmax = 7 and qmin = −8. This is fake quantization rather than integer-only execution: the activations are rounded to the 4-bit grid in the forward pass and immediately dequantized back to floating point, while gradients pass through with a straight-through estimator. Because the scale is recomputed over the last dimension of each input vector, this is a per-token dynamic symmetric activation quantizer, with separate scales for the paired real and imaginary halves. The activation quantizer is applied uniformly across the quantized qkv, attention-output, MLP fc1/fc2, and classifier linear layers when these layers are replaced by RPFQ modules. By contrast, patch embedding, LayerNorm, GELU, Softmax, residual adds, biases, and all non-replaced modules stay in high precision during training and evaluation. This design matches the main paper’s focus on low-bit linear projections rather than a fully integerized Transformer graph.

We exclude camera capture, image decoding, network transfer, and UI rendering, and time only the inference path from a preprocessed input tensor to the Top-1 prediction. Each run performs 50 warm-up iterations and 500 timed iterations. We report median latency (p50), tail latency (p90), and model loading time so that model-initialization effects are not conflated with steady-state inference. Model size is measured as the exported on-disk container rather than a ZIP-compressed archive. For RPFQ, the container stores 2-bit packed weight codes together with group-wise scales, rotations, anchors, biases, and the remaining non-quantized modules. Concretely, in the default K = 4 , W 2 setting, each group stores 7 metadata scalars: one rotation θg , four anchors {ϕk}4

> k=1

, and two scales (αg , β g ). Thus the metadata cost is exactly 7 scalars per group, i.e., 7bmeta bits per group or 7bmeta /(2 G) bits per original real weight for a group of G complex weights. The mobile exporters preserve the packed 2-bit weight representation but do not export a native INT4 activation path: the Apple bundle stores packed phase-coded linear weights plus floating-point tensors for the remaining modules, and the Android bundle manifest explicitly sets activation_quantization=false . Accordingly, the W/A notation in Table 4 identifies the training/evaluation quantization setting of the source checkpoint rather than an end-to-end on-phone integer activation pipeline. Inference on both phones therefore runs packed low-bit weights together with floating-point activations, LayerNorm, GELU, Softmax, residual additions, patch embedding, and classification logic. After export, the treatment of packed weights is runtime-dependent: the backend may keep packed storage, unpack weights ahead of execution, or partially convert them during kernel dispatch. We therefore report end-to-end latency, load time, and model size rather than a stagewise kernel decomposition. Our current harness also does not expose stable, directly comparable per-inference energy counters across the two mobile stacks, so we leave power and energy measurements to future work.
