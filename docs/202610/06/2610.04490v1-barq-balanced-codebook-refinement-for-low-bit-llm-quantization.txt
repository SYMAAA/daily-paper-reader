ABSTRACT 

As large language models (LLMs) grow in parameter count, model storage and parameter memory traffic have become major bottlenecks to efficient deployment. Codebook-based weight quantization reduces these costs, but imbalanced nearest-codeword assignments during fitting can leave some codewords insufficiently up-dated, limiting effective codebook utilization. To address this limitation, we propose Balanced Assignment Refinement for Quantization (BARQ), which im-proves quantization quality through balanced fitting of existing codebooks. Specif-ically, we first compute joint soft assignments between weight blocks and code-words through entropically regularized optimal transport with uniform marginals and curvature-weighted reconstruction costs, ensuring equal positive fitting mass for every codeword in the exact solution. We then refine the codewords through an assignment-weighted barycentric update, which we prove minimizes the fitting objective for fixed assignments. For finite Sinkhorn iterations, the implemented update retains this optimality provided all codeword masses exceed the denomina-tor floor. Finally, we discard the soft assignments and use the refined codebook for standard hard nearest-codeword encoding, with our analysis establishing sufficient conditions for reducing hard-quantization distortion and evaluation loss. Across multiple LLMs, BARQ achieves lower perplexity and higher mean zero-shot ac-curacy than the evaluated baselines at comparable bit budgets. The code is available at https://github.com/chenhangcuisg-code/ BARQ .

Weight-only quantization (Fran-tar et al., 2023; Lin et al., 2024; Dettmers et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2024; Chee et al., 2023) reduces model storage and data-transfer costs through low-bit parameter representations and has become a common approach to ef-ficient LLM deployment. Conventional scalar quantization maps individual weights to a limited set of quantization levels. At extremely low bit widths, this representation can struggle to preserve weight information, leading to substantial reconstruction error and model-quality degradation (van Baalen et al., 2024; Egiazar-ian et al., 2024). Codebook-based quantization (van Baalen et al., 2024; Egiazarian et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024a; Tseng et al., 2024) jointly represents short weight blocks with shared codewords, offering a more flexible representation for extremely low-bit compression. However, realizing this representational advantage also depends on the effective use of codewords during fitting. Conven-tional codebook fitting (Lloyd, 1982; Ge et al., 2014; van Baalen et al., 2024) typically alternates between nearest-codeword assignment and codeword updates based on the assigned blocks. In-dependent nearest-codeword assignment (Bradley et al., 2000; Malinen & Fränti, 2014; Zheng & Vedaldi, 2023) places no constraint on the total fitting mass received by each codeword, potentially leaving some codewords with few or no supporting blocks and limiting effective codebook utiliza-tion. Further improving low-bit codebook quantization therefore calls for better codeword fitting and an analysis linking this refinement to final hard-quantization distortion and model performance. To address this limitation, we propose Balanced Assignment Refinement for Quantization (BARQ), a fitting-stage refinement procedure for existing codebooks. Specifically, BARQ uses curvature-weighted reconstruction costs to compute joint soft assignments between weight blocks and code-words through entropically regularized optimal transport (Cuturi, 2013; Peyré & Cuturi, 2019) with uniform marginals, giving every codeword the same positive fitting mass in the exact solution. It then refines the codewords through assignment-weighted barycentric updates, which minimize the fitting objective for fixed assignments. For finite Sinkhorn iterations, the implemented update re-tains this optimality provided every codeword’s fitting mass is at least the denominator floor. Fi-nally, BARQ discards the soft assignments and uses the refined codebook for standard hard nearest-codeword encoding, preserving the codebook size, index width, and inference path. Our analysis proves that an exact assignment–update pair does not increase the balanced fitting objective and further establishes sufficient conditions for reducing final hard-quantization distortion and evalu-ation loss. The main contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose BARQ, which combines curvature-weighted balanced entropic assignment with barycentric updates to refine existing codebooks during fitting while preserving the standard hard encoding rule and inference path. • We establish a theoretical connection between balanced fitting and final quantization qual-ity by proving that exact refinement does not increase the fitting objective, characterizing codeword optimality under finite Sinkhorn assignments, and deriving sufficient conditions for reduced hard-quantization distortion and evaluation loss. • We evaluate BARQ across six LLMs. In paired comparisons with GPT-VQ at matched bit budgets, the mean per-configuration relative changes yield a 7.0% reduction in perplexity and a 3.0% improvement in five-task mean accuracy. Further analyses examine assignment concentration, hard codeword utilization, and curvature-weighted reconstruction error. 

2 RELATED WORK 

Weight and Codebook Quantization. Scalar quantization improves low-bit LLM fidelity through error compensation (Frantar et al., 2023), activation-aware scaling (Lin et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2023), quantization-parameter optimization (Badri & Shaji, 2023; Shao et al., 2024), sparse–dense decomposition (Kim et al., 2024), and weight rotations (Ashkboos et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024b). Codebook-based methods jointly represent weight blocks using shared codewords. GPT-VQ (van Baalen et al., 2024) combines learned codebooks with error compensation, AQLM (Egiazarian et al., 2024) adopts additive codebooks, and QuIP# (Tseng et al., 2024) combines incoherence processing with lattice codebooks. VPTQ (Liu et al., 2024a) targets extreme low-bit vector quantization, while PV-Tuning (Malinovskii et al., 2024) improves the fine-tuning of compressed models. However, fitting learned codebooks through independent nearest-codeword assignment can yield imbalanced fitting mass, leaving some codewords insufficiently updated and limiting effective codebook utiliza-tion. Balanced Assignment and Entropic Optimal Transport. Sinkhorn–Knopp scaling (Sinkhorn & Knopp, 1967; Knight, 2008) is a classical method for matrix balancing. Entropic regularization (Cu-turi, 2013; Peyré & Cuturi, 2019) enables efficient approximate optimal transport via Sinkhorn it-erations, motivating faster solvers (Altschuler et al., 2017; Lin et al., 2019) and transport-based objectives across machine learning (Frogner et al., 2015; Genevay et al., 2018; Mena et al., 2018). Balanced or uniform-marginal assignments have been used to avoid degenerate solutions and im-prove discrete representation utilization in settings such as representation learning and vector quan-tization (Caron et al., 2020; Asano et al., 2020). Optimal transport has also been used to infer cross-neuron correspondences for weight-space fusion between heterogeneous language models (Cui et al., 2026). However, balanced codebook utilization alone does not guarantee lower hard-quantization distortion or evaluation loss in LLM weight quantization. 

3 METHOD 

This section introduces BARQ for post-training vector quantization of LLM weights. The presenta-tion first reviews the standard codebook fitting formulation and the limitation of independent nearest-codeword assignments. It then describes how BARQ refines an existing codebook through balanced, curvature-aware assignments while preserving the original hard quantization rule for deployment. 4 THEORETICAL ANALYSIS 

This section analyzes when balanced codebook fitting improves the hard quantizer used at deploy-ment. We first establish existence and positivity of the balanced-entropic assignment and charac-terize the barycentric codeword update. We then derive fitting-objective descent, give a sufficient condition for improvement after hard encoding, and connect hard distortion to downstream model metrics. These post-encoding changes are largest where the six-model matched comparison also records its greatest mean perplexity reduction. 96 CONCLUSION 

This work presents BARQ, a balanced codebook fitting method for post-training vector quantiza-tion of LLMs. By replacing independent hard assignments with balanced entropic assignments during refinement and updating codewords through barycentric fitting, BARQ improves codebook utilization while preserving the standard hard nearest-codeword quantization rule at deployment. The theoretical analysis establishes well-posed balanced fitting, objective descent under the code-word update, and sufficient conditions under which these improvements transfer to lower hard-quantization distortion and better model metrics. Empirically, BARQ generally improves quan-tization quality across different LLMs and low-bit settings, with more balanced codeword usage and lower reconstruction error. The paired comparisons and post-encoding diagnostics place this effect at both the model-output and weight-reconstruction levels. At K = 4, both methods nearly saturate the codebook and the average perplexity gain is smaller. Larger codebooks show stronger average perplexity reductions and larger post-encoding decreases in curvature-weighted reconstruction error across the three plotted models. These measurements connect codebook fit-ting to the quality of the hard quantizer used at inference. REPRODUCIBILITY STATEMENT

A.1 IMPLEMENTATION CHOICES 

Layer-Wise Grouping and Block Construction. For each linear layer, the weight matrix is partitioned into independent groups. Within a group, the weights are reshaped into N blocks 

xn ∈ Rd. The block dimension is d ∈ { 1, 2, 4} in the reported grid. For each block dimen-sion, the nominal codebook has K = 2Bd entries, with B = 2 index bits per scalar weight in the experiments. The tables report recorded bpv, excluding the separate codebook term. Ap-pendix A.1 gives the accounting term by term. 

Bit-Budget Accounting. The total budget uses the bits-per-value metric of GPT-VQ (van Baalen et al., 2024), which charges the indices and the codebook that decodes them, 

bpv GPT-VQ = log 2 Kd + K d b c

l , (12) with K centroids, block dimension d, codebook bit-width bc, and l the number of weights shar-ing one codebook. We instantiate it as follows. 13 Index term. A block of d weights stores one index into a codebook of K = 2 Bd entries, so the index term is log 2(K)/d = B bits per value, independent of d. These classical conditions connect the BARQ update to standard codebook generation. A codebook quantizer is determined by three components: an encoder that maps each weight block to an index, a decoder that maps each index back to a codeword, and a distortion criterion that scores the reconstruction (Ge et al., 2014; Jégou et al., 2011). BARQ modifies the assignment step within each codebook-refinement update and preserves the hard encoder. The analysis as-sumes a fixed groupwise quadratic metric. Section A explains how the Hessian-derived weights used in the current implementation differ across the stages. Quantization distortion is the common objective minimized by vector quantization methods, whose codebook constraints define the resulting quantizer family (Ge et al., 2014). Second-order weight quantization methods (Frantar et al., 2023; van Baalen et al., 2024) further de-fine a local quadratic metric using Hessian information estimated from calibration data. In the implementation, H is a positive diagonal matrix constructed by averaging the inverse-Hessian-derived refinement weights across blocks. The earlier hard fitting and the later lookup-table re-finement use their own stage-specific metrics. A distortion-minimizing quantizer satisfies two classical necessary conditions. For a fixed code-book, a distortion-minimizing encoder must map each block to its nearest codeword, which is the first Lloyd condition (Lloyd, 1982); the resulting quantizer is a Voronoi quantizer (Gray, 1984; Lloyd, 1982). For a fixed encoder and an unconstrained codebook, the distortion-minimizing code-word is the mean of the blocks assigned to it, which is the second Lloyd condition (Ge et al., 2014; Lloyd, 1982). Both statements are classically given for the unweighted Euclidean distortion un-der hard assignment. Under Equation (1), nearest is measured by the H-weighted cost. Because the same positive-definite H applies to every block in the group, it can be removed from the first-order condition for the codeword update. The optimal codeword therefore remains the arithmetic mean of its assigned blocks. Classical codebook generation alternates between nearest-codeword as-signment and mean-based codeword updates until reaching a Lloyd fixed point. Learned-codebook quantizers (van Baalen et al., 2024) commonly follow the same procedure. For qk(P ) > 0, the fixed-plan objective for codeword k has Hessian 2qk(P )H ≻ 0, so the sec-ond Lloyd condition produces the unique assignment-weighted barycenter. F LIMITATIONS 

The study focuses on codebook-based weight quantization at low bit widths. Quantization be-havior may also depend on model architecture, calibration data, and deployment conditions. Ex-ploring the method across a broader range of models and settings may further clarify how code-book refinement relates to downstream performance. 

G MORE DISCUSSIONS 

Q1. Does balanced fitting force uniform codeword use at deployment? 

No. The uniform row marginal fixes the fitting mass of each codeword in the exact soft assignment (Theorem 1). Deployment discards that assignment and applies the ordinary nearest-codeword en-coder (Equation 1). Hard use is therefore measured separately. Figure 7 shows higher realized use for BARQ at every measured layer for K = 16 and in 74 of 75 matched layers for K = 256 ; it is an observed result, not a consequence of the marginal constraint alone. 

Q2. What remains true when Sinkhorn scaling stops after finitely many iterations? 

The exact E-step has a unique positive coupling with the prescribed marginals. A finite iterate can retain a row-marginal residual, as described in Appendix A. The implemented M-step divides by the actual row mass with a 10 −12 floor. Whenever each mass exceeds that floor, its codeword up-date is still the exact barycenter of the computed assignment (Equation 21); the exact-coupling guarantee and the fixed-assignment update are distinct statements. 26 Q3. Why can a better fitting objective improve the final hard quantizer? 

Fitting-objective descent alone does not establish hard-distortion descent. Theorem 3 separates the barycentric gain ∆0 from the cost Γ0 of using the balanced assignment at the old codebook: 

∆0 > Γ0 is sufficient for a lower hard distortion after reassignment. The measured perplexity and zero-shot differences in Table 1 are empirical comparisons of the deployed quantizers; the theo-rem states the condition under which the local fitting gain transfers. 

Q4. How should the reported bit budgets be compared? 

The displayed bpv values count the index and group-scale terms. The full expression also con-tains codebook storage Kdb c/l (Equation 12), whose size depends on codebook sharing. The term is identical within each paired GPT-VQ/BARQ setting because the two methods use the same codebook size and sharing configuration. The external-baseline table instead places pub-lished methods at nearby reported operating points; its bpv values should be read as approxi-mate budget matches rather than exact compressed-file sizes. 

Q5. Is higher codeword utilization sufficient to explain the performance gains? 

The measurements separate use from reconstruction quality. At K = 4 , both methods nearly satu-rate the codebook and several paired configurations still regress; at K = 16 and K = 256 , BARQ usually uses more codewords and has larger average perplexity gains (Figure 5; Table 1). Fig-ure 4 also measures lower curvature-weighted reconstruction error in the plotted larger-codebook settings. These observations support reading utilization alongside the encoded reconstruction er-ror, not as a stand-alone performance metric. 

Q6. Does the GPT-VQ comparison isolate only the assignment rule? 

The paired rows share model weights, calibration data, nominal codebook size, and reported bpv. They begin from the same hard k-means codebook, but the GPT-VQ pipeline may include a later lookup-table refinement while BARQ uses a continuous barycentric update (Appendix B). Ta-ble 1 therefore compares the resulting end-to-end quantizers under matched settings; it does not attribute the entire difference to one assignment step. 27
