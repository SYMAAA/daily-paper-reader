## Abstract 

SVD-based pruning and quantization have recently emerged as a promising strategy for the ultra-efficient compression of large language models. In these methods, compression is performed in two stages: components are first truncated, and the remaining ones are subsequently quantized. Although this decoupled pipeline benefits from both pruning and quantization, it requires separate optimization for each stage and fails to fully exploit their balance, which can lead to suboptimal performance under aggressive compression. To address this limitation, we propose a new LLM compression method that co-optimizes pruning and quantization in a unified framework. Our key idea is a differentiable method for learning component-wise bit-widths, allowing less important components to be assigned 0-bit precision and pruned away. Notably, our method performs favorably against two-stage baselines, even when subjected to extreme quantization settings ( 1.61 bits) designed for ultra-efficiency. ## 1 Introduction 

Ultra-efficient compression serves as a key enabler for the seamless deployment of large language models (LLMs) in real-world environments [43]. Given that LLMs should operate on edge devices using limited hardware resources, it is imperative that compression methods significantly reduce memory usage and latency to accommodate such constraints. For example, to execute LLaMA-7B [30] on a consumer laptop GPU with 2GB of VRAM, we need to compress its parameters by approximately an order of magnitude. This lowers the memory footprint from 12.31GB 2 to 1.56GB, facilitating its deployment on such a laptop. However, it is widely recognized that reducing the parameters by more than 3×–4× will drastically degrade the performance of LLMs [21]. Recently, model pruning and quantization using singular value decomposition (SVD) have emerged as a promising approach to ultra-efficient LLM compression [39, 33, 34]. They have shown that model parameters can be effectively pruned by truncating the low singular-value components from the decomposed matrix. Additionally, the model can be further compressed by applying a quantization method to the non-truncated components. Although this two-stage approach enjoys the benefits of both pruning and quantization, it has the limitation that it operates as a decoupled process, which requires separate optimization for each stage. Obviously, the pruning ratio and quantization bit-width are closely related factors that play a critical role in optimizing LLM compression. A higher pruning ratio allows non-truncated components to be quantized at higher bit-widths, while a lower pruning ratio requires lower bit-widths. However, two-stage approaches that treat pruning and quantization as decoupled sequential steps often fail to find the exact balance, resulting in suboptimal trade-offs.

This limitation is consistent with a widely recognized principle [16, 1] in machine learning: jointly optimizing multiple constraints typically yields better performance compared to isolated sequential approaches. In a sequential pipeline, the quantization stage cannot recover the representational capacity irreversibly lost during the pruning stage, causing compound errors. On the other hand, co-optimization facilitates the efficient exploration of the combined parameter space of a model, helping it to adeptly negotiate the complex dependencies between sparsity and precision. Therefore, it is key to optimize pruning and quantization simultaneously to maximize their benefits for achieving ultra-efficient compression of LLMs. Motivated by this, we propose a co-optimized compression method that tightly couples pruning and quantization. Specifically, the proposed method applies an adaptive bit-width strategy to SVD-decomposed matrices. To realize this mixed-bit allocation, we use a differentiable bit-width (DBW) that defines pruning and quantization as a co-optimization problem, wherein the preci-sion for each SVD component is chosen from a set of integer candidates including zero bits ( e.g. , within the range [0,5]) in a learnable manner. By completely discard-ing the 0-bit components, we perform rank truncation, which fundamentally acts as pa-rameter pruning. Furthermore, our DBW is inherently free from the strict constraints of maintaining the original structure of the weight matrix. This is because, unlike unstructured pruning that results in irregular memory layouts [36], our DBW naturally preserves hardware-efficient dense computation. The 0-bit truncation effectively minimizes the inner rank dimension of the decomposed matrices, while maintaining the integrity of dense matrix multiplication. In addition, to ensure practicality for real-world deployment, our DBW sorts SVD components into distinct bit-groups. Therefore, we can optimize both memory footprint and computational overhead in mixed-bit-based quantization settings. This bit-grouping approach enables standardized weight packing and supports weight-quantized general matrix multiplication (GEMM) within each group. To validate its practical applicability, we implement a CUDA kernel that performs GEMM operations on our mixed-bit weight matrices, achieving reasonable speedups over the cuBLAS library. We evaluate our DBW on ten benchmarks: perplexity in WikiText-2 and C4, and zero-shot accuracy on eight commonsense reasoning datasets. Our experiments cover eight LLMs from the LLaMA-1/2/3 families, spanning parameter scales from 7B to 70B. We show that our DBW achieves favorable results compared to recent LLM compression approaches, including quantization, structured pruning, SVD, and SVD-based hybrid compression methods under ultra-efficient compression settings. 

## 2 Method 

2.1 Motivation: Rethinking Mixed-Bit Allocation 

Before delving into the technical details of the proposed method, we first rethink the current paradigm of mixed-bit allocation in the context of singular value decomposition. Precisely, we seek to answer two fundamental questions: first, an underlying reason for a limited exploration of mixed-bit allocation in prior works; and second, the analytical justification for why our differentiable method is effective. 

Why is Mixed-Bit Allocation Underexplored in Existing SVD-based Methods? Although SVD-based pruning has shown great promise in LLM compression, mixed-bit allocation has remained largely unexplored in prior works [39, 33]. The primary bottleneck lies in the conventional reliance on a non-differentiable bit-width approach. Prior methods [12, 41] treat bit-widths as discrete and non-differentiable parameters, which strictly prevents gradient-based optimization. Therefore, discovering optimal mixed-bit widths presents a significant challenge in terms of time complexity. Indeed, directly searching for the optimal mixed-bit configuration is intractable, as it requires O(BC ) time for bit candidates B and components C. Since navigating this enormous search space is practically difficult 2using manual or heuristic approaches, previous SVD-based methods have naturally avoided mixed-bit assignments. Experimental results supporting our analysis are provided in Table 5. 

Why is Co-optimization of SVD Pruning and Quantization Effective? An Entropy Perspective. 

To overcome the intractable search space, making the bit-widths differentiable proves to be a highly effective solution. The core benefit of a differentiable bit-width is that it enables the smooth co-optimization of both weight quantization and rank truncation. We can analyze the effectiveness of this co-optimization through an entropy perspective. SVD inherently concentrates the representational energy of a matrix into a small number of dominant singular values, and decomposed matrices naturally produce a low-entropy state with different levels of significance. Although prior SVD-based methods also utilize this property, they typically perform pruning and quantization sequentially, ultimately applying a uniform-precision quantization to the surviving components. This sequential approach is fundamentally incapable of adapting to the various entropy levels within the decomposed matrix. Our differentiable approach directly exploits this entropy variance. By co-optimizing both operations, it adaptively allocates varying mixed bit-widths, assigning zero bits (effective truncation) to less important components and more bits to information-rich ones. This process precisely aligns the quantization resolution with the specific entropy of each of the SVD components. Experimental support and entropy-based analyses on this adaptive co-optimization are detailed in Figure 6 and §C.1.

More empirical results of MMLU and GSM8K can be found in §D.2. 

Calibration. Our calibration dataset consists of 4096 samples with a sequence length of 2048, which are randomly selected from the RedPajama-1T dataset [35]. ## 4 Related Work 

Post-training Quantization. Recent studies [11, 37, 27, 17] propose post-training methods for quantizing LLMs to low bit-widths with modest computational overhead. Chen et al. [6] further introduce a block-wise QAT approach, in which all other layers are fixed and each block is optimized sequentially, reducing memory cost while requiring only a small calibration set. 

Mixed-bit Quantization. The unstructured mixed-bit quantization method [26] assigns a high bit for the salient weights while setting a low bit for the remaining weights for ultra-efficiency. They recover accuracy by using an efficient post-training process ( e.g. , GPTQ or QAT). Similarly, Huang et al. [12] define Hessian-based importance to select a prominent weight, which works favorably compared to the existing criterion. Zhao et al. [41] propose a column-wise mixed-bit quantization method to lower the storage requirements of bit masks in unstructured approaches. 

Singular Value Decomposition. The main advantage of SVD is that it reduces both memory usage and computational cost simultaneously, which is particularly beneficial for LLM acceleration. [39] incorporate activation statistics into the decomposition process to preserve the most prominent channels, while [33] use a whitening matrix to better align the truncation loss. Several studies [39, 33] have explored integrating pruning and quantization through SVD and reported promising results. However, they treats pruning and quantization separately, as discussed further in Appendix of §B. 

## 5 Conclusion 

We study a hybrid compression approach that combines pruning and quantization to achieve an effective trade-off in singular value decomposition. We propose an integrated framework that unifies the two techniques as a single bit-allocation problem, where each SVD component is assigned a precision selected from zero-inclusive integer bit candidates. To make the framework practical, we further develop a differentiable bit-width method that learns this trade-off in a gradient-based manner and generalizes well across diverse singular-value distributions. Experimental results show that our DBW framework performs favorably against existing compression methods, particularly in the ultra-efficient regime. We hope that this work provides a foundation for subsequent research devoted to compressing large-scale models to an ultra-small scale. Limitation. Our evaluation is currently limited to language models. Although we focus on pre-trained LLMs under constrained computational resources to support edge deployment, future work should further validate the generality of our framework across vision, speech, and multimodal architectures. 9Acknowledgments

A.3 Our DBW Hyperparameters 

Since our DBW introduces two types of learnable parameters ( i.e. , matrix-level and component-level bit-width) to co-optimize pruning and quantization in a single step, we must specify their learning rates. For simplicity, we use the same learning-rate setting across all target LLMs. Specifically, we set the component-level learning rate to 2e-5, following the default learning rate used for weight optimization, and the matrix-level learning rate to 1e-4 to encourage faster convergence compared to other learnable parameters. As our primary goal is to demonstrate the effectiveness of the differentiable bit-width learning framework, other than the effect of optimal hyperparameter choices, we adopt a single learning-rate configuration that generalizes across a wide range of model sizes, thereby minimizing the influence of model-specific hyperparameter tuning. However, in practical applications, more carefully tuned learning rates for each model could further improve performance.

## B Extended Related Work 

B.1 Hybrid Compression 

Previously, a few works studied the hybrid methods that combine two different compression ap-proaches. As discussed in the manuscript, a mix of truncation and quantization performs well in the ultra-efficient compression ratio. In addition to the already introduced approach, there are two extra branches of hybrid compression methods: (1) unstructured pruning with quantization, and (2) structured pruning with quantization. The representative example of (1) unstructured pruning with quantization [20] is to apply quantization on the already pruned weight, which, however, includes a complicated design due to the unstructured pruning to accelerate the compressed model on the GPUs. 14 Similarly, (2) structured pruning with quantization [42] also applies the structured pruning and then performs a quantization on it. This approach can have a practical speed gain from the modern GPU; however, it loses substantial information in the first pruning step when the pruning ratio is higher than 20%. 

B.2 Unified Mixed-bit Quantization 

Prior work on unified mixed-bit quantization that includes 0-bit as a candidate bit-width is limited. Van Baalen et al. [32] propose a bit-width learning approach that includes 0-bit as a candidate. In theory, this method can learn the optimal trade-off between quantization and pruning. However, there are four limitations that prevent this method from being directly applied to ultra-efficient LLM compression. First, this method brings a considerable training overhead according to the length of bit candidates, which is moderate in a lightweight vision CNN; however, it becomes prohibitive in an LLM with more than 100 × as many parameters as the CNN. Therefore, the existing method is difficult to use to extremely compress current LLMs that use a billion-scale transformer-based architecture. Furthermore, it needs to repeatedly retrain the model to find an exact bits-per-weight. Since there is no regularization of bit budget that keeps the average learned bit under the budget, we need a full hyperparameter search to achieve the specific target bit-width, which can lead to our training cost being intractable for compressing LLMs. Moreover, since this method selects bit-widths from the limited candidates such as 0, 2, 4, and 8, it is difficult to find an optimal trade-off under the ultra-efficient compression ratio. Finally, they perform channel-pruning at the original weight matrix and remove whole channels from the forward pass if the learned bit is selected as a 0-bit, which could disrupt the whole computation flow and yield unexpected results in practical transformer architectures that LLMs are usually built on.

Bit packing. Since our DBW employs bit-width from 0 to 5, we use two different bit-packing schemes depending on whether the bit-width can evenly pack into a 32-bit word: (1) evenly packable and (2) non-evenly packable bit-widths. For (1) evenly packable bit-widths, we can easily use the widely used packing approach [17] to pack weight with the power of 2 bits since the commonly used 32-bit word is divisible by 1, 2, and 4 bits. However, for (2) non-evenly packable bit-widths, directly saving them results in extra memory overhead because they lead to leftover bits in a 32-bit word. Therefore, we need to design a packing approach that can save the weight into non-divisible bit-widths without extra memory overhead. For this, we employ a divide-and-conquer strategy that saves a weight in separate bit-widths that can evenly pack into a 32-bit word ( e.g. , 3-bit into 2 and 1-bit) and concatenate them via our on-the-fly dequantization kernel where we can utilize a fast shift and & operator as shown in Figure 12, which introduce a small overhead from the baseline dequantization computation cost of O(n) to O(1 .5n) regarding to the matrix size n that is still negligible compared to ones of GEMM operation O(nT ) when the token size T is large enough. Consequently, this divide-and-conquer method achieves the theoretical memory compression ratio for non-dividable bit-width as 16 /bit while being fast to load weight into memory since the adding one more item of extra weight into a source memory address list that has more than three (e.g., weight, scale, and zero-point) does not introduce the memory access bottleneck and the concatenation overhead is negligible in the efficient CUDA kernel (see Table 12). 

Parallel GEMM execution. We decompose a large GEMM kernel into multiple smaller GEMMs with different bit-widths. To execute these kernels in parallel, we use CUDA streams, which allow multiple kernels to run concurrently through the official CUDA API. We adopt this straightforward implementation to focus on validating the effectiveness of the proposed mixed-precision GEMM, rather than optimizing kernel-level performance. However, developing a fully fused CUDA kernel 19 could further reduce kernel launch overhead and improve runtime efficiency. Therefore, in future research, we believe that researchers and practitioners interested in production-level deployment can achieve additional speed improvements by implementing such fused kernels.

E.2 Bi-level Differentiable Bit-Width 

Table 19 shows that our algorithm yields more flexible bit-width by allowing moderate differences in the bits-per-weight and memory compression ratios within a block. Instead of imposing a strictly uniform quantization pattern, this design provides additional freedom to adapt the allocation to the local characteristics of the parameters in each block. Such flexibility leads to more efficient use of the available bit budget while better preserving the information required for model performance. 

E.3 Singular Value Entropy Minimization 

We propose a minimization loss designed to reduce the entropy of singular values during training for LLM compression. As shown in Figure 14, this loss effectively encourages the singular-value distribution to become more concentrated, allowing the model information to be represented by a smaller number of dominant SVD components compared with the baseline SVD approach. In other words, our method promotes a more compact representation by increasing the relative importance of a few major components while suppressing less important ones. In addition, our method drives SVD components associated with small singular values closer to zero. This behavior is especially beneficial during inference, because it enables more aggressive truncation of low-rank components than the original SVD without severely affecting the representation quality. As a result, the proposed method provides a more compression-friendly singular-value structure, which can improve the efficiency of LLM deployment.
