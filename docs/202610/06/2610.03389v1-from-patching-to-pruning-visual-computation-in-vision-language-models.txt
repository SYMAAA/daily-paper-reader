ABSTRACT
Vision–language models (VLMs) incur substantial inference cost because every
visual token is processed by the attention and MLP projections of every decoder
layer, even when token-specific visual computation is unnecessary at many depths.
We introduce Patch-to-Prune (P2P), inspired by Mechanistic Interpretability, a
training-free framework that converts activation patching from a diagnostic tool
into an inference-time computation bypass. P2P performs validation-guided for-
ward and backward layer sweeps to identify decoder regions whose visual-token
projection outputs can be replaced by fixed neutral proxy activation vectors within
a user-specified accuracy tolerance. Unlike conventional token-pruning methods,
P2P preserves the sequence length, token order, positional information, attention
mask, and residual pathways, thereby pruning computation without removing to-
kens or modifying the pretrained model weights. We evaluate P2P on four VLMs
from the Qwen2.5-VL and LLaVA families across seven multi-modal benchmarks
using mutually disjoint calibration, validation, and test partitions. P2P at a 3%
tolerance retains around 94% of dense accuracy while reducing FLOPs by 55%.
Beyond these efficiency gains, our layer-wise analysis suggests that visual pro-
cessing in VLMs is non-uniformly distributed across decoder depth: early and
late layers often require little token-specific visual computation, whereas inter-
mediate layers appear to perform most task-relevant visual integration, enabling
later reasoning to rely largely on visual information already embedded in shared
residual and textual representations. This makes P2P both an efficient inference
framework and a causal lens into visual information processing in VLMs.

INTRODUCTION
Vision–language models (VLMs) have achieved superior performance across visual question an-
swering, multimodal reasoning, object recognition, and spatial understanding. This capability, how-
ever, comes with substantial inference cost. An input image is typically represented by hundreds or
thousands of visual tokens, which are processed repeatedly by every decoder layer. Since visual to-
kens typically dominate the input sequence, repeatedly applying the attention transformations makes
the language decoder a major computational bottleneck in VLM inference Zhao et al. (2024a); Shen
et al. (2025e; 2026); Zhao et al. (2025a); Mi et al. (2026); Zhao et al. (2026a); Li et al. (2022); Zhang
et al. (2022); Li et al. (2023a); Yang et al. (2023); Shen et al. (2025b).
Prior training-free acceleration methods like FastV Chen et al. (2024b) and PyramidDrop Xing
et al. (2024) tackle this problem mainly by pruning visual tokens: they estimate each token’s impor-
tance from early attention or similarity scores, then permanently discard tokens judged less relevant.
However, this approach ignores how visual information actually flows through the network, and
it overlooks the fact that a token’s importance in later layers may not decrease monotonically—a
token that seems unimportant early on could become critical downstream. This limitation raises a
complementary question: which layers are actually responsible for processing visual tokens?

To investigate this problem, we first perform a layer sensitivity analysis through activation patching,
which replaces the output of a selected module with a reference activation to identify its causal sig-
nificance. Our analysis reveals a pronounced non-uniformity: substitution of visual-token projection
outputs is well tolerated in many early and late layers, whereas an intermediate band of layers ex-
hibits markedly greater sensitivity to such substitution. This pattern suggests that redundancy within
VLM decoders is not uniformly distributed, but is instead systematically structured across depth.
Based on our layer sensitivity analysis, we propose Patch-to-Prune (P2P) shown in Figure 1, an
acceleration framework that converts this diagnostic intervention into an actual inference-time com-
putation bypass. P2P first runs the frozen VLM on a small calibration set and constructs a modality-
conditioned activation prototype for every decoder layer and linear module. It then performs cu-
mulative forward and backward patching sweeps on a disjoint validation set. These sweeps identify
the layers whose visual-token computations can be replaced by corresponding prototypes while re-
maining within a specified accuracy tolerance. The union of these selected regions defines the set of
pressable or safe layers during inference.
At test time, P2P does not perform the full computations. Instead, for visual tokens in safe layers,
their outputs of attention projection modules are directly filled with cached activation prototype,
without actual computations. Only textual tokens in safe layers and all tokens in other layers go
through the typical attention computations. Importantly, P2P prunes computation rather than to-
kens. Input embeddings of visual tokens remain in the sequence throughout the network, and the
original sequence length, token order, attention mask, and residual pathways are preserved. This de-
sign avoids the irreversible token-removal decisions by conventional pruning methods and enables
P2P to be applied directly to pretrained VLMs without modifying their parameters, changing their
architecture, or performing additional training. Our main contributions are as follows:
• We introduce P2P, an acceleration framework that uses modality-conditioned activation
patching to localize redundant visual-token computations across decoder depth.
• We present a validation-guided, tolerance-controlled layer-selection procedure that pro-
vides an interpretable accuracy–efficiency trade-off.
• We convert post-hoc activation replacement into an actual computation bypass that skips
selected attention and MLP projection matmuls while preserving all tokens and the original
sequence structure.
• We demonstrate consistent FLOP and latency reductions across four VLMs and seven mul-
timodal benchmarks, and show that decoder redundancy is concentrated near the early and
late layers of the network. We demonstrate that our P2P-aware finetuning can adapt the
model directly under its patched computation graph and substantially recover the accuracy
lost under aggressive pressing.

Activation patching and causal localization.
Activation patching, also referred to as interchange
intervention or causal tracing, determines the causal importance of an internal model component by
replacing its activation and measuring the resulting behavioral change Geiger et al. (2021); Zhang
& Nanda (2024). It has been used to localize information processing to particular layers, token
positions, attention heads, and intermediate representations in language models. Prior work has
also studied methodological choices for patching and scalable approximations that reduce the cost
of evaluating large numbers of interventions Kram´ar et al. (2024); Heimersheim & Nanda (2024).
These methods are primarily interpretability tools: the original computation is normally executed
before its activation is overwritten, so the intervention itself does not accelerate inference.

LAYER SENSITIVITY ANALYSIS
To identify which layers are actually responsible for processing vision and text tokens when the
model answers questions, we first perform a layer sensitivity analysis.
3.1
NOTATIONS
Let M be a frozen, instruction-tuned VLM whose language backbone consists of N decoder layers.
Given an image–question pair, the processor produces a token sequence of length T that interleaves
projected visual tokens with text tokens. Each decoder layer ℓ∈{0, . . . , N −1} contains a set of
linear projections S = {W Q, W K, W V , W O, W gate, W up, W down}, i.e., the four attention projec-
tions and the three gated-MLP projections. For a module m ∈S at layer ℓ, we write aℓ,m(t) ∈Rdm
for its output activation at token position t, where dm is the module’s output length.

3.3
CUMULATIVE LAYER INTERVENTION
To localize the causally load-bearing depth band, we run cumulative sweeps on a held-out validation
split. These sweeps are done on three setups of streams: image only (image positions ←image
means), text only (text positions ←text means), and dual (both, same-stream). We perform
sweep from two directions with patch layers: (i) Forward: patch layers Lfwd(L) = {0, . . . , L} with
L starting from 0 and growing from the input side; and (ii) Backward: patch layers Lbwd(L) =
{L, . . . , N −1} with L starting from N −1 and decreasing from output. For each value of L during
each direction in each stream, we replace the module outputs of patch layers with the example-level
mean activation, and record the corresponding accuracy drop on this split. Each sweep point re-
registers the intervention on the specific layer set and these points localize how much token-specific
information each modality contributes at a given depth.

Observations.
Figure 2, Figure 6 (Appendix A.2) and Figure 7 (Appendix A.2) reveal a consistent
depth-dependent pattern: replacing visual-token projection outputs is often tolerated across substan-
tial early and late decoder regions (demonstrated by the small accuracy drop of a few first layers
in the forward sweep or a few last layers in the backward sweep), whereas extending the interven-
tion into intermediate layers causes markedly larger accuracy degradation. In contrast, patching
text-token computations remains broadly damaging, indicating that this redundancy is primarily as-
sociated with the visual stream. We hypothesize that visual information follows a staged computa-
tional pathway. Early layers begin encoding visual features, intermediate layers perform the primary
task-relevant integration into the shared residual stream and textual representations, and later lay-
ers generate the answer largely from information already embedded in these representations. This
motivates replacing unnecessary visual-token projection computations at pressable depths with a
modality-conditioned fixed proxy activation. This preserves the average contribution of each layer
while bypassing token-specific computation.

PATCH-TO-PRUNE FRAMEWORK
4.1
OVERVIEW
We propose Patch-to-Prune (P2P), a training-free method for reducing the inference cost of VLMs.
P2P framework consists of three stages: (i) layer sensitivity analysis with mechanistic profiling of
model’s dependence on visual computation at each projection module, (ii) validation-guided safe-
layer selection, and (iii) computation-bypassed inference.

4.3.1
TOLERANCE SENSITIVITY.
The tolerance τ is the single control parameter of P2P: relaxing it admits more boundaries under the
selection rule above, expanding the safe set inward from both ends of the decoder. To characterize
this trade-off on Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, we sweep τ from 1% to 10%, re-running safe-layer se-
lection on the validation split at each setting and evaluating the resulting configuration on the full
test set. Figure 3(a) shows that increasing τ produces a consistent accuracy–efficiency trade-off:
average accuracy falls from 74.6% at 1% to 70.6% at 7% and 67.1% at 10%, while average compu-
tation drops from 2.1 to 1.3 TFLOPs against a 3.1 TFLOP dense reference. Figure 3(b) providing
the layer-wise view explains this: pressed safe layers first appear at the top of the decoder and near
the input, and as τ grows they expand inward toward the sensitive intermediate band (approximately
layers 14–21), which is precisely where accuracy begins to break down. We therefore adopt τ = 3%
as the default operating point with a better trade-off between accuracy and computations.

4.5
P2P-AWARE FINE-TUNING
We propose an optional P2P-aware finetuning under P2P-patched computation graph for accuracy.
Fine-tuning under mean patching.
For the safe layers S∗
τ identified as safe under an accuracy
tolerance τ, we insert LoRA adapters into the attention and MLP projections of these layers. To
mitigate the training and test mismatch problem, we use the same P2P-patched computation graph
during training. Specifically, for visual tokens in safe layers, we skip their computations in projection
modules and directly use the cached mean activation in Eq. 1 to replace their original outputs. Thus
the corresponding projection computation is bypassed and no gradient is propagated through the
LoRA adapter for those patched positions. The adapters are instead optimized through the remaining
active computation, including unpatched textual tokens and modules outside the patched layers. This
encourages the adapters to compensate for the information removed by P2P rather than relying on
activations that are unavailable during patched inference.
Training objective.
We optimize adapters using teacher-forced causal language modeling. The
multimodal input prompt is masked from the loss, and cross-entropy is computed only over the

EVALUATION
5.1
EXPERIMENT SETUP
Datasets.
We evaluate P2P on seven benchmarks spanning diverse multimodal capabili-
ties:WhatsUp-VLM Kamath et al. (2023),POPE (pope) Li et al. (2023b) , ScienceQA-IMG
(sqa) Lu et al. (2022), GQA (gqa) Hudson & Manning (2019), TextVQA (tvqa) Singh et al. (2019),
CV-Bench (cvb) Tong et al. (2024), and AI2D (ai2d) Kembhavi et al. (2016). For each benchmark,
we use 100 samples for proxy estimation and a disjoint set of 100 samples for layer selection. We
set the proxy-estimation size to 100 based on the ablation in Figure 5. Finally, we evaluate on a large
held-out test set. More details are in Appendix A.6.
Models.
We evaluate P2P on four VLMs from two model families, Qwen Bai et al. (2023); Wang
et al. (2024); Team (2025) and LLaVA Liu et al. (2023; 2024a;b), spanning parameter scales of 3B,
7B, and 13B. Specifically, we use Qwen2.5-VL-3/7B-Instruct, and LLaVA-v1.6-Vicuna-7/13B-HF.
Baselines.
We compare P2P with the dense model and two training-free visual-token pruning
methods, FastV Chen et al. (2024b) and PyramidDrop Xing et al. (2024), implemented within the
same evaluation framework using identical models, prompts, precision, test examples, and metrics.
The Vanilla baseline applies no token removal, activation replacement, or layer removal. We include
token pruning baselins (FastV and PyramidDrop) not as competitors but to put P2P in perspective:
obviating per-layer visual computation matches the efficiency of token pruning, and opens a com-
plementary direction—pruning tokens within the vital layers P2P preserves.
P2P-aware finetuning protocol.
For main results, we do not finetune the model to make a fair
comparison. In ablation study, we further demonstrate that our finetuning can improve the per-
formance effectively. Our finetuning trains on the 100-sample statistics split that built the mean
prototypes and validates on the 100-sample split that produced the saliency profiles, adding no data
beyond the training-free pipeline. We train with batch size 1, LoRA rank r = 1, scaling factor
α = 1, and AdamW with a learning rate of 2 × 10−4. Training is performed for 10 epochs.

MAIN RESULT FOR P2P
From mechanistic discovery to full-test acceleration.
P2P tests whether visual-computation re-
dundancy discovered from small, disjoint subsets generalizes to the full held-out test set. We eval-
uate three operating points: a conservative 1% tolerance, the default 3% tolerance, and a more ag-
gressive 5% tolerance, which progressively expand the pressed regions to trade accuracy for greater
computation reduction.
Accuracy–computation trade-off.
Table 1 shows that at the conservative 1% tolerance, P2P pre-
serves most of the Vanilla model accuracy while already removing a substantial amount of com-
putation. For LLaVA-7B, average accuracy (computed from Table 1) decreases only from 0.641
to 0.626, retaining 97.7% of Vanilla accuracy, while average FLOPs decrease from 30.8 to 17.5 T,
corresponding to a 43.2% reduction. For LLaVA-13B, P2P retains 98.1% of Vanilla accuracy, with
average accuracy changing from 0.693 to 0.680, while reducing FLOPs from 59.2 to 39.8 T, a
32.8% reduction. The default 3% tolerance provides a stronger accuracy–efficiency trade-off. Ta-
ble 1 shows that on LLaVA-7B, P2P reduces average FLOPs from 30.8 to 12.6 T, a 59.1% reduction,
7


while retaining 93.8% of Vanilla average accuracy. On LLaVA-13B, average FLOPs decrease from
59.2 to 22.4 T, a 62.2% reduction, while retaining 94.8% of Vanilla accuracy. Averaged across the
two model sizes, P2P at 3% therefore retains approximately 94% of Vanilla accuracy while reducing
the reported FLOPs by approximately 60%. Increasing the tolerance to 5% further reduces average
FLOPs to 10.1 T for LLaVA-7B and 18.8 T for LLaVA-13B, corresponding to reductions of 67.2%
and 68.2%, respectively. However, the average accuracies decrease to 0.574 and 0.605. The toler-
ance therefore controls a genuine trade-off: relaxing it expands the pressed prefix and suffix toward
task-sensitive intermediate layers, yielding greater computation reduction but also increasing the
risk of removing task-relevant visual processing. More results on Qwen models are in Table 3 of
Appendix A.3.
Comparison with the baseline pruning.
At the default 3% setting, P2P reaches a substantially
lower computation regime than FastV and PyramidDrop. For LLaVA-7B, P2P requires 12.6 T
FLOPs on average, compared with 16.2 T for FastV and 14.5 T for PyramidDrop. For LLaVA-
13B, P2P requires 22.4 T, compared with 31.0 T for FastV and 28.1 T for PyramidDrop. P2P
therefore reduces average FLOPs by an additional 22.2% relative to FastV and 13.1% relative to
PyramidDrop on LLaVA-7B, and by 27.7% and 20.3%, respectively, on LLaVA-13B.
This more aggressive computation reduction is accompanied by lower average accuracy than the
token-pruning baselines. At 3%, P2P obtains average accuracies of 0.601 and 0.657 on LLaVA-
7B and LLaVA-13B, whereas FastV obtains 0.635 and 0.686, and PyramidDrop obtains 0.639 and
0.689. Thus, P2P provides a distinct operating point rather than uniformly dominating baselines.
Wall-clock acceleration.
The reduction in visual-token projection operations translates into mea-
surable wall-clock improvements at the default operating point. In Table 1, on LLaVA-7B, P2P at
3% reduces average latency by 15.4%: from 0.351 to 0.297 seconds per sample, whereas FastV
and PyramidDrop show higher latency than Vanilla despite their FLOP reductions. On LLaVA-
13B, average latency decreases from 0.619s to 0.462s, a 25.4% reduction. P2P is also faster than
both token-pruning baselines at this setting: FastV and PyramidDrop require 0.377s and 0.369s on
LLaVA-7B and 0.578s and 0.573s seconds on LLaVA-13B, respectively.
These results are notable since lower theoretical FLOPs need not translate into lower latency. Token-
pruning baselines add ranking, indexing, and sequence-reorganization overhead, whereas P2P di-
rectly skips selected visual-row projection matmuls. Yet P2P is not strictly monotonic either: be-
cause each patched layer carries a fixed patching cost, a higher tolerance can reduce FLOPs while
increasing latency, so the lowest-FLOP configuration is not always the fastest. Overall, the 3% con-
figuration therefore provides the strongest overall balance among accuracy, FLOPs, and observed
wall-clock latency in Table 1.
Generalization across tasks.
The full-test results also show that the amount of pressable visual
computation is task dependent. At 1%, P2P improves LLaVA-7B accuracy on WhatsUp-VGQA
from 0.580 to 0.635. For LLaVA-13B, it improves WhatsUp-COCOQA from 0.754 to 0.762 and
WhatsUp-VGQA from 0.744 to 0.769. At 3%, LLaVA-7B nearly preserves its dense AI2D accuracy,
obtaining 0.641 compared with 0.642. Other tasks, particularly TextVQA and CV-Bench, do not
collapse but are more sensitive to aggressive pressing. We note that the full-test accuracy drop
can, on some datasets, exceed the specified tolerance. This motivates a fine-tuning setup that tests
whether lightweight LoRA adaptation can compensate for that sensitivity.
Overall, despite this dataset-specific variation, the pressable regions identified yield on an average
over all four models and 8 datasets a 55% FLOPs reduction and 22% latency reduction while re-
taining 94% of dense accuracy—consistent computation savings without collapse, confirming that
lightweight mechanistic discovery and activation replacements generalize to large-scale evaluation.

CONCLUSION
P2P turns activation patching from a diagnostic tool into a practical inference mechanism: by iden-
tifying decoder depths where visual-token projection outputs can be safely replaced with fixed prox-
ies, it bypasses unnecessary computation without removing tokens or disrupting the original se-
quence structure. Beyond its efficiency gains, P2P reveals that visual processing is concentrated in a
task-sensitive intermediate region, while substantial early- and late-layer computation is redundant.
This depth-aware perspective opens a promising direction for token pruning, where future methods
can decide not only which visual tokens matter, but also where their computation is truly necessary.

APPENDIX
A.1
ADVANTAGES
Bi-directional cumulative sweeps with unique observations and insights.
Our sensitivity anal-
ysis show that replacing visual-token projection outputs is often tolerated across substantial early
and late layers, while replacing textual token computations remains damaging. Our unique observa-
tions provide deep insights for how visual information flows in VLMs, as detailed in Section 3.4.
Pruning computations instead of tokens.
P2P prunes computations along depth without modi-
fying tokens, whereas prior methods prune tokens along the sequence. Our method is orthogonal
and complementary to exist token pruning research.
Finetuning under P2P framework.
Different from other methods finetuning the whole model
with LoRA, our P2P-aware fine-tuning places LoRA adapters only in safe layers and keeps patches
active during training. The adapters are consequently optimized under the same computation graph
used at inference and learn to compensate for the information removed by the intervention.
Minor memory cost for caches.
P2P introduces only a minor memory overhead as it stores a sin-
gle activation vector for each projection module at each decoder layer. These fixed vectors are reused
across all visual tokens, making the cache independent of sequence length and batch size. Thus, P2P
trades a small amount of static memory for substantially reduced visual-token computation.

A.4
PATCH-TYPE ABLATION
We examine whether the observed layer sensitivity depends on the modality-conditioned mean proxy
or can be reproduced by arbitrary activation replacement. Using Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, we per-
form the same cumulative forward sweep on WhatsUp, POPE, and ScienceQA while replacing the
image-token outputs of the attention and MLP projections with one of four alternatives: the image-
stream mean, zero, Gaussian noise, or a randomly sampled real visual-token activation. As shown
in Figure 4, mean replacement most consistently preserves the dense behavior across the pressable
early layers. Zero and noise introduce larger immediate degradation, particularly on ScienceQA,
showing that simply suppressing or perturbing the visual contribution does not provide a suitable
computation proxy. Random token replacement can remain competitive in some layers because it
retains a realistic activation scale, but its behavior is less stable and depends on the sampled ac-
tivation. These results support the modality-conditioned mean as a deterministic, distributionally
grounded proxy that preserves the typical contribution of each module while removing example-
specific visual computation.
A.5
PROXY-ESTIMATION SET SIZE ABLATION
We further study how many examples are required to estimate the proxy activations. We vary the
statistics-set size over Nstat ∈{1, 25, 50, 100, 250, 500} while keeping the 100-example validation
sweep and all other settings unchanged. Figure 5 shows that the overall depth-dependent sensitivity
pattern remains similar across all sizes: the early layers tolerate mean replacement, whereas ex-
14


tending the intervention toward the intermediate layers causes a pronounced accuracy drop. Larger
statistics sets do not yield a consistent improvement across WhatsUp, POPE, and ScienceQA, and
the transition into the sensitive depth region is already visible with relatively few examples. We
therefore use 100 statistics examples throughout the main experiments, as this provides a stable
layer- and module-specific proxy while keeping the mechanistic discovery stage inexpensive.
