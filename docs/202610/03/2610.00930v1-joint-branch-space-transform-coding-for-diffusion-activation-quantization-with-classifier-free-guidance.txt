# Abstract 

Post-training quantization for diffusion models increasingly exploits timestep, feature, and layer structure. While recent work has begun incorporating CFG structure into diffusion quantization, activation quantization still operates independently across conditional and unconditional coordinates, leaving cross-activation structure unexploited. We show that matched CFG activations form a strongly correlated two-dimensional source and that, under a fixed bit budget, the choice of branch coding basis materially affects quantization fidelity. Motivated by this observation, we introduce branch-space transform coding ,which rotates matched CFG branches via an offline derived 2 × 2 orthogonal matrix,

We further derive the Guidance-Correlation Branch Transform (GCBT) , which jointly incorporates the CFG guidance direction and cross-branch second moments. Applied on top of existing diffusion PTQ methods, GCBT yields statistically significant fidelity gains in most evaluated comparisons with no statistically significant degradation, while leaving the underlying host quantization pipeline unchanged. 

# 1. Introduction 

Diffusion models (Ho et al., 2020; Nichol and Dhariwal, 2021; Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015; Song et al., 2021) achieve high generation quality at the cost of repeated, computationally heavy denoising steps. This has motivated extensive work on post-training quantization (PTQ) (Chen et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025, 2023; Shafi and Suma, 2026; Shao et al., 2026; Zhao et al., 2025) to reduce inference overhead via low-bit representations of weights and activations. Recent methods increasingly exploit activation structure during diffusion inference, exploiting (i) variation across denoising timesteps (Chen et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025), (ii) architectural and feature heterogeneity within the denoising model (Chen et al., 2025; Li et al., 2023), and (iii) activation outliers (Li et al., 2025, 2026). These advances raise a natural question: what additional structural information in diffusion inference can be exploited to improve activation quantization? 

One such structure is the paired computation in classifier-free guidance (CFG) (Ho and Salimans, 2022). CFG plays a central role in modern diffusion pipelines by strengthening the influence of the condition on generation. At each denoising step, CFG evaluates the same model weights with and without condition, producing matched predictions 𝑝 𝑐 and 𝑝 𝑢 and then combining them using a guidance scale 𝑠 . We denote the corresponding activations as 𝑋 𝑐 and 𝑋 𝑢 , and refer to the two-dimensional axis formed by this matched pair as the CFG branch space .

While recent work has begun incorporating CFG into post-training calibration (Shafi and Suma, 2026; Shao et al., 2026), activation quantization still typically operates on 𝑋 𝑐 and 𝑋 𝑢 independently in separate branch coordinates. This choice implicitly fixes the branch basis, overlooking the fact that both activations arise from matched CFG passes through the same model weights. 0 0.25 0.5 0.75 1.0 Uncentered branch correlation 00.5 1Fraction of linear sites  Matched Mismatched

We find two properties of this branch space that are relevant to activation quantization. First, matched 𝑋 𝑐 and 𝑋 𝑢 activations are strongly correlated, exhibiting cross-branch correlation coefficients of a median uncentered correlation of 0.86 (vs. 0.33 for mismatched pairs) across DiT-XL/2 (Peebles and Xie, 2023) layers (Figure 2). Second, the two CFG branches are coupled at the model output by the known guidance direction 𝑔 𝑠 = [𝑠, 1 − 𝑠 ]⊤, which varies with the guidance scale 𝑠 . As illustrated in Figure 3, standard PTQ quantizes activations independently along native branch axes, imposing a struc-tural mismatch between the quantization grid, principal activation variance, and the guidance vector 𝑔 𝑠 . Under a fixed activation-bit budget, this directional mismatch limits quantization fidelity. Motivated by these observations, we introduce branch-space trans-form coding . Before activation quantization, we apply a 2 × 2orthogonal branch rotation matrix ( 𝑅 ) across each matched con-ditional/unconditional pair, quantize the transformed activations on top of the existing low-bit quantizer, and invert the transform 2afterward. The procedure does not change model parameters, nominal activation precision, or the number of quantized values; it changes only the branch coordinates in which activation quantization is performed. Branch-space coding is therefore complementary to existing timestep-, feature-, layer-, and precision-aware diffusion PTQ methods. To determine the optimal rotation matrix 𝑅 , we derive the Guidance-Correlation Branch Transform (GCBT) ,which jointly incorporates the CFG guidance direction and per-layer cross-branch second moments. Under an equal-rate quantization-noise surrogate, this formulation yields a closed-form per-layer solution without gradient optimization or angle search. Across diverse diffusion PTQ backbones, models, and datasets, GCBT consistently improves reconstruction and perceptual fidelity, establishing CFG branch space as a complementary axis for activation quantization. Our work makes the following contributions: 

• CFG Branch Space as a Design Axis. To our knowledge, we are the first to treat the CFG branch axis as an explicit coding space, demonstrating that cross-branch structure between matched activations directly governs quantization fidelity. 

• Branch-Space Transform Coding & GCBT. We introduce a lightweight branch-space transform coding framework that incorporates the CFG guidance direction and cross-branch second moments into the coding basis. 

• Closed-Form Global Optimum. We derive a guidance-weighted distortion objective over 2 × 2 orthogonal rotations and prove that its global optimum admits a closed-form per-layer solution under an equal-rate quantization-noise surrogate. 

• Broad PTQ Compatibility. We apply GCBT on top of existing activation quantizers without modifying the underlying quantization method, achieving consistent improvements in reconstruction and perceptual fidelity. 

# 2. Related Work 

Post-Training Quantization for Diffusion Models. Diffusion PTQ increasingly exploits structure specific to iterative generation. Q-Diffusion (Li et al., 2023) addresses timestep-dependent activation distributions and architectural outliers, while Q-DiT (Chen et al., 2025) and ViDiT-Q (Zhao et al., 2025) further account for spatial, temporal, layer, and channel variation in Diffusion Transformers. SVDQuant (Li et al., 2025) handles activation outliers through smoothing and low-rank compensation, and DeltaQuant (Li et al., 2026) exploits spatiotemporal activation structure for low-bit video diffusion. These methods adapt quantization to diffusion-specific activation structure, but retain the native conditional/unconditional branch coordinates during activation quantization. 

Rotation-Based Quantization. Rotary quantization has been widely studied beyond diffusion models. QuaRot (Ashkboos et al., 2024) applies orthogonal rotations to reduce activation outliers, while SpinQuant (Liu et al., 2025) learns rotation matrices for improved low-bit quantization. Related methods use Hadamard-based incoherence processing (Tseng et al., 2024) or learnable linear transformations (Hu et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2025) to improve the quantizability of weights and activations. In diffusion models, TR-DQ (Shao et al., 2026) introduces timestep-dependent feature rotations, while OrbitQuant (Lee et al., 2026) quantizes in a normalized rotated basis. These approaches transform feature or hidden-state coordinates; in contrast, our transform acts across the two-dimensional CFG branch axis while leaving token and feature coordinates unchanged. 3Xc

CFG-Aware Quantization. Recent diffusion PTQ methods have also begun to exploit the relation between CFG branches. TR-DQ (Shao et al., 2026) uses redundancy across CFG executions, while guidance-aware methods such as GAMP (Shafi and Suma, 2026) calibrate quantization with respect to the guided prediction and CFG sensitivity. These methods incorporate CFG structure while retaining the native conditional/unconditional branch basis. Our work studies a complementary variable: the branch coding basis itself. We treat the CFG branch axis as an explicit quantization space and select its basis from the CFG branch direction and cross-branch second moments.

.43.2. Branch-Space Structure and Transform Coding 

Standard diffusion PTQ (Chen et al., 2025; Lee et al., 2026; Li et al., 2025, 2023, 2026; Zhao et al., 2025) quantizes CFG activations directly in the native (𝑐, 𝑢 ) branch coordinates. To determine whether activation quantization can benefit from a different branch coding basis, we examine whether matched CFG activations exhibit cross-branch structure and whether changing only the branch coding basis affects quantization fidelity under a fixed activation-bit budget. 

Observation I: CFG branches form a structured matched source. On DiT-XL/2 with W4A4 SVDQuant (CFG = 2, 64 trajectories), the median correlation between matched branch activations across all linear sites in DiT-XL/2 is 0.859 , compared with 0.328 for deliberately mismatched pairs (Figure 2, Table 6). The matched activations are also highly directional: their dominant axis lies at a median 42 .71 ◦ from the conditional axis, close to the diagonal 𝑐 = 𝑢 . Figure 3(a) illustrates this structure schematically. These measurements motivate considering the matched activations jointly when choosing a quantization basis. 

Observation II: Activation-quantization fidelity depends on the branch basis. Under the same activation-bit budget and host activation quantizer, changing only the branch coding basis produces measurable differences in generation fidelity. At CFG = 4.5, a guidance-aligned rotation improves PSNR and SSIM while reducing LPIPS under the same host quantizer and activation-bit budget; see Table 4.

4.2. Main Results 

Image generation. Across the full image-generation matrix, GCBT yields statistically significant gains in most paired metric comparisons, while no comparison shows a statistically significant degradation. All statistically resolved effects therefore favor GCBT. The significant gains span both SVDQuant models, Q-DiT on PixArt-Σ,and the larger SD3.5-Large model, consistently across MJHQ and sDCI. The remaining comparisons are statistically inconclusive, with no evidence of a systematic regression. Overall, these results show that the benefits of GCBT transfer across architectures, PTQ hosts, datasets, and model scales. 

Video generation. The video experiments extend this pattern beyond image generation and, more importantly, beyond teacher-referenced reconstruction alone. Across Wan2.1, CogVideoX, and OpenSora, GCBT improves both FP-referenced fidelity and VBench generation-quality metrics. We isolate the design choices underlying GCBT and examine its robustness and systems cost. Unless stated otherwise, ablations use PixArt-Σ with SVDQuant W4A4 on the fixed 𝑛 = 256 MJHQ subset at CFG = 4.5, with 64 calibration samples disjoint from evaluation.

Full statistics are reported in Appendix B.3. 

Adaptation across guidance scales. Because GCBT optimizes its basis using the CFG branch direction 𝑔 𝑠 , we evaluate its behavior across varying guidance scales while holding all other configurations fixed. As illustrated in Figure 5, GCBT exhibits a clear scale-dependent pattern: its fidelity gains over the host quantizer grow monotonically as guidance becomes stronger. At weak guidance ( 𝑠 = 2), the perceptual improvement is marginal; however, from 𝑠 = 3 onward, GCBT consistently strengthens performance across LPIPS, SSIM, and PSNR. This trend directly reflects the role of 𝑔 𝑠 in our objective function: as stronger CFG alters the guidance readout orientation, adapting the branch coding basis becomes increasingly consequential for preserving activation fidelity.

# 5. Conclusion 

We identify CFG branch space as an overlooked coding dimension for diffusion activation quantization and introduce GCBT, a closed-form transform that jointly exploits matched-branch geometry and guidance sensitivity. Across many settings, GCBT consistently yields favorable fidelity and generation-quality improvements without modifying model parameters or the underlying quantization pipeline. For each of the 168 activation quantizers (dynamic group-64 A4), we fit one positive clipping multiplier in [0.1, 2.0] by minimizing the MSE between the quantized and full-precision guided noise predictions, 𝜖 𝑢 + 𝑠 (𝜖 𝑐 − 𝜖 𝑢 ) with 𝑠 = 4.5. We use 1,024 paired states from 64 training prompts and 256 states from 16 validation prompts, 800 Adam updates (learning rate 0 .01, batch size one), and keep the checkpoint with the lowest validation error. The calibrated host is then frozen, and GCBT angles are computed from 64 complete Native trajectories on this host. Host-training, validation, GCBT-calibration, and evaluation prompts are disjoint. This study uses a separate execution setup from Table 2: BF16 execution and a BF16 teacher, with initial noise drawn from a CPU generator. Evaluation uses the same 256 MJHQ and 256 sDCI prompts and seeds (1024 ×1024, 20 DPM-Solver++ steps), but the different dtype and noise generator yield different teacher images. Absolute values are therefore not comparable across the two tables; the evidence is the paired Host versus +GCBT comparison under an identical host, prompts, seeds, and teacher. As shown in Table 7, the paired intervals are entirely favorable for LPIPS and SSIM on both datasets, whereas the PSNR intervals overlap zero. Paired evaluation protocol. For video, we use a fixed 𝑛 = 256 subset of VBench (Huang et al., 2024), stratified by category. On the absolute LPIPS values for Wan. The relatively high BF16-referenced LPIPS on Wan is not specific to GCBT: it is already present in the OrbitQuant baseline. In video diffusion, quantization can shift the sampling trajectory enough to produce a different yet still prompt-consistent realization, making frame-aligned distance to a single BF16 sample large even when visual quality remains good. Figure 7 illustrates this behavior. Accordingly, we interpret LPIPS here as BF16-reference fidelity rather than standalone generation quality, and report VBench metrics alongside it. 

# C. Limitations 

GCBT has several scope limitations. First, its closed-form optimality is derived under the equal-rate quantization-noise surrogate in Sec. 3.3, which assumes that transformed-coordinate quantization error scales with projected second moments and neglects cross-coordinate error terms. The resulting rotation is therefore optimal for this surrogate, rather than being guaranteed to minimize the exact distortion of every host quantizer or end-to-end generation error. Second, GCBT requires an explicit matched conditional–unconditional branch pair and is therefore directly applicable to inference pipelines with active two-branch CFG. We do not study distilled or single-pass guidance schemes, nor timestep-varying or dynamic CFG schedules. Finally, our CFG-aware comparison uses a GAMP-inspired calibration that we implemented ourselves because no public implementation of GAMP is available.
