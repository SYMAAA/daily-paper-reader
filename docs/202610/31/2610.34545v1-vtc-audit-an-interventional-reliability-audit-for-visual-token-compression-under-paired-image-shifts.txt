# VTC-AUDIT: An Interventional Reliability Audit for Visual-Token Compression under Paired Image Shifts 

## Isaac(Rucheng) Li University of Pittsburgh 

## isl74@pitt.edu 

## Abstract 

Visual-token compression is usually evaluated by clean accu-racy and efficiency, but these averages do not reveal failures that compression introduces under an image shift where the uncompressed model stays correct. We introduce VTC-AUDIT, which estimates this incremental risk only on pairs where the full model is correct on both images and the com-pressed model is correct on the clean image. The four arms then cross two binary choices: which image selects the re-tained token positions, and which image supplies the visual features. We audit three open 8–9B vision-language mod-els on four tasks and eight pre-specified shifts. We evalu-ate two training-free compressors—hard-pruning DivPrune and prune-and-merge ApET—at a matched 25% retained-position budget, producing 708,000 inference records. Rese-lecting positions on the shifted image, rather than reusing the clean-image positions, raises the failure rate in all six model– compressor settings. The increase is 3.04–9.60 percentage points for DivPrune and 2.05–4.20 percentage points for ApET, with every 95% interval above zero. Repeating the audit on a separately resampled second manifest reproduces all six increases. In a pooled monitor audit across the four benchmarks, support Jaccard instability fails the practi-cal AUROC criterion of 0.65 in all six settings, whereas clean compressed-model uncertainty clears it. Thus, support turnover is a weak ranking signal under the audited bench-mark mixture, while clean compressed-model uncertainty is substantially more informative. These results suggest that future visual-token compression evaluations should report conditional reliability alongside clean accuracy and effi-ciency. 

## 1. Introduction 

Large vision-language models (VLMs) process images as sequences of visual tokens, often hundreds or thousands per input. Training-free visual-token compression reduces this cost while preserving much of clean-task accuracy [1– 3]. However, clean accuracy and efficiency do not reveal whether compression creates new failures under image shifts that the uncompressed model still handles correctly. We condition on pairs where the full model is correct on both images and compression succeeds on the clean image, defining the population on which strict compression-specific failure rate (SCFR) is measured. Image shifts can affect both visual features and which visual-token indices a compressor retains. We refer to the set of retained indices as the support . VTC-AUDIT there-fore forms a four-arm intervention by crossing the image used to select the support with the image supplying the vi-sual features. The dynamic–frozen contrast measures the additional failure from selecting support on the shifted im-age rather than reusing the clean-image support, while the clean-feature counterfactual tests whether shift-selected sup-port remains harmful when applied to clean features. We also ask whether larger changes in the retained-index set are more predictive of failure, using Jaccard instability and clean compressed-model uncertainty as two simple predictors. Our audit combines paired full-model conditioning, four-arm support/feature interventions, and per-example failure prediction under non-adversarial image shifts. We evaluate this protocol on two text-agnostic compressors with different operators: DivPrune discards unselected tokens, while ApET merges their information into the retained positions. Across three open 8–9B VLMs, four tasks, eight image shifts, and both compressors, the audit shows a consistent four-arm pattern: dynamic and clean-feature-counterfactual failure rates are close to each other and well above frozen-support failure. Reselecting positions on the shifted image, rather than reusing the clean-image positions, raises the fail-ure rate in every model–compressor setting. Across the three models the increase spans 3.04–9.60 percentage points for DivPrune and 2.05–4.20 percentage points for ApET, ev-ery 95% interval excludes zero, and a separately resampled second manifest reproduces all six increases. Moreover, 74.8–81.6% of DivPrune dynamic failures and 55.6–64.1% of ApET dynamic failures persist after restoring clean fea-tures, showing substantial but compressor-dependent support 1arXiv:2610.34545v1 [cs.CV] 25 Oct 2026 sensitivity. Support movement itself is nevertheless a weak per-example failure signal. On the primary seed-42 sample, pooled across the four benchmarks, the orientation-free Jac-card AUROC bound is at most 0.605 for DivPrune and 0.592 for ApET, both below the pre-specified 0.65 target on every model. Clean compressed-model uncertainty is substantially stronger, with AUROC 0.705–0.844 across the two com-pressors. These results motivate conditional reliability as a complement to clean accuracy and efficiency. Code, manifests, and experimental outputs are available at 

https://github.com/IsaacLi74/VTC-AUDIT .

## 2. Related Work 

Visual-token compression. DivPrune greedily maximizes diversity among retained visual tokens [1]; ApET ranks tokens by linear approximation error and merges removed in-formation into retained positions [2]. Both are text-agnostic: neither conditions selection on the user instruction. CD-Pruner instead conditions selection on the instruction via a determinantal point process [3], and is outside our text-agnostic scope. These methods are primarily evaluated through clean accuracy–efficiency trade-offs. 

Compression evaluation and failure analysis. VTC-Bench filters existing benchmarks for examples that better distinguish visual-token compression methods [4], while LLMC+ standardizes comparisons across models, tasks, and compressors [5]. Acceleration-induced prediction instability under input perturbations is examined in [6]. Wang et al. show that existing training-free pruning methods can per-form no better than random pruning in deeper VLM layers, attributing this degradation to vanishing visual-token infor-mation [7]. VTC-AUDIT instead asks a different reliability question: when the full model remains correct, does com-pression introduce a new failure under a non-adversarial image shift, and through which support/feature pathway? 

Compression-specific robustness. Compression-aware at-tacks show that token-ranking instability can induce fail-ures specific to compressed inference [8], and that robust-ness evaluated without compression is overly optimistic [9]. A concurrent provenance audit examines whether retained tokens remain spatially traceable to supporting evidence [10]. Compression-specific adversarial failures and counter-factual support reallocation are studied in [11]. We study non-adversarial paired image shifts instead, conditioning on full-model correctness and localizing compression-specific failures through support/feature interventions.

We evaluate ranking ability by AUROC over the pooled eligi-ble configurations from all four benchmarks, using the same benchmark-stratified image-cluster bootstrap for 95% inter-vals. As a model-state comparator, we use clean compressed-model uncertainty, defined as the negative mean token log-probability of the clean compressed answer. The monitor therefore evaluates ranking performance under the audited benchmark mixture, complementing the population-level four-arm contrasts. We apply the same monitor analysis to both compres-sors, treating DivPrune as the pre-specified confirmatory setting and ApET as a descriptive replication. We require at least 100 positive image clusters and an AUROC interval width of at most 0.15, and pre-specify 0.65 as the practi-cal AUROC target. This threshold is a practical decision criterion rather than a universal AUROC standard, so we report the continuous AUROC estimates and their full 95% intervals alongside the threshold-based conclusions. Jaccard instability has no assumed direction, so for a raw AUROC in-terval [L, H ] we use the conservative orientation-free upper bound U± = max( H, 1 − L). We consider Jaccard practi-cally uninformative when U± < 0.65 . For clean uncertainty, the direction is pre-specified—higher uncertainty indicates higher risk—so it clears the target only when its lower 95% bound exceeds 0.65.

Audit-specific ApET instantiation. ApET originally ap-plies compression at two stages: after the visual encoder and again inside the LLM [2]. We use only the post-projector stage, where clean and shifted representations have aligned visual-token grids, so a support selected from one image can be applied to the corresponding token positions of the other. We retain ApET’s farthest-point sampling (FPS) for basis selection and its similarity-based average merging. To make FPS deterministic, we choose its initial point using a hash of the record UID, with the same initialization for each clean–shift pair. In the frozen arm, the retained indices are fixed to those selected on the clean image, while removed-to-retained merge assignments are recomputed from the current arm’s features. Because ApET merges information from removed tokens whereas DivPrune discards them, the shared 25% retained-position budget does not imply equal retained information. 5.1. Performance and eligibility 

Table 1 summarizes model performance and eligibility. At the model-level aggregate, the shifts minimally affect full-model performance: the largest clean-to-shifted mean-score change is 0.65 percentage points. Compression is more task-dependent, preserving nearly all POPE and most GQA performance, with ChartQA generally showing the largest degradation; on InternVL, the clean ChartQA score falls from 78.00% for the full model to 29.80% with DivPrune and 33.00% with ApET. The compressors also differ by task: ApET yields higher clean ChartQA scores on all three models but lower TextVQA scores, especially for Qwen and InternVL. Eligibility remains substantial, ranging from 49.26% to 68.39% across model–compressor settings. 

5.2. Reselecting support consistently increases SCFR 

Table 2 reports the three non-clean arms; the clean arm is omitted because eligibility requires full credit on it. Dynamic SCFR exceeds frozen SCFR in all six model–compressor settings, by 3.04–9.60 percentage points for DivPrune and 2.05–4.20 percentage points for ApET, with every 95% inter-val above zero. The largest effects occur on InternVL, where SCFR falls from 14.24% to 4.63% for DivPrune and from 9.88% to 5.68% for ApET. Table 3 examines the same reselection effect while re-taining partial benchmark credit. Unlike strict SCFR, this analysis uses score loss 1 − σ, reported as a percentage, for every eligible example and reports the average dynamic and frozen losses together with their difference ∆score . Across all tasks, dynamic loss exceeds frozen loss in all six model– compressor settings, with every 95% interval for ∆score 

above zero. Because TextVQA is the only graded bench-mark, we also report it separately: all six point estimates are positive, and five of six intervals exclude zero. 

5.3. Shift-selected support remains harmful on clean features 

Table 2 shows substantial configuration-level persistence: 74.8–81.6% of DivPrune dynamic failures and 55.6–64.1% of ApET dynamic failures also fail in the counterfactual arm after clean features are restored. Persistence is lower for ApET, meaning that a smaller fraction of its dynamic failures remain failures after restoring clean features. 

5.4. The reselection effect is strongly task dependent 

The reselection effect varies sharply across tasks. In the pre-specified DivPrune audit, Table 4 shows that ChartQA has the largest positive ∆u,m across all three models, while POPE is consistently much less sensitive. Averaging first across the four shift-family cells within each model and then across the three models, DivPrune has dynamic/frozen SCFR of 21.86%/7.20% on ChartQA, compared with 2.23%/1.33% on POPE. ApET shows the same contrast, with 15.00%/8.55% on ChartQA versus 1.35%/1.03% on POPE. Qwen is an exception: its largest ApET reselection effects occur on TextVQA rather than ChartQA. The effect is also broad across transformations. Averag-ing equally across the 12 model–task combinations, ∆u,m is positive for JPEG, Gaussian noise, blur, and brightness un-der both compressors, ranging from 4.71 to 6.37 percentage points for DivPrune and from 2.35 to 3.31 percentage points for ApET. Of the 48 task–shift-family cells per compressor, 47 are positive for DivPrune; ApET has 41 positive, three zero, and four negative cells. The negative cells are small in magnitude: the minimum is −0.62 percentage points, com-pared with a maximum positive contrast of 31.75 percentage points for InternVL–DivPrune on ChartQA under brightness.

## 6. Discussion 

The results separate two questions about support sensitiv-ity. Reselecting support after an image shift consistently increases SCFR and score loss, yet the amount of support turnover is a weak predictor of which individual configura-tions fail. The population-level effect of reselection therefore does not translate into a useful per-example signal based on set-level turnover alone. This suggests that support sensi-tivity depends on more than the magnitude of the support change. 7The counterfactual arm provides complementary evidence about the role of shift-selected support. A substantial fraction of dynamic failures remain failures after clean visual features are restored, so these failures cannot be attributed to shifted features alone. Persistence differs between the two audited compression operators, with consistently lower values for ApET than for DivPrune. More generally, the same amount of support change can have different effects under different compression operators. DivPrune discards removed tokens, whereas ApET merges their information into the retained positions, so support overlap alone does not fully describe how the compressed representation changes. These findings broaden what should be measured when evaluating visual-token compression. The reselection effect appears across tasks and shift types, but its magnitude varies substantially across tasks and with shift severity. Clean performance alone therefore does not reveal whether com-pression introduces new failures under input changes that the uncompressed model still handles correctly. Reporting con-ditional reliability alongside accuracy and efficiency makes this failure mode visible. These findings suggest two practical directions. First, con-ditional reliability should be characterized across compres-sion budgets rather than at a single operating point. Second, the dynamic–frozen gap provides a concrete reliability target for support-stabilization and adaptive-retention mechanisms; the frozen arm is diagnostic rather than a proposed deploy-ment policy. The weak performance of set-level Jaccard further motivates predictors that distinguish which retained positions change, rather than treating all support changes equally. 

## 7. Limitations 

Conditional estimand. Eligibility conditions on success-ful clean compressed inference under Sm(Vu), so the frozen arm reuses a support already known to succeed on that con-figuration. Thus, ∆u,m characterizes the dynamic–frozen gap within this clean-successful population, but does not separate shift-specific reselection from support replacement more generally. 

Empirical coverage and compression budget. Our em-pirical scope covers three open 8–9B VLMs, two training-free text-agnostic compressors, four benchmarks, and four non-adversarial shift families. The main audit uses a single 25% retained-position operating point. Exploratory 50%-retention runs on a subset of configurations preserve the qualitative direction of the main contrast, but we do not perform a systematic sweep over retention ratios. The de-pendence of the reselection effect on compression budget is therefore not characterized. 

Shift validation and intervention applicability. We man-ually inspected 100 randomly sampled examples per task from the seed-42 benchmark sample to verify that the ap-plied shifts preserved task semantics. Nearly all inspected shifted examples remained human-answerable with the orig-inal reference answer. However, this check covered only 100 of the 500 examples in each task and was not repeated for the independently resampled seed-2026 benchmark sample. Semantic preservation is therefore not exhaustively verified across the full evaluation set. The four-arm intervention also relies on a shared clean–shift token index space. Geometry-changing transformations or pre-projector compression set-tings without aligned token grids would require an explicit correspondence between the two support spaces. 

ApET instantiation. Our ApET audit uses only its post-projector compression stage, rather than the original multi-stage operating point, to provide the aligned support space required by the intervention. 

Monitor scope. The monitor audit evaluates Jaccard sup-port instability and clean compressed-model uncertainty, with AUROC pooled across the four benchmarks. It there-fore characterizes ranking under the audited benchmark mix-ture; task-specific monitor behavior is not analyzed sepa-rately. We also do not evaluate alternative support-based failure signals beyond set-level overlap. 

## 8. Conclusion 

We introduced VTC-AUDIT, a paired interventional audit for evaluating compression-specific reliability under image shifts. By conditioning on cases where the uncompressed model remains correct and clean compressed inference suc-ceeds, the audit focuses on failures that emerge under com-pressed inference despite preserved full-model correctness. Across three VLMs and two training-free compressors at a 25% retained-position budget, reselecting support on the shifted image consistently increases SCFR relative to reusing the clean-image support, and many of these failures persist after clean visual features are restored. The audit also reveals a clear distinction between population-level reliability and per-example ranking. Al-though support reselection increases failure on average, sup-port turnover is a weak ranking signal in the pooled moni-tor audit, whereas clean compressed-model uncertainty is substantially more informative under the same benchmark mixture. Both conclusions reproduce on a separately resam-pled benchmark sample.
