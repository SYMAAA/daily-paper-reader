NJU, 2Jiutian AI, 3CASIA The rapid growth of video data has created an urgent need for efficient video understanding sys-tems that can handle long and continuous visual inputs, posing significant challenges for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) due to severe memory bottlenecks. Moreover, existing compression methods often overlook the explosive accumulation of approximation errors. To address this issue, we propose Video-MemComp , a training-free and sensitivity-aware context management framework for memory-constrained streaming video understanding. Our method theoretically decomposes com-pression error into orthogonal local approximation error and layer-wise structural sensitivity via a first-order Taylor expansion. To minimize local approximation error , we perform token aggregation in a pre-RoPE safe zone with an attention-conserving logit bias, mitigating geometric distortions caused by RoPE rotations. To manage layer-wise structural sensitivity , we dynamically allocate the KV cache budget based on offline gradient priors and online context entropy, prioritizing high-sensitivity layers. Video-MemComp outperforms or achieves performance comparable to full-cache and state-of-the-art compression methods on benchmarks including MLVU, VideoMME, and EgoSchema. Notably, it improves the overall accuracy of Qwen2.5-VL-3B from 62.3% to 64.1% while reducing the KV cache to an equivalent of 6,000 tokens per layer, saving over 90% of KV memory.

## 1 Introduction 

As Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) evolve from static image understanding to long-form stream-ing video reasoning, they exhibit strong emergent capabilities in temporal reasoning and narrative comprehen-sion OpenAI (2023); Liu et al. (2023); Bai et al. (2025b). However, the paradigm is quickly shifting towards real-time, continuous multimedia applications, such as AR/VR smart glasses, embodied agents, and intelli-gent surveillance systems. In these streaming video scenarios, video assistants must continuously perceive infinite visual streams while enabling seamless, low-latency human-computer interaction. This imposes a severe conflict: the streaming multimedia input is temporally unbounded, yet the system’s computational re-sources and GPU memory are strictly finite. A fatal system bottleneck emerges as the Key-Value (KV) cache grows linearly with the sequence length ( O(T )), making continuous real-time inference highly susceptible to Out-Of-Memory failures, especially on resource-constrained edge devices or consumer-grade GPUs Shao et al. (2025b); Ning et al. (2025); Chen et al. (2025). To alleviate memory pressure, recent works have explored memory compression techniques, including heuristic pruning and token merging. However, these approaches exhibit limitations in addressing the error accumula-tion mechanism, where minute approximation errors in specific layers (typically shallow ones) can amplify in the final output. Specifically, pruning methods, whether utilizing static budgets Zhang et al. (2023); Li et al. (2024b) or adaptive allocation based on information density Shen et al. (2025); Feng et al. (2024), often fail to fully account for the non-uniform error distribution across layers. Conversely, merging strategies Bolya

3.1 Problem Reformulation via Taylor Expansion 

Under the constraint of maintaining O(1) -memory overhead, directly optimizing the global output error 

∥∆O∥ is intractable due to the high-dimensional non-linearity of the model. To derive a feasible surrogate, we shift our analytical focus to the attention layer outputs A(l), which serve as the direct interface for KV cache compression. Here, L represents the total number of layers in the model and l ∈ { 0, . . . , L − 1} denotes the layer index. We define A = {A(0) , . . . , A (L−1) } as the collection of the states. By abstracting the model output as a multivariate function O = G(A), the error ∥∆O∥ can be characterized layer-wise. When compression is applied, it introduces a local perturbation ∆A(l) at the l-th layer. Assuming the perturbations are sufficiently small, we can linearize the global output deviation ∥∆O∥ using a first-order multivariate Taylor expansion (see Appendix B for details):

The local approximation error El = ∥∆A(l)∥ indicates that compression must be optimized locally within a specific layer to preserve semantic fidelity. Layers with high Sl (e.g., shallow layers) propagate errors more strongly and therefore require a larger share of the total memory budget to suppress the global error bound. 

3.2 Minimizing Local Approximation Error El 

> Pre-RoPE Aggregation.

To reduce El, we build upon prior KV cache compression methods Yang et al. (2025) but fundamentally rethink redundancy handling. Since the injection of Rotary Positional Embeddings (RoPE) entangles positional information with semantic similarity during the prefill stage, merging within the KV cache becomes geometrically unreliable. Therefore, we shift similarity-based aggregation to the Pre-RoPE stage. Furthermore, we compensate for the attention score shift caused by token aggregation, thereby minimizing 

El as much as possible. Based on the principle of attention conservation, we merge similar tokens and subsequently correct the attention scores to ensure faithful token aggregation (see Appendix B.3.1 for details). Our goal is to minimize information loss during compression. Considering that the Transformer’s core self-attention mechanism fundamentally measures relevance via the dot product between Query and Key vectors, which induces a directional matching bias, we adopt cosine similarity as the metric for redundancy. Intuitively, if two tokens share the same direction in feature space, their response patterns to any Query will be highly similar. We provide a mathematical justification for this choice in Appendix C.1. To eliminate similarity redundancy at the input, we design a three-stage process (Algorithm 1). We first leverage temporal inertia to filter out inter-frame static redundancy. Subsequently, for intra-frame spatial redundancy, we employ an iterative bipartite matching strategy. Most critically, to prevent the Attention Dilution caused by heuristic merging, we track the effective fusion count fi for each surviving token (repre-senting the number of raw tokens it encapsulates) and inject a logit bias correction Bi = log( fi) into the attention logits. ## 4 Experiments 

4.1 Experimental Setup  

> Benchmarks and Evaluation.

We evaluate Video-MemComp on a comprehensive suite of offline and streaming benchmarks. For offline datasets, we use VideoMME Fu et al. (2025), MLVU Zhou et al. (2025), and EgoSchema Mangalam et al. (2023) following the online streaming evaluation protocol. Following prior work Di et al. (2025); Yang et al. (2025); Kim et al. (2025), results are reported on the official development sets. ## 5 Conclusion 

In this work, we present Video-MemComp , a training-free, model-agnostic, and structure-aware hybrid frame-work for infinite streaming video understanding under a strict O(1) memory budget. Moving beyond heuristic pruning, we revisit KV cache compression through the lens of error propagation. Using a first-order Taylor expansion as an analytical surrogate, we decompose the global output error into two orthogonal components: 

Local Approximation Error (El) and Layer-wise Structural Sensitivity (Sl), revealing the mechanism of non-uniform error propagation in MLLMs. Guided by this insight, Video-MemComp reduces El via Pre-RoPE aggregation that bypasses RoPE-induced geometric constraints, and regulates Sl through gradient-guided dynamic budget allocation. Extensive experiments demonstrate a counter-intuitive “less is more” effect: by distilling high-density information, our method matches or surpasses full-cache baselines with minimal mem-ory. This work offers a scalable solution for long-form video understanding on resource-constrained devices and provides a principled perspective on memory optimization. 

Limitations and Future Work. Our Taylor-based decomposition is heuristic and may break down under extreme compression where local perturbation assumptions no longer hold. The structural sensitivity prior relies on offline calibration and may degrade under distribution shifts, while Pre-RoPE aggregation introduces moderate overhead. Future work will explore validating higher-order approximations, developing online or adaptive sensitivity estimation, and designing more efficient aggregation for ultra-long streams.

D.3 Robust Logarithmic Surrogate 

Although Eq. (33) provides a theoretical optimum, directly using the linear product p · ∥ v∥ as a scoring function is numerically unstable. This is attributed to the Distributional Heterogeneity of the MLLM feature space. 

Statistical Instability. LLM activations typically follow a significant heavy-tailed distribution. Outlier layers may have ∥v∥ magnitudes orders of magnitude higher than the average. If budgets are allocated directly based on the linear upper bound, these outlier layers will trigger a Winner-takes-all effect, hijacking the vast majority of the budget and causing layer collapse in semantically critical but numerically smaller layers. Furthermore, model performance error sensitivity typically exhibits non-linear saturation. To address this, we transform the optimization objective from minimizing linear error upper bound to max-imizing logarithmic utility. Specifically, we introduce a Variance-Stabilizing Transformation as an empirical heuristic. The choice of the logarithmic function ensures diminishing returns for overly dense regions, thereby yielding a robust Pareto frontier. We construct the dynamic surrogate metric Mdyn as follows:

In this section, we provide detailed experimental settings and numerical data for the compression strategy performance comparison graph (Figure 5), and explain our adopted compression method from an information-theoretic view.  

> E.2.1 Experimental Setup and Hyperparameters

Experiments were conducted using the Qwen2.5-VL-3B Bai et al. (2025b) model on the VideoMME Fu et al. (2025) long video benchmark. We compared three strategies: 1. KV Cache Compression Only: Prunes the KV cache using only sparsity metrics (Attention Sparsity). 2. Two-Stage Compression (Hybrid Strategy): Reduces input tokens via Inter-Frame Filtering and Intra-Frame Aggregation. We set the inter-frame similarity threshold τinter = 0 .25 and the default intra-frame merge threshold τintra = 0 .75 . The only exception is under extremely low budgets (KV cache size = 500), where we relax τintra to 0.70 to accommodate strict capacity limits, followed by streaming prefill and KV cache compression if the budget limit is exceeded. 3. Token Compression Only: Uses the same token compression strategy as the Hybrid Strategy . Subse-quently, if limits are exceeded, the remaining tokens for each frame are proportionally compressed to fit exactly into a window of the same size as the KV cache using our intra-frame token compression algorithm.  

> E.2.2 Detailed Performance Data

> E.2.3 Results Analysis

Analyzing Table 8 and the trend graph leads to the following key observations: 

Saturation Effect of KV Cache Compression: The method relying solely on KV cache compression (Orange line) is robust at low budgets, but performance growth stagnates as the budget increases ( > 3000 ), stabilizing near the level of the original model (Original Model, 50.56%). This indicates that while retaining critical KV pairs via sparsity maintains performance, it struggles to break the context processing bottleneck of the original model. 

Performance Gains from Token Compression: In contrast, the Token Compression strategy (Blue line) exhibits superior performance at higher budgets, significantly surpassing the original model (e.g., reaching 52.89% at a budget of 12000). We attribute this to two factors: • Redundancy Removal and Dynamic Capture: The inter-frame filtering mechanism effectively removes static redundancy, allowing the model to focus on dynamic changes in the video. • Attention Concentration: Through intra-frame merging and subsequent attention score correction, we shorten the effective context length. This avoids the Attention Dilution problem common in long sequences, enabling the model to capture key cues more sharply, thus achieving accuracy superior to full input in long video understanding tasks. Ultimately, our Two-Stage Hybrid method (Green line) combines the robustness of KV cache compression with the high information density of token compression, achieving an excellent performance trade-off across the entire budget range. 26 E.2.4 An Information-Theoretic Perspective 

From an information-theoretic perspective, our hybrid framework can be interpreted as an effective Joint Source-Channel Coding scheme: • Token Compression (Source Coding): Exploits data autocorrelation (similarity) to perform lossless (or low-distortion) information aggregation via feature reconstruction and logit correction, maximizing the information entropy density of input tokens. • KV Cache Compression (Channel Allocation): Exploits attention sparsity to dynamically allocate limited storage channel capacity (KV cache budget) to features with high signal-to-noise ratios (high-sensitivity layers, high-activation tokens). Unlike the explicit fusion in Token Compression, this is a Selective Memory mechanism based on importance evaluation. The experimental results in Figure 5 corroborate this theory: token compression alone preserves more com-plete information at high budgets (Blue curve), while KV cache compression alone is more robust at screening key information at low budgets (Orange curve). Our hybrid method (Green curve) combines the advantages of both, achieving a superior performance-memory trade-off across the full budget range.
