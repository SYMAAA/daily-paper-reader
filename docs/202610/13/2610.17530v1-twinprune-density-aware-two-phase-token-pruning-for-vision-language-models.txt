Abstract
We propose a unified spatial-density signal that scores token importance for both
phases of inference, instantiated as a bipartite merge during prefill and a KV evic-
tion during decode, with measurable gains in each phase. To verify the two phases
independently, we partition benchmarks into two diagnostic roles by their scoring
rule. Prefill probes such as MMBench, POPE, and ScienceQA score only the first
decoded token, so their accuracy reflects only the argmax of prefill logits, computed
before any decode-phase KV eviction takes effect. Full-pipeline probes such as
TextVQA, DocVQA, and MM-Vet score the full decoded sequence and therefore
reveal both phases. We expose a fundamental evaluation flaw: published claims of
decode-phase token pruning validated only on prefill probes have measured prefill
quality, not decode quality. Empirically, removing all visual KV at decode leaves
prefill-probe accuracy essentially unchanged while collapsing TextVQA and MM-
Vet by tens of points, and the insensitivity is a property of the scoring rule, not of the
compression strength. Across seven model variants from the InternVL-3.5, Qwen3-

Introduction
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) now drive a fast-growing set of applications such as document
understanding, embodied agents, multi-step visual reasoning, and GUI control. Each of these demands
processing hundreds to thousands of visual tokens through tens of transformer layers. As image
resolution and tile count rise, two efficiency bottlenecks tighten in tandem. The first is prefill compute,
dominated by encoding all visual tokens through the language model. The second is decode memory,
dominated by the per-layer key/value cache that must persist for autoregressive generation. Whether
a model can be deployed at all, whether on a memory-constrained device, in a low-latency interactive
setting, or under a high-throughput serving regime, is increasingly determined less by how the model
is trained and more by how aggressively redundant visual tokens can be removed at inference time.
The token-pruning literature has expanded rapidly in response. Prefill-time methods such as FastV [6],
ToMe [4], DivPrune [1], and DART [28] target the compute axis, while decode-time methods such as
H2O [33], StreamingLLM [29], and SnapKV [13] target the memory axis. These works converge on
a striking shared finding: aggressive compression preserves accuracy almost perfectly, often within a
fraction of a point of the uncompressed baseline. The implication, repeated across dozens of papers,
is that visual tokens are deeply redundant and can be cut without harm. If that picture were correct,
token pruning would be a solved problem, a routine deployment knob rather than an open question.
We argue this consensus rests on a measurement artifact. The benchmarks most commonly used to
validate VLM pruning are dominated by single-token outputs such as a multiple-choice letter or a
yes/no answer. Their score depends only on the first decoded logit, which is fixed by the prefill pass
before any decode-phase KV eviction is executed. By construction, decode-phase eviction therefore
cannot move those scores, so what is measured under a “decode-phase” label is in fact prefill-phase
quality. Whether a method preserves behavior under longer generation, in which information has to be
retrieved from the cache during decode, is a separate question, and it is not the one those benchmarks
answer. The same critique applies in a milder form to prefill compression: prefill-probe scores see
only the argmax shift, not the distributional damage that compression does to longer continuations.
We make the gap concrete by running a VLM with all visual KV removed at decode time. On
the prefill-probe benchmarks (MMBench, POPE, ScienceQA) the accuracy is nearly unchanged.
On full-pipeline-probe benchmarks such as TextVQA and MM-Vet, accuracy drops sharply, and
prediction strings flip on a large fraction of samples (Tab. 1, Fig. 2, Sec. 3). The model is changing
its behavior under compression, but the prefill-probe benchmarks simply cannot register that change.
This re-scopes what an honest unified compression framework should look like and what it should
be evaluated on. We propose a spatial-density signal ρ that scores each visual token by the local
non-redundancy of its w×w neighborhood on the regular spatial grid, computed once at an early
decoder layer from token-to-token cosine distances. The same ρ drives both compression phases.
During prefill, we deploy it inside a density-tilted farthest-point recurrence that combines k-center
coverage with density priority through a single tilt scalar β, bipartitions the visual tokens, and merges
the discarded-token features into the kept set before passing the reduced sequence forward. During
decode, the same ρ values are reused as a top-k score for visual-KV eviction, with no new attention
pattern computed. The two phases can be applied independently or jointly, and we supply a three-axis
(compute, memory, accuracy) Pareto study across seven model variants and three benchmarks.

Empirically, the unified density signal is competitive on the prefill accuracy frontier and is roughly
two orders of magnitude faster in token selection at high resolution, and the implementation, with a
Triton-fused farthest-point inner loop and a cached CUDA graph, delivers a 3.24× end-to-end prefill
speedup at the most aggressive operating point on InternVL-3.5-8B. On the decode side, density
top-k KV eviction wins every TextVQA and MM-Vet cell at three keep ratios against H2O, SnapKV,
and StreamingLLM, with the gap widening to 9.6 percentage points over H2O at 12.5% keep on
TextVQA and holding 7 to 10pp across the InternVL-3.5 series from 2B to 38B. Compression cost
does not grow with model size in the 8B-to-38B regime. Our contributions are as follows:
1. Methodology finding (Sec. 3): prefill-probe benchmarks (those scoring only the first
decoded token) structurally cannot measure decode-phase compression. We provide the
proof, an extended cross-architecture mismatch table, and a recommendation for how the
field should evaluate decode-phase claims going forward.
2. Unified density framework (Sec. 4): a single spatial-density signal that scores token
importance for both compression phases, with one kernel and two deployment points,
guiding bipartite merge during prefill and top-k eviction during decode. We also derive a
coreset-theoretic justification (Sec. 4.1) showing why the same ranking serves both phases.
3. Three-axis Pareto study (Sec. 5 and Sec. 6): across seven model variants and three
full-pipeline-probe benchmarks, density-based prefill compression matches the strongest
accuracy frontier while scaling linearly in the visual-token count and selecting tokens an
order of magnitude faster at high resolution.
4. Cross-architecture and cross-scale robustness (Appendix P): a structural risk taxonomy
explaining why pure-selection methods catastrophically fail on Qwen3-VL-4B’s multi-
axis RoPE while merge-based density does not, together with the empirical finding that
compression cost does not grow with model size across the InternVL-3.5 from 2B to 38B.

Related Work
Prefill-stage compression.
FastV [6] drops visual tokens by attention score after a chosen layer.
ToMe [4] performs bipartite token merging for ViTs and was adapted to VLMs. DivPrune [1]
uses farthest-point sampling on visual features for diversity-driven selection. DART [28] combines
L1-norm pivots with cosine-distance expansion. VisionZip [30] selects dominant tokens by CLS-
attention and merges the rest by key-state similarity. TokenPacker [11] introduces a coarse-to-fine
visual projector that compresses tokens by 75 to 89 percent before the LLM backbone. AvgPool,
FitPrune [31], and PruMerge [23] round out a crowded landscape, and concurrent preprints continue
to appear, including SAINT [9]. All evaluate primarily on the four-benchmark set we critique below.
Decode-stage KV eviction.
For text-only LLMs, H2O [33] and StreamingLLM [29] maintain
“heavy hitter” KV entries. SnapKV [13] aggregates attention. FastGen [8] profiles attention-head
patterns to build a query-adaptive eviction policy. Quest [25] selects KV pages by query similarity
for efficient long-context inference. KV quantization, exemplified by the 2-bit asymmetric KIVI [16],
provides a complementary compression axis orthogonal to eviction. On the visual side, LOOK-M [26]
merges multimodal KV entries guided by text-prior attention. ElasticCache [17] uses importance-
driven KV merging for vision instruction-following models. R-KV [5] combines attention aggregation
with a redundancy penalty for reasoning-LLM KV compression. Most works report on the same
prefill-probe benchmarks that, as we show, cannot measure their decode-phase effect.
Two-phase view.
To our knowledge, no prior work explicitly separates prefill (compute) and decode
(memory) as orthogonal axes with distinct algorithms and evaluation requirements. Our framework
makes this separation a first-class design principle.
Token pruning as coreset selection.
Keeping k of N visual tokens is a coreset query on X ∈
RN×d, with two dual classical strategies: k-center diversity (DivPrune [1], Sener and Savarese [22])
and density- or sensitivity-weighted sampling (Feldman and Langberg [7], Bachem, Lucic, and
Krause [2], submodular maximization [19]). Our density signal ρ is a training-free, deterministic-
threshold proxy of the latter family (Appendix H). We are not aware of prior work framing VLM
token pruning as a two-phase coreset whose subset must simultaneously approximate prefill attention
and decode KV recall, and this paper is the first to exploit the resulting signal reuse.

Two-Phase Compression, Two-Phase Diagnostic
3.1
The argmax mechanism
Consider a VLM prefill that yields hidden states H ∈RT ×d over T tokens (visual + text). The first
decoded token is

example KV eviction E acting on the cached {(Kℓ, Vℓ)}, takes effect only when computing y2, y3, . . .
from the now-reduced cache. For benchmarks whose ground-truth answer is a single token, such
as a multiple-choice letter or a yes/no token, the score depends only on y1. Thus decode-phase E
has no effect on these scores by construction. Density-based compression operates on both prefill
and decode, with measurable gains in each phase (Sec. 5 and Sec. 6). To verify the two phases
independently, we partition benchmarks into two diagnostic roles based on their scoring rule. A
benchmark is a prefill probe if its score depends only on y1, as in a multiple-choice letter, a yes/no
token, or any single-letter argmax over a fixed set such as MMBench, POPE, and ScienceQA. Such a
probe isolates prefill quality and is structurally blind to any decode-phase change. A benchmark is
a full-pipeline probe if its score depends on the decoded sequence y1, y2, . . ., including free-form
answers scored by exact-match, ANLS edit distance, or an LLM judge such as TextVQA, DocVQA,
and MM-Vet. Such a probe reveals both phases and their interaction effects. The split is a property of
the scoring rule, not the literal answer length during generation.

Implication.
The pattern is universal across architectures: TextVQA’s 45.52% mismatch under

confirm full-pipeline probes register both phases while prefill probes register neither in practice. Any

ScienceQA must be re-examined; future VLM token-pruning evaluation should include at least one
full-pipeline-probe benchmark such as MM-Vet, DocVQA, or LLaVA-Bench-Wilder.

Why density?
Density is an intrinsic, query-invariant feature-tensor statistic. It is monotone

(information-geometric framing and the failed QC-density extension are in Appendix F).

cuBLAS-plus-graph realisation issues five kernel launches per step, and at our operating point
(N=3072, D=4096, K=614) the per-launch overhead dominates the actual compute. We fuse the
body into two Triton kernels, an argmax reduction and an inner-iter mind update with a 32×64 row-

4.3
Decode: density-guided KV eviction
After prefill completes, with or without prefill-stage compression, evict visual KV entries by density
rank in a single shot. We keep kdec = ⌈Npost · rdecode⌉entries with the highest ρ, where Npost is the
post-prefill visual-token count. Surviving KV rows retain their original RoPE-rotated keys without
rotation re-application, and the first decoded token is positioned at original_prefill_length+1 to
maintain query and key alignment. Density is reused from prefill when both phases are active, so
the decode stage adds no extra similarity computation. Eviction is one-shot at the prefill-decode
boundary, in contrast to streaming approaches that maintain a sliding window during generation. The
choice is justified by the coreset reading in Sec. 4.1: a density-coreset of the post-prefill visual tokens
approximates every subsequent attention aggregate that queries those tokens.
4.4
Joint and the three-axis Pareto
Joint compression composes prefill merge with decode eviction. The resulting design space is
naturally three-dimensional: (i) prefill FLOPs (reduced by merge), (ii) KV memory (reduced by
eviction), (iii) accuracy. Single-phase methods occupy the axes, and joint configurations fill the
interior. We characterize this Pareto surface in Sections 5 and 6, and Algorithm 1 in Appendix A
gives the full pseudocode.

5.3
Signal ablation
Is the advantage of our method really density, or could a simpler score match it? Holding every
pipeline component fixed (L=15, prune 50%, InternVL-3.5-8B, prune-only top-k) and swapping
only the ranking score across four benchmarks (TextVQA with n=5000, MMBench, V∗Bench,
MM-Vet), density wins or ties on all four: it outperforms L2 norm on TextVQA by 2.2 percentage
points (0.616 vs. 0.594), attention V-norm on TextVQA by 3.2 percentage points, and V-norm on
V∗Bench by 4.7 percentage points. MMBench scores cluster near 0.86 because the prefill-probe
scoring rule is insensitive to which visual tokens are kept (Sec. 3). Multi-scale density is nearly
indistinguishable from single-scale, differing by 0.03 percentage points on TextVQA, and the product
ρ·∥v∥is neutral-to-hurt: the two signals are not independent, so combining them injects noise. Full
four-benchmark table in Appendix I.

Per-model MM-Vet scores under TwinPrune _L3_50, decode_only_D75, and joint_P50_D50

7
Discussion and Conclusion
Summary and deployment.
VLM token pruning has been evaluated on a benchmark mix that
limits visibility into decode-phase effects. We provide the proof, propose a unified density-based
framework that scores token importance for both phases, and supply a three-axis Pareto across
seven model variants and three full-pipeline-probe benchmarks. We further identify a structural risk
class explaining when pure-selection methods fail on multi-axis RoPE backbones, and we show
compression cost does not grow with model size in the 8B-to-38B regime. In deployment, latency-
bound use cases use prefill compression (3.24× speedup at the B3 cell), memory-bound use cases
use decode eviction, and joint configurations apply when both axes are constrained.3
Limitations.
We identify two principled limitations. (i) Single-stage prefill compression, with
multi-stage scheduling unexplored. (ii) Spatial-grid density assumes regular tokenization, so non-grid
tokenizers such as Q-Former require alternative formulations.
Broader impact and future work.
Efficient VLM serving lowers energy use and enables edge
deployment. Compression can subtly alter behavior on long-tail or safety-critical inputs without
changing prefill-probe scores, so high-stakes deployments must validate on full-pipeline-probe
benchmarks. Future work: learning the optimal density-aggregation kernel, and task-conditioned
signals beyond pre-attention geometry (Appendix F).
