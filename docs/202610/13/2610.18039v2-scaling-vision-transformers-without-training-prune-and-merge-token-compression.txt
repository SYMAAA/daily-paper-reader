Abstract
Vision Transformers achieve strong performance but incur high computational
costs from quadratic token interactions. Training-free token reduction methods im-
prove efﬁciency, but their robustness remains underexplored. We propose Prune-
and-Merge ViT, a training-free framework for off-the-shelf models. It combines
class-attention-based pruning with similarity-based merging to reduce redundancy
while preserving semantic information. To evaluate robustness we introduce two
metrics. Relative Accuracy Drop (RAD) measures relative accuracy degradation
under transformations, and Prediction Flip Rate (PFR) quantiﬁes prediction con-
sistency. On pretrained ViT and DeiT, Prune-and-Merge reduces computation
competitively while maintaining robustness and better accuracy than existing to-
ken reduction methods. Our analysis suggests that hybrid token reduction does not
introduce hallucination-like behavior and preserves prediction stability for com-
mon image transformations.

Introduction
Vision Transformers (ViTs) forgo convolutional inductive biases and treat an image as a sequence of
visual tokens [4]. Non-overlapping patches are projected into N tokens and passed, with a learnable
class ([CLS]) token, through Transformer blocks. The token count is constant across layers, so cost
scales quadratically, O(N 2).
To address this issue, token pruning removes redundant tokens and merging aggregates similar ones
to cut computation [2, 8]. Many such methods are training-free and apply directly to pretrained
models. While such approaches improve efﬁciency, they may introduce instability under input trans-
formations, potentially affecting prediction consistency. Aggressive reduction at lower keep rates
can also degrade performance, as fewer tokens remain to capture essential visual information. This
motivates a systematic analysis of prediction stability under aggressive reduction.
Existing approaches apply pruning or merging in isolation, which causes semantic loss or
hallucination-like errors under input transformations. We address this with Prune-and-Merge ViT
(Figure 1), a plug-and-play, training-free framework that combines class-attention-based pruning
with similarity-driven merging for off-the-shelf ViT [4] and DeiT [13]. The ﬁrst stage discards
low-importance tokens via top-k selection on class-attention scores [8], which ﬁlters noise while

Related work
ViT [4] and DeiT [13] keep the sequence length ﬁxed, so inference stays costly. DynamicViT [12]
learns a pruning module, but it has to be trained. EViT [8] and Top-K need no training. They rank
patches by class-attention and discard the rest, while V-Pruner [15] ranks globally rather than locally,
and Zhan et al. [17] carry the paradigm to state-space models.
ToMe [2] fuses similar tokens by bipartite matching instead, which shortens the sequence physi-
cally rather than masking it. The same mechanism accelerates Stable Diffusion [1], and Haurum
et al. [6] ﬁnd that ToMe works best when at least half the token budget is kept. Spatial-preserving
hybrids [5, 9] enhance locality and VisionTrim [16] favors throughput, but neither line offers robust-
ness guarantees, and hybrids of the two families remain underexplored.
We address that gap in the plug-and-play spirit of ToCom [7] and its vision-language extensions [18],
where transfer to a narrow subset such as ImageNet-100 needs careful alignment [11]; see Section A.

Method
3.1
Framework overview
We propose Prune-and-Merge ViT (see Figure 1), a dual-stage, training-free framework. It treats
token reduction as sequential ﬁltering and compression. Given N input tokens x, we apply a hi-

erarchical reduction at layers l ∈{3, 6, 9} [6]. This schedule preserves high-resolution spatial
information in early layers before progressively reducing the sequence length in deeper blocks.
The composite keep rate K = kp · km governs reduction intensity at each stage. Here, kp ∈(0, 1] is
the pruning keep rate, the fraction of tokens retained according to semantic importance; pruning thus
acts as a semantic ﬁlter. Conversely, km ∈(0, 1] is the merging keep rate, the fraction remaining
after bipartite matching of the pruned subset. This multiplicative form allows ﬁne-grained control
over the total token budget while ensuring the reduction scales proportionally with the remaining
sequence length.

4.2
Results and discussion
Accuracy-efﬁciency trade-offs.
While our framework maintains a computational proﬁle compet-
itive with EViT and ToMe, it exhibits higher top-1 accuracy across all architectural scales (Ta-
bles 1 and 3). This performance gap is most pronounced in aggressive compression regimes (e.g.,
K = 0.25), representing a marginal but consistent improvement over standalone techniques. This
suggests that the interplay between pruning and merging preserves the representational power of the
backbones even under signiﬁcant reduction.
Controlled aggression.
A primary contribution of this work is the introduction of ‘Controlled
Aggression’ within the reduction pipeline. Unlike ﬁxed-strategy baselines, our framework lets the
two stages be balanced dynamically. At low keep rates, prioritizing aggressive merging over pruning
yields higher representational stability (Table 2). By shifting the reduction burden from Stage 1
(noise removal) to Stage 2 (information fusion) as K decreases, we mitigate the ‘cliff-edge’ accuracy
degradation typically observed in pure-pruning baselines. Critical semantic tokens are fused rather
than discarded, which maintains a more coherent global context.

5
Conclusion
We introduced Prune-and-Merge ViT, a dual-stage token reduction framework designed to optimize
the trade-off between inference efﬁciency and representational faithfulness. Under ‘Controlled Ag-
gression’ it transitions from top-k pruning in the early stages to bipartite matching for information
4


fusion in deeper layers. On ImageNet-100 it signiﬁcantly outperforms standalone pruning and merg-
ing baselines, achieving a 3× speedup on ViT-Base at aggressive keep rates while maintaining su-
perior accuracy (Tables 1 and 7). Furthermore, RAD and PFR conﬁrm that the hybrid approach
mitigates hallucinations and maintains a more robust attention manifold than ﬁxed-strategy alter-
natives. These results validate that a hardware-aware, hybrid reduction paradigm is essential for
scaling ViTs to resource-constrained environments without sacriﬁcing reliability.

Extended related work
This appendix expands the condensed discussion of prior work given in the main text, covering
Vision Transformer backbones, learned and training-free token pruning, token merging, and plug-
and-play compression in turn.
A.1
Vision Transformer foundations and distillation
The Vision Transformer (ViT) architecture [4] has been further optimized through specialized train-
ing strategies. Notably, DeiT [13] introduced a distillation token that interacts with patch embed-
dings via self-attention to inherit the inductive biases of convolutional teacher models. That work
demonstrated that hard distillation can signiﬁcantly outperform soft distillation, and that the inter-
play between the [CLS] and distillation tokens provides a more robust classiﬁer than either token
in isolation. While effective, these models still maintain a ﬁxed sequence length, leading to high
inference costs.
A.2
Dynamic and learned token pruning
To reduce computation, several methods focus on discarding redundant tokens. DynamicViT [12]
introduced a learned prediction module that hierarchically prunes tokens throughout the network. A
key ﬁnding in learned pruning is that masking tokens at the input layer signiﬁcantly harms perfor-
mance; sparsiﬁcation is therefore typically applied between transformer blocks. While learned mod-
ules offer high accuracy, they require additional training and backpropagation. In contrast, EViT
[8] and Top-K strategies provide training-free alternatives by ranking token importance based on
class-attention scores, identifying and removing background patches that contribute minimally to
the ﬁnal classiﬁcation. Recent advances such as V-Pruner [15] reﬁne this idea by employing glob-
ally informed importance metrics, ensuring that locally important but globally redundant features
are identiﬁed accurately. Extensions to state-space models [17] further reﬁne this paradigm, yet
hybrids remain underexplored.
A.3
Token merging and compression
A more recent paradigm shifts from discarding information to aggregating it. Token merging (ToMe)
[2] uses bipartite soft matching to fuse similar tokens based on the dot product of their key vectors.
Unlike pruning, which often relies on masking that does not always translate into hardware speedups,
merging physically reduces the number of tokens passed to the MLP block, leading to tangible
throughput gains. Recent extensions have explored unequal bipartite graphs to achieve higher com-
pression ratios (> 50%) and have applied these techniques to Stable Diffusion in order to accelerate
generative tasks [1]. Furthermore, studies such as “Which Tokens to Use?” [6] suggest that ToMe
is most effective when at least 50% of the token budget is kept, often outperforming baseline DeiT
models by leveraging proportional attention to account for the varying weights of merged tokens.
Spatial-preserving hybrids such as that of Mao et al. [9] and adaptive merging [5] enhance local-
ity but lack robustness guarantees, which our framework addresses. In addition, hardware-centric
frameworks such as VisionTrim [16] have begun optimizing token reduction for speciﬁc edge-device
latencies, though they often prioritize throughput over prediction stability.
A.4
Plug-and-play compression and transferability
Recent research has addressed the gap between models trained at different compression degrees. To-
Com [7] identiﬁes that transferring a model between different compression levels is often inefﬁcient.
To mitigate this, it introduces a pretrained plug-in module that models the performance gap between
source and target compression degrees, enabling more efﬁcient inference-time scaling without re-
training. Extensions to vision-language models [18] further validate this paradigm. Our work builds
on this plug-and-play philosophy by combining pruning and merging into a single, training-free
framework that offers more granular control than these monolithic strategies. Finally, as noted by
Peng and Jin [11], the transferability of compressed weights to smaller, specialized subsets such as
ImageNet-100 requires careful alignment in order to maintain the original semantic robustness of
the model.

Baselines.
We benchmark our method against the original, uncompressed backbone operating on
the full sequence length; Random Drop, a naive baseline where tokens are discarded uniformly at
random, representing a lower bound for pruning; Top-K pruning, a basic strategy that retains high-
attention tokens but discards the remainder without context fusion; and EViT [8] and ToMe [2], the
state of the art in semantic pruning and similarity-based merging, respectively. For a fair comparison,
all baselines are evaluated under identical hardware and software constraints.
Hyperparameter conﬁguration.
All experiments were conducted using the PyTorch framework
[10] and the timm library [14] on a Google Colab T4 GPU environment. For our hybrid Prune-
and-Merge blocks, the similarity metric used for token merging in Stage 2 was the cosine similarity
computed on the token features (embeddings). Proportional attention is applied in Stage 2, and for
all ToMe-based baselines, to maintain mathematical consistency during self-attention.
Hardware environment.
All benchmarks, namely the latency ﬁgures in Table 6 and the through-
put ﬁgures in Table 7, were measured on a single NVIDIA T4 GPU (16 GB VRAM).
ImageNet-100 class mapping.
The 100 classes were selected as a subset of the ImageNet-1K
label set. The backbone was initialized with ImageNet-1K pre-trained weights and the linear projec-
tion weights corresponding to the selected 100 classes were retained to obtain the 100-class output
space.

Prediction Flip Rate (PFR, Table 8) and (ii) the absolute change in PFR (∆PFR, Table 9) across

faithfulness is often tested by removing important tokens, we evaluate the inverse: a robust model
should maintain its prediction if an input is slightly transformed such that its semantic informa-
tion remains unchanged. We calculate ∆PFR for ViT-Tiny and ViT-Base to capture trends across
increasing model scales.
