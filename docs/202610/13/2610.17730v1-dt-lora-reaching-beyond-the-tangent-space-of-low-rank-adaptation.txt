DT-LoRA: Reaching Beyond the Tangent Space of Low-Rank Adaptation
Yangming Zhang1, Ling Yi1*, Datong Li2, Jinliang Ding3,
Xiaojie Wang1, Tiecheng Song1, Zhaolong Ning1

Abstract
Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) often underperforms full fine-
tuning.
We investigate this performance gap through a
tangent-space lens: factorized updates are confined to the
tangent space of the current low-rank factors, leaving gra-
dient components outside this space unexploited. An anal-
ysis of ten representative LoRA variants reveals that these
residual components account for most of the gradient en-
ergy and exhibit strong spectral concentration. Motivated
by this finding, we propose Dynamic Tangent LoRA (DT-
LoRA), which partitions a fixed rank budget into a stable
branch and a dynamic branch to capture the principal resid-
ual gradient directions. We show that these directions de-
fine the optimal update of a local quadratic model under
orthogonal-complement and rank constraints. Matrix-free
randomized SVD recovers them without materializing the
residual gradient, while controlled write-back preserves ac-
cumulated updates and stabilizes training within the allo-
cated rank. DT-LoRA adds no trainable parameters or in-
ference cost over LoRA. Experiments across computer vi-
sion and natural language processing tasks demonstrate
consistent improvements over LoRA and its variants. More-
over, despite being a general-purpose adaptation method,
DT-LoRA outperforms task-specific approaches to open-
vocabulary domain generalization for semantic segmenta-
tion, achieving state-of-the-art results. Code is available at
https://github.com/Snipine/DT-LoRA.
1. Introduction
Large-scale pre-trained models serve as general-purpose
foundations for a wide range of vision and language tasks.
However, as model sizes increase, full fine-tuning becomes
prohibitively expensive, requiring all model parameters to
be updated and a separate set of weights to be stored
*Corresponding authors.
for each downstream task. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning
(PEFT) reduces this cost by freezing the pre-trained weights
and optimizing only a small set of parameters [11, 14, 17].
Among PEFT methods, low-rank adaptation (LoRA) [12]
has been widely adopted for its low-rank parameterization
of weight updates, which can be merged into the pre-trained
weights without additional inference overhead.
Nevertheless, LoRA often falls short of full fine-tuning.
Prior work narrows this gap by modifying the update
structure (DoRA [25]), improving subspace initialization
(PiSSA [26], LoRA-GA [35]), or correcting the factor gra-
dients and optimizer states to better approximate full fine-
tuning updates (LoRA-Pro [36], AltLoRA [41], LoFT [31]).
However, a fundamental local constraint remains: under
factor perturbations δA and δB, the first-order weight up-
date lies entirely in the tangent space defined by the current
factors. The dense gradient component orthogonal to this
space therefore cannot be captured by any first-order up-
date, regardless of how the factor gradients are corrected at
the current step.
This raises a question: how much gradient informa-
tion lies outside the current tangent space?
We analyze
the tangent-space residual gradient across ten representative
LoRA variants (Fig. 1) and identify two consistent proper-
ties. First, the residual accounts for more than 85% of the
total gradient energy across all methods. Second, it exhibits
strong spectral concentration: the leading singular compo-
nent captures more than 87% of the residual energy, while
the top four capture more than 94%. These findings reveal
that the current tangent space leaves substantial gradient in-
formation unexploited, yet most of this information can be
recovered through a very low-rank approximation.
A natural idea to exploit the residual gradient is to con-
tinually incorporate its principal directions into the train-
able subspace. However, increasing the rank incurs a pro-
portional parameter cost, whereas replacing existing fac-
tors at a fixed rank can discard accumulated adaptation in-
formation and disrupt training continuity. ReLoRA [20],

Flora [8], and GaLore [47] also switch low-rank subspaces
during training, primarily to overcome rank limitations or
reduce training memory, but do not explicitly target gradi-
ent components outside the current tangent space. The key
challenge is therefore to incorporate these principal residual
directions within a fixed rank budget while retaining learned
information, maintaining training stability, and preserving
mergeability into the pre-trained weights.
To address this challenge, we propose Dynamic Tangent
LoRA (DT-LoRA), which partitions a fixed rank budget
into a stable branch that accumulates task-specific direc-
tions as in standard LoRA and a dynamic branch that pe-
riodically absorbs principal residual directions outside the
current tangent space. We prove that, under orthogonal-
complement and rank constraints, the optimal update for
a local quadratic model is given by the truncated SVD of
the tangent-space residual gradient. Crucially, this resid-
ual inherits a factored structure from backpropagation, en-
abling its products with narrow matrices to be computed
directly from cached input activations and output gradients.
This structure allows us to construct matrix-free operators
for Matrix-Free Randomized SVD, which extracts the prin-
cipal residual directions via randomized SVD [7] without
explicitly forming the residual gradient.
To incorporate
these directions while maintaining training stability, we in-
troduce Controlled Write-back mechanism, which merges
them with the current dynamic branch update, compresses
the result back to the original rank, and bounds the rela-
tive change at each refresh. After training, both branches
are merged into the pre-trained weights, yielding the same
inference cost as standard LoRA.
We evaluate DT-LoRA across diverse vision and lan-
guage tasks: the 19 image classification tasks in VTAB-
1K; object detection and instance segmentation on MS
COCO; open-vocabulary domain generalization for se-
mantic segmentation (OVDG-SS) in both real-to-real and
synthetic-to-real settings; eight commonsense reasoning
tasks using LLaMA2-7B and LLaMA3-8B; and GLUE us-
ing DeBERTaV3-base. Under matched trainable parameter
budgets, DT-LoRA consistently outperforms LoRA and its
variants. On MS COCO, DT-LoRA at r = 16 achieves an
average score of 46.6, exceeding LoRA at the same rank by
8.2 points and outperforming LieRA [29], which uses twice
as many trainable parameters. On OVDG-SS, DT-LoRA
establishes new state-of-the-art results, surpassing the task-
specific S2-Corr [46] despite being a general-purpose fine-
tuning method.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
• We identify a geometric limitation of factorized LoRA:
substantial gradient energy lies outside the current tan-
gent space. Across ten representative LoRA variants, we
show that this tangent-space residual exhibits strong spec-
tral concentration, and establish that its truncated SVD
yields the optimal update under orthogonal-complement
and rank constraints for a local quadratic model.
• We propose DT-LoRA, which partitions a fixed rank bud-
get into Stable and Dynamic branches to incorporate prin-
cipal residual directions. Matrix-Free Randomized SVD
extracts these directions without materializing the resid-
ual gradient, while Controlled Write-back integrates them
with accumulated updates and bounds the change at each
refresh, enabling dynamic tangent-space adaptation with-
out additional trainable parameters or inference overhead.
• We demonstrate the effectiveness of DT-LoRA across di-
verse vision and language tasks, with consistent improve-
ments over LoRA and its variants under matched train-
able parameter budgets. On OVDG-SS, DT-LoRA out-
performs task-specific methods and establishes new state-
of-the-art results.
2. Related Work
2.1. Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning
To avoid the memory and storage costs of full fine-tuning,
PEFT freezes the pre-trained backbone and trains only a
small number of parameters. Early methods can be roughly
divided into three categories.
The first category inserts
additional trainable modules into the network, such as
Adapter [11], which adds bottleneck modules sequentially
within Transformer layers, and AdaptFormer [3], which
places them in parallel with the MLP. The second category
introduces learnable continuous prompts into the input or
intermediate representations, such as Prompt Tuning [17],
Prefix-Tuning [18], and VPT [14] for vision Transformers.
The third category tunes only a subset of the model param-
eters or features, such as BitFit [1], which fine-tunes only
the bias terms, and SSF [21], which scales and shifts the
features. However, the first two categories retain extra mod-
ules or prompt tokens at inference, adding inference over-
head, while the third offers limited flexibility for adaptation.
In contrast, reparameterization methods such as LoRA [12]
represent the weight update with low-rank matrices that can
be merged into the pre-trained weights after training, bal-
ancing expressiveness and deployment efficiency.
2.2. Low-Rank Adaptation and Its Variants
Subsequent work improves LoRA mainly in three aspects.
The first concerns the structure of the update and rank allo-
cation: DoRA [25] decomposes the weight into magnitude
and direction and updates the direction in low-rank form;
LieRA [29] extends low-rank updates to high-dimensional
parameters such as convolution kernels via the exponential
map of Lie groups; AdaLoRA [44] and FlexLoRA [24] dy-
namically allocate the rank budget across layers according
to matrix importance. The second concerns the choice of
the low-rank subspace: PiSSA [26] initializes the adapter

low-rank branch.
4. Method
DT-LoRA splits a fixed rank budget into a Stable branch
that accumulates task-specific directions and a Dynamic
branch that periodically absorbs the principal directions of
the tangent-space residual gradient (Fig. 2).
Every τ steps, Matrix-Free Randomized SVD extracts
the top-q residual directions directly from cached activa-
tions X and output gradients R, without explicitly forming
Gres. Controlled Write-back then integrates these directions
into the Dynamic branch. At all other steps, both branches
follow standard LoRA updates.

5. Experiments
We evaluate DT-LoRA on vision tasks spanning image
classification, object detection, instance segmentation, and
OVDG-SS, as well as language tasks covering common-
sense reasoning and natural language understanding. We
compare DT-LoRA with LoRA and representative variants
across these tasks, and additionally with task-specific meth-
ods on OVDG-SS.
5.1. Experimental Setup
Datasets and Models.
For image classification, we use
VTAB-1K, which comprises 19 tasks across natural, spe-
cialized, and structured categories, each with 1,000 train-
ing examples. Following [38, 43], we adapt ViT-B/16 [5]
pre-trained on ImageNet-21K. For object detection and in-
stance segmentation, we follow LieRA [29] and fine-tune a
ConvNeXt-V2-B backbone within Mask R-CNN [9] on MS
COCO, using MMDetection [2].
For OVDG-SS, we adopt the benchmark protocol of S2-
Corr [46], training on CS-7 for real-to-real transfer or GTA-
7 for synthetic-to-real transfer and reporting mIoU on two
groups of unseen target domains, Dv-19 and Dv-58. We
use EVA02-CLIP [30] encoders with ViT-B/16 and ViT-
L/14 backbones within the CAT-Seg [4] and PCA-Seg [40]
frameworks.
For commonsense reasoning, we follow [13] to fine-tune
LLaMA2-7B and LLaMA3-8B on Commonsense170K and
evaluate on the test sets of eight tasks, including BoolQ and
PIQA. For natural language understanding, we fine-tune
DeBERTaV3-base [10] on eight GLUE tasks [33]. Dataset
details are provided in Sec. C.

5.5. Efficiency and Deployment
As shown in Tab. 8, DT-LoRA matches LoRA’s trainable
parameter count while introducing modest training over-
head. With τ = 5, it requires 0.89 s per iteration, only
70 ms more than LoRA, and is 5.11× faster than explic-
itly forming the residual gradient and applying full SVD
(4.55 s per iteration). The four configurations isolate the
contributions of randomized SVD and matrix-free compu-
tation. Randomized SVD restricts the decomposition to an
ℓ-dimensional sketch and accounts for most of the measured
runtime reduction; the two full-SVD configurations have
similar runtimes of 4.30 and 4.55 s per iteration. Matrix-
free operators eliminate the need to materialize Gres, reduc-
ing additional workspace from O(dmind) to O((N + d)q),
excluding cached activations, output gradients, and projec-
tion bases. After training, both branches merge into the pre-
trained weights, introducing no additional inference over-
head.
6. Conclusion
In this work, we presented DT-LoRA, a parameter-efficient
adaptation method that addresses the tangent-space con-
straint on factorized LoRA’s first-order updates. Our anal-
ysis showed that the gradient components outside the cur-
rent tangent space carry substantial energy concentrated in
a few dominant directions, motivating their periodic in-
corporation within a fixed rank budget. DT-LoRA com-
bines matrix-free residual direction extraction with con-
trolled write-back to adapt the trainable subspace while lim-
iting refresh-induced perturbations. Experiments across vi-
sion and language tasks demonstrated consistent improve-
ments over LoRA and its variants, including new state-of-
the-art results on OVDG-SS, without additional trainable
parameters or inference overhead. These findings highlight
the value of adapting low-rank update subspaces to previ-
ously unexploited gradient information.

C. Dataset Details
C.1. VTAB-1K
VTAB-1K contains 19 classification tasks grouped into Nat-
ural, Specialized, and Structured categories. The number of
classes and the size of the official test set for each task are
listed in Tab. 9. Following the VTAB-1K protocol, for each
task we first select hyperparameters by training on 800 sam-
ples and validating on the remaining 200, then retrain on all
1,000 samples and report the top-1 accuracy on the test set.
C.2. MS COCO
For object detection and instance segmentation, we fol-
low the setting of LieRA [29], training on MS COCO [23]
train2017 and evaluating on val2017 (Tab. 10). Standard
COCO metrics are computed on bounding boxes for detec-
tion and on masks for segmentation: mAP is the average
precision over IoU thresholds from 0.5 to 0.95, and we addi-
tionally report AP50, AP75, and APS, APM, APL for small,
medium, and large objects. All Avg. in the main text is the
average of these 12 detection and segmentation metrics.

C.3. OVDG-SS

D. Hyperparameter Settings
All tables use the same notation as the main text: r is the
total rank, rs and rd are the ranks of the Stable and Dynamic
branches, and α is the LoRA scaling factor. The refresh
interval τ is measured in optimization steps, and ρrefresh
and ρjump are the refresh ratio in Eq. (18) and the upper
bound on the relative jump in Eq. (20), respectively. If the
jump constraint is violated, we reduce β and recompress
until the constraint is satisfied or the maximum number of
backtracks is reached.
