# Abstract 

Learned post-training quantization (PTQ) calibration fits quantizer parameters on a small calibration set, and for a text embedder the natural supervision is retrieval relevance. We ask two questions that are usually con-flated: how far to calibrate, and whether to calibrate at all. Across three embedders (e5-base-v2, BGE-M3, EmbeddingGemma-300m) at 4-bit weight quantization, 161 calibration runs and five learning rates, we find that the outcome of a fixed procedure ranges from a gain to catastrophic degradation, and that the driver is the dose of the update, measured as the functional displacement Dfunc 

of the quantized model from its uncalibrated start. On EmbeddingGemma the same learning-rate grid gives +1.47 to −71.53 points of in-domain NDCG@10 change; a label-free cosine reconstruction objective collapses at the same dose ( −69.52 vs −71.63 in the highest dose bin), so the col-lapse is a property of learnable calibration rather than of retrieval supervision. Objective sensitivity is nevertheless heterogeneous: on e5, ranking calibration is substantially more robust than cosine calibration at matched displace-ment ( +14.16 ± 4.34 pp , 95% t-CI [ +3.37, +24.96], three paired slates), whereas on BGE-M3 the ordering is direc-tionally opposite but unresolved ([ −5.18, +2.38]). Dose is the main axis but not a sufficient statistic: equal Dfunc 

reached along different paths differs by up to 1.61 pp . We therefore treat calibration as an optional intervention. A pre-registered rule that accepts a checkpoint only when a per-query paired confidence interval on a 25% calibration-validation split clears the uncalibrated baseline was run on 6 independent calibration slates and five arms. It never selected a checkpoint below its slate’s baseline (0 harmful selections in 30 decisions; pre-registered harm-prevention score 0.56), retained 8 of 11 beneficial updates, and paid a mean oracle regret of 1.52 pp (slate-cluster 95% [0.59, 2.76]) with one abstention that forfeited 11.13 pp . The result is a safety–opportunity trade-off, not a free improve-ment. We report what remains open: the ordering on BGE-M3, the mechanism behind path residuals, and label efficiency, which our audit shows was never established. 

# 1 Introduction 

Post-training quantization of a text embedder to 4-bit weights is cheap when the quantizer parameters are set by a closed-form rule (min–max or percentile scaling) and expensive to get wrong: the deployed artifact ranks documents, and a small shift in the embedding geometry can reorder the top of a result list without changing any reconstruction statistic much. Learned calibration [13, 5, 26, 22, 28] fits those parameters with gradient steps on a small calibration set, and for a retriever the tempting supervision is retrieval relevance itself—a ranking loss over labelled queries [3]. This paper is about what that procedure actually does to three embedders, measured carefully enough to separate two questions that are usually asked together. The first is a dose question: how far should the quantizer move from its uncalibrated start? The second is a gate question: should it move at all, and can that be decided without touching held-out labels? We find that the first question has no universal answer, that the second one has a usable partial answer, and that several claims one would naturally make along the way—that retrieval supervision causes the collapse, that a ranking objective is safer, that selected calibration queries are label-efficient—are not supported by the ledger once the comparison is dose-matched, slate-paired, or counted in the right unit. 

Contributions. (1) On EmbeddingGemma-300m the outcome of one fixed procedure spans +1.47 to −71.53 pp 

of in-domain NDCG@10 across a four-point learning-rate grid, and the collapse is reproduced by a label-free ob-jective at the same functional displacement; the unsafe regime belongs to learnable calibration, not to retrieval supervision (§3). (2) Objective sensitivity is heterogeneous across models: a large ranking-over-cosine robustness ad-vantage on e5, no established ordering on BGE-M3 (§4). (3) Dfunc is a useful screening variable but not a sufficient statistic; a pre-registered scalar-threshold hypothesis is rejected (§5). (4) A pre-registered validation rule, run on six independent calibration slates, never selected a 1arXiv:2610.27397v1 [cs.AI] 19 Oct 2026 checkpoint below the uncalibrated baseline while forfeit-ing some beneficial updates; we report it as a four-number scorecard, keeping the pre-registered metric beside the realized outcome (§6). (5) An implementation audit that changed several conclusions is reported as part of the re-sult, including a label-budget accounting that retracts an earlier efficiency claim (§7, §8). We claim model-agnosticism of the procedure , not model-invariance of the numbers . The three embedders differ in backbone, pooling, prefix conventions and normaliza-tion; a single safe-dose constant is neither expected nor observed. What generalizes is that one frozen rule adapts to each model through its own validation signal—model A receiving a small update and model B receiving none is the content of the generalization, not a failure of it. 

# 2 Experimental setup 

Models. e5-base-v2 [34] (BERT [9] backbone, mean pooling, “query:/passage:” prefixes), BGE-M3 [4] (XLM-R [7] backbone, CLS pooling, no prefix) and EmbeddingGemma-300m [30] (Gemma 3 [17] decoder backbone, mean pooling with a dense head, task pre-fixes). All linear layers are quantized to 4-bit weights with a learned step-size quantizer (LSQ [13], with rounding passed through the straight-through estimator [2]); the quantizer parameters are the only trainable variables. 

Data. Calibration draws 32 labelled queries from each of four BEIR [29] domains (MS MARCO [27], FiQA, NF-Corpus, SciFact [32]) over a 2,000-document corpus per domain. In-domain generalization is measured on 25 dis-joint queries per domain that share no query with the cal-ibration set. Cross-domain generalization is measured on two held-out datasets never used for calibration (ArguAna, SciDocs [6]; 100 queries, 2,000 documents). Three fur-ther datasets (TREC-COVID [31], Climate-FEVER [10], Quora) remain sealed and were not evaluated in any run re-ported here. MS MARCO and SciFact are ceiling-limited in this setting (more than 70% of full-precision queries already at NDCG@10 = 1.0), so we keep the four-domain macro average only as a continuity figure and read im-provement on the two unsaturated domains and on the held-out sets. 

Objectives. The retrieval-supervised objective is Lamb-daRank [3] over each query’s positives and mined neg-atives ( lambdarank ). Two label-free controls share ev-ery other setting: cosine , which pulls quantized em-beddings toward their full-precision counterparts, and 

mse , which reconstructs weights. Calibration runs for 12 steps with Adam [21] at a learning rate from 

{10 −4, 3×10 −4, 10 −3, 3×10 −3}, extended to 10 −2 where a control could not otherwise reach the dose of interest. 

Dose. Dfunc is the mean cosine distance between the calibrated and the uncalibrated quantized embeddings of the calibration queries and their candidate documents at a given step. It measures how far the quantized function 

has moved, independently of the objective that moved it, and is the matching variable for every cross-objective comparison. 

Metrics and noise. We report NDCG@10 change in percentage points (pp) relative to the uncalibrated quan-tized model (step 0) unless stated otherwise; retention is the quantized/full- precision ratio. Noise floors are measured per model and per learning rate rather than borrowed: on e5, re-running the same configuration gives 

σruntime = 0 .184 pp and varying only the optimizer seed 

σopt = 0 .192 pp (SciDocs), while varying only the calibra-tion slate gives σslate = 0 .66 pp on ArguAna and 2.54 pp 

on SciDocs—the slate, not the optimizer, is the domi-nant variance source. On EmbeddingGemma σopt is het-eroscedastic across the learning-rate grid (0.504, 1.141, 3.470, 0.675 pp ), so significance is judged against the cell’s own σ and repeated statistics are reported as median [min, max] or mean ± SD with n stated. Every headline comparison below is paired within a calibration slate. 

Pre-registration. The selection protocol (§6), the dose-matched comparator (§3), the saturation criterion and the hypotheses in §8 were frozen before the corresponding batches ran; where a post-hoc analysis is added it is labelled as such. All numbers in this paper are generated from the experiment ledger by code; none is typed by hand. 

# 3 Result 1: collapse is a dose effect, and not retrieval-specific 

On EmbeddingGemma the four learning rates give +1.47, 

−6.28, −30.48 and −71.53 pp (medians of three optimizer seeds; Fig. 1). The same procedure that helps at 10 −4

destroys the model at 3×10 −3. e5 shows the same shape at a different scale ( +2.07, +4.26, +6.05, −8.60 pp ): an earlier draft of this work read the two models as an archi-tectural dichotomy—“works on e5, fails on Gemma”—and that reading was an uncontrolled learning-rate comparison, not a property of either architecture.

# 4 Result 2: objective sensitivity is heterogeneous across models 

Whether the two objectives differ within the safe regime is a separate question, and the answer differs by model. On e5 the cosine objective stays near Dfunc ≈ 0.06 across the original grid and only reaches the collapse dose at 10 −2

(Dfunc = 0 .148 , −11.5 pp , n=3 ); in the same Dfunc bin the ranking objective is still positive ( +3.22 vs −9.83 pp ,gap −13.05), and the comparator returns control_worse .On BGE-M3 neither objective collapses anywhere on the grid (cosine reaches at most Dfunc = 0 .201 ), and the slate-0 comparator favours the cosine objective ( +5.35 vs +2.46 pp in the 0.20–0.35 bin, gap +2.89 against a tolerance of 0.92 pp ; retrieval_specific ). A single-slate bin comparison is the weaker instrument, so the two decisive cells per model were re-run on three independent slates and matched step by step (Table 2). On e5 the ranking advantage is large and clear of zero: 

∆ = +14 .16 ± 4.34 pp , 95% t-CI [ +3.37, +24.96]. On BGE-M3 the direction is the opposite but the interval contains zero widely: -1.40 ± 1.52 pp , [ −5.18, +2.38]. We place the paired multi-slate result above the single-slate comparator and state the outcome accordingly: on e5, ranking calibra-tion is substantially more robust than cosine calibration at matched functional displacement; on BGE-M3 the or-dering is unresolved; on EmbeddingGemma both collapse. We do not claim that the ordering reverses by model, only that objective sensitivity is heterogeneous, and we do not claim that either objective is generally safer.

Regret over accepted runs alone is 0.49 pp and is reported only as an auxiliary figure, since it hides exactly the forfeited opportunities that matter. # 7 Validity 

Fake-quantization. All numbers come from simulated quantization, so every calibrated checkpoint is also ex-ported to packed INT4 integer codes and compared bit by bit against the simulated path: 85,524,480 weights across 73 layers on e5 and 101,449,728 across 168 on Embed-dingGemma, zero mismatches, at exactly four bits per weight. The check runs by default on every run, and objec-tives without an integer code (soft rounding) are refused export. 

Evaluation determinism. Retrieval scores are float32 on GPU and ties near the cut-off can flip NDCG@10 between otherwise identical runs. An audit records, per held-out query, the distance from the best positive to the nearest irrelevant score; over all slate runs the worst case on ArguAna is 16 near-ties below 10 −4 with a tie swing of 1.48 pp , and on SciDocs 10 near-ties with a swing of 0.50 pp ; differences below those swings are below the instrument and are not headlined. 

Label specificity. On e5 the LambdaRank gain exceeds all 50 within-domain shuffled-label controls (calibration-fit retention 1.0369 vs a null maximum of 0.7830 over 50 seeds; randomization p = 0 .0196 ), and the same holds for the pre-registered metrics on EmbeddingGemma. A derangement null and a same-code re-run of the plain null were added as sensitivity analyses; neither changes the verdict.

# 8 What we do not claim 

Label efficiency. An earlier draft described the selected calibration set as “32 labelled queries”. The audit showed that the margin-diversity selector reads the relevance la-bels of its entire candidate pool, so the labels accessed 

exceed the labels selected , which exceed the labels the gra-dient uses . In the gate’s own unit (Table 4) 128 selected and 512 random calibration examples give indistinguish-able point estimates at the present sample size ( +0.0004 NDCG, Welch 95% CI [ −0.037, +0.038], n=3 each), while the selected arm accessed 1,024 labels against 512. The pre-registered margin of 0.2 NDCG is wider than any plau-sible effect and is recorded as a specification defect: the gate passed without information. We therefore make no label-efficiency claim; the supportable observation is that a smaller selected calibration set produced a similar point estimate to a four-times-larger random one, at higher label access. 

The BGE-M3 ordering is unresolved (§4), and BGE-M3 has not been pushed to a collapsing dose; “every model collapses if pushed far enough” is not a claim this paper supports. 

Mechanism. The path residual of §5 and the model dependence of objective sensitivity have no mechanistic account here; update direction, objective geometry, repre-sentation geometry, curvature and quantizer parameteriza-tion are all candidates, and we did not begin that search because it would have reopened dozens of variables before the descriptive results were pinned. 

Scale and sealed data. Held-out evaluation uses 100 queries over 2,000 documents per dataset, so absolute NDCG is not headlined; three test datasets remain sealed for a single post-freeze evaluation that this version has not performed. 

# 9 Related work 

Post-training quantization is surveyed in [18]. Learned PTQ calibration [13, 5, 26, 22] and the large-model meth-ods it grew into [15, 14, 23, 28] optimise reconstruction or output error and report language-model perplexity, as do the outlier-handling schemes developed alongside them [8, 35]; the question of when the calibration it-self becomes harmful is rarely asked because the loss is bounded by construction. Mixed-precision allocation by Hessian sensitivity [12, 11], search [33], information flow [1], layer-wise geometry [36] or inter-layer interac-tion [39, 24] concerns which modules to protect, an axis we hold fixed. Dense retrievers [20, 19] are commonly eval-uated on zero-shot suites such as BEIR and MTEB [29, 25], and retrieval-specific compression has focused on the index and the output vectors [37, 38]; our object is the encoder’s weights and the failure mode is a calibration dose, not a codebook. The closest methodological relative is early stopping with a validation split, whose failure mode—flat validation curves that let an argmax wander—is exactly what forced the interval rule of §6. Declining to calibrate when the evidence is weak is also the stance of selective prediction [16], applied here to a deployment decision rather than to individual predictions. 610 Conclusion 

Learned PTQ calibration of text embedders has a dose-driven unsafe regime that label-reading and label-free objectives share. Inside the safe regime, ranking supervi-sion can be genuinely useful on some models and is not established as useful on others. A pre-registered, label-free validation rule refused every harmful update on six independent calibration slates while forfeiting some bene-ficial ones; the honest description is a safety–opportunity trade-off whose harm side is observed at zero and whose opportunity side is measured at 8/11. Calibration should be treated as an optional intervention with a gate, not as an unconditional optimization. 

Reproducibility. The runner, the frozen protocol, the pre-registered comparator, the selection rule, the audit log and the ledger from which every number in this paper is generated are released with the paper.
